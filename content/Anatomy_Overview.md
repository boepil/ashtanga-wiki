# Anatomy Overview in Ashtanga Yoga

## Summary
Anatomy in Ashtanga Yoga is the study of the body's structural framework and its functional mechanics within the practice of asana. It moves beyond aesthetic ideals to prioritize functional integrity, safety, and individual skeletal variation. The core inquiry of anatomical practice is **"What Stops Me?" (WSM?)**, distinguishing between modifiable tension and fixed skeletal compression.

## Detail
A thorough understanding of anatomy is paramount for ensuring proper posture and efficient movement. While general anatomical charts present an idealized form, every practitioner possesses a unique skeletal anatomy.

### Skeletal Variation
The appearance of a pose is dictated by inherent structural factors:
- **Bone Shape**: The orientation of hip sockets (acetabulum) or the angle of the femur (neck-shaft angle) determines the ultimate range of motion.
- **Compression vs. Tension**: 
    - **Tension (Soft Tissue)**: Resistance from muscles, fascia, or ligaments. This is adaptable and can be changed through consistent practice.
    - **Compression (Bone-on-Bone)**: Resistance occurring when one part of the body makes contact with another. This is a structural limit that cannot be changed.
- **Functional vs. Aesthetic**: Teachers and students should shift focus from "what the pose looks like" to "how the pose feels and functions for this unique body."

### Biomechanics and Functional Integrity
Alignment is the intelligent arrangement of the body to maximize energy flow and minimize injury risk.
- **Joint Congruency**: Maintaining optimal contact between joint surfaces to manage mechanical forces safely.
- **Dynamic Stabilization**: Stability is not rigidity; it is the ability to return to center through co-contraction (hugging muscle to bone).
- **Proprioception**: The nervous system's ability to sense joint position, which is enhanced through slow, controlled movement.
