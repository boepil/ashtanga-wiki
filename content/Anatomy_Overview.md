# Anatomy Overview

## Summary
Anatomy in Ashtanga Yoga is the study of the body's structural framework and its functional mechanics within the practice of asana. It moves beyond aesthetic ideals to prioritize **functional integrity**, safety, and individual skeletal variation [2, 3]. The core inquiry of anatomical practice is the **"What Stops Me?" (WSM?)** mantra, which distinguishes between modifiable soft-tissue tension and unchangeable skeletal compression [1, 14].

## Detail

### Skeletal Variation: The Biological "Hard Stop"
Every practitioner possesses a unique skeletal anatomy, meaning "universal" alignment cues are often structurally impossible or harmful for many bodies [3, 7].
- **Unique Architecture**: Variations in the size, shape, and orientation of bones—such as the depth of the hip socket (acetabulum) or the femoral neck-shaft angle (varying from 110° to 150°)—dictate an individual's ultimate range of motion [6, 7].
- **The Flaw of Averages**: Standardized cues like "feet parallel" fail to account for variations in tibial torsion or femoral angles. Designing the practice must adapt to the individual, not vice-versa [7, 8].

### The Tension-Compression Spectrum
Physical limits in any pose fall into two primary categories [14, 35]:

| Feature | **Tension** (Soft Tissue) | **Compression** (Bone-on-Bone) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Origin** | Muscles, fascia, ligaments, joint capsules. | Body-on-body contact (soft, medium, or hard). |
| **Location** | Felt *away* from the direction of movement. | Felt *in* the direction of movement (pinching/stuck). |
| **Adaptability** | Adaptable; changes through consistent practice. | Fundamental; unchangeable structural limit. |
| **Risk** | Safe to work with using breath/patience. | Pushing through is a primary cause of injury. |

### The "What Stops Me?" (WSM?) Inquiry
The WSM? mantra is a tool for self-assessment to identify the specific limit encountered in a pose [14, 68].
- **Skillful Action**: 
    - If stopped by **tension**, the student uses breath and time to facilitate lengthening.
    - If stopped by **compression**, the student must accept the limit or "go around" it by adjusting the angle of the pose (e.g., widening the feet in a fold to bypass hip compression) [14, 35].

## Biomechanics and Functional Integrity
Alignment is the intelligent arrangement of the body to maximize energy flow and minimize injury risk.
- **Dynamic Stabilization**: Stability is not rigidity; it is the ability to return to center through co-contraction (hugging muscle to bone) [4].
- **Joint Congruency**: Maintaining optimal contact between joint surfaces to manage mechanical forces safely.
- **Proprioception**: The nervous system's ability to sense joint position, enhanced through slow, controlled movement and "message" loops from deep core stabilizers like the multifidus [9].
