# Jalandhara Bandha (Throat Lock)

*Jalandhara Bandha* is the uppermost of the three primary bandhas. The name comes from *Jala* (net or mesh) and *Dhara* (holding). It functions to "hold the network" of nadis (energy channels) in the neck and serves as a seal for the thoracic outlet.

## Anatomical Mechanics

The lock involves the coordination of muscles in the neck and throat to manage pressure:
- **Sternocleidomastoid & Deep Neck Flexors:** Contract to flex the neck forward and draw the chin toward the sternal notch.
- **Glottis:** The vocal folds are partially or fully closed to regulate airflow and intra-thoracic pressure.
- **Vagal Stimulation:** The compression in the throat may stimulate the carotid sinus, influencing the parasympathetic nervous system and slowing the heart rate.

## Integration with Pranayama

Jalandhara Bandha is essential for managing the increased internal pressure generated during breath retention (*Kumbhaka*).

- **Pressure Regulation:** By sealing the glottis, the lock prevents the "upward rush" of air from putting excessive pressure on the brain, eyes, or inner ears during internal retention (*Antara Kumbhaka*).
- **Sealing Prana:** Traditionally, it is said to bind the sixteen "vital spots" (*Adharas*) and prevent the nectar (*Amrita*) of the Moon in the head from dropping into the "Sun" in the navel, where it would be consumed.
- **Directional Flow:** It directs *Prana* downward into the central channel (*Sushumna*), assisting its union with the upward-moving *Apana* at the navel.

## Engagement Techniques

1. **Sit Tall:** Begin in a stable seated posture (*Padmasana* or *Siddhasana*).
2. **Lift the Sternum:** Inhale deeply and lift the chest/sternum toward the chin. The lock is as much about lifting the heart as it is about lowering the head.
3. **Flex the Neck:** Slowly lower the chin toward the sternal notch (the pit of the throat).
4. **Close the Glottis:** Engage the throat muscles to seal the airway.
5. **Release:** To exit, lift the head to a neutral position first, then slowly exhale.

> **Asana Integration:** The lock is naturally occurring in inverted postures like *Sarvangasana* (Shoulderstand) and *Halasana* (Plow Pose), where the chest presses firmly against the chin.

## Modifications and Safety

> [!WARNING]
> Students with cervical spondylosis, neck injuries, or high blood pressure should exercise caution and avoid the full lock.

- **Rolled Towel:** If the chin cannot comfortably reach the chest, placing a small rolled-up washcloth in the throat pit provides tactile feedback and a gentle seal without forcing the cervical spine.
- **Neutral Alignment:** For those with neck pain, maintain a neutral "long neck" position without forward flexion, utilizing only the glottal seal.
