# Traditional Pranayama Techniques

Beyond Ujjayi and Nadi Shodhana, classical Hatha Yoga texts outline several specialized breath control techniques designed to manipulate internal body temperature, stimulate digestion, and induce specific states of consciousness.

## Heating and Invigorating Practices

### 1. Bhastrika (Bellows Breath)
*Bhastrika* mimics the action of a blacksmith's bellows to rapidly increase internal heat (*jathara agni*).
- **Method:** Forceful, rapid, and equal inhalations and exhalations through the nose, driven by vigorous abdominal pumping.
- **Purpose:** Energizes the body, clears sinuses, improves digestion, and is traditionally said to pierce the energetic "knots" (*granthis*) blocking the rise of Kundalini.
- **Contraindications:** High blood pressure, heart conditions, glaucoma, or weak lung capacity.

### 2. Kapalabhati (Skull-Shining Breath)
Though often taught as a pranayama, classical texts classify *Kapalabhati* as a *Shatkarma* (purification technique). See [[Kapalabhati]] for more details.
- **Method:** Passive inhalations followed by sharp, forceful exhalations driven by contracting the abdominal muscles.
- **Purpose:** Dries up excess phlegm (*kapha*), removes impurities from the sinuses, oxygenates the blood, and "illuminates" the mind.
- **Contraindications:** Pregnancy, high blood pressure, and intraocular pressure.

### 3. Surya Bhedana (Sun-Piercing Breath)
Activates the solar channel (*Pingala*).
- **Method:** Inhale exclusively through the right nostril, retain the breath (*Antara Kumbhaka*), and exhale exclusively through the left nostril.
- **Purpose:** Increases body heat, stimulates mental alertness, and alleviates wind (*vata*) imbalances.
- **Contraindications:** High blood pressure, heart conditions, or practice during hot weather.

## Cooling and Calming Practices

### 4. Chandra Bhedana (Moon-Piercing Breath)
The opposite of Surya Bhedana; activates the lunar channel (*Ida*).
- **Method:** Inhale through the left nostril, retain, and exhale through the right.
- **Purpose:** Cools the body, calms the mind, and lowers systemic heat.

### 5. Sheetali (Cooling Breath)
- **Method:** Curl the tongue into a tube, projecting it slightly past the lips. Inhale through the rolled tongue, creating a cooling sensation. Exhale through the nose.
- **Purpose:** Reduces body temperature, alleviates thirst and hunger, and soothes inflammation.

### 6. Sheetkari (Hissing Breath)
An alternative for those unable to roll their tongue for Sheetali.
- **Method:** Clench the teeth gently together, open the lips, and inhale through the teeth with a hissing sound. Exhale through the nose.
- **Purpose:** Identical to Sheetali; cools the system and calms mental agitation.

## Meditative and Advanced Practices

### 7. Bhramari (Humming Bee Breath)
- **Method:** Inhale deeply, then exhale slowly while making a continuous, resonant humming sound in the throat (like a bee). Often practiced with the ears plugged (via *Shanmukhi Mudra*).
- **Purpose:** Calms the nervous system, alleviates anxiety and insomnia, and induces a deep meditative state.

### 8. Murcha (Fainting Breath)
- **Method:** Deep inhalation followed by a strong retention (*Kumbhaka*) with chin lock (*Jalandhara Bandha*) until a feeling of lightheadedness or "fainting" occurs, followed by a slow exhale.
- **Purpose:** Induces a state of "conscious unconsciousness" or deep sensory withdrawal. *Highly advanced; requires expert guidance.*

### 9. Plavini (Floating Breath)
- **Method:** Swallowing air to fill the stomach (not just the lungs) and retaining it.
- **Purpose:** Traditionally said to make the body buoyant enough to float on water. *Rarely taught in modern yoga.*
