# Asana Fundamentals

## Summary
The foundation of Ashtanga Yoga postures (Asanas) relies on precise alignment, breath integration, and structural integrity. Understanding functional anatomy and the mechanics of movement allows practitioners to cultivate stability, avoid injury, and facilitate the unobstructed flow of prana (vital energy).

## Detail
True alignment in yoga balances **Mobility** (freedom of movement) with **Stability** (control and safety). It is a dynamic process of constant inquiry ("What Stops Me?" — tension vs. compression).

### Tristhana: The Three Pillars
Every asana must incorporate the three pillars of attention:
1. **Asana (Posture)**: Functional alignment over aesthetic shape. Establishing roots to rise.
2. **[[Pranayama_Overview|Pranayama]] (Breath)**: Ujjayi breath creates internal heat and synchronizes movement.
3. **[[Drishti]] (Gaze)**: Focusing the eyes on a specific point to induce Pratyahara (sense withdrawal).

### Alignment Principles
*   **Foundation & Grounding**: "Root down to rise up." Stability must be built from the ground up, utilizing the tripod of the foot and the starfish shape of the hand (Hasta Bandha).
*   **Dynamic Stabilization & Co-contraction**: "Hugging muscle to bone." Engaging opposing muscle groups stiffens the joint, providing stability against loads.
*   **Energy Lines & The Spiral Line**: Engaging myofascial meridians to maintain balance. Spiraling the limbs helps stabilize joints.
*   **Proximal Stability for Distal Mobility**: Stabilize the center (core/pelvis) to allow freedom in the extremities (limbs).

### Anatomical Focus
*   **Backbends**: Distributing the curve. The flexible lumbar spine must be stabilized while the stiff thoracic spine must be mobilized. Pelvic stability and internal rotation of the thighs protect the lower back.
*   **Twists**: "Extension BEFORE Rotation." The spine must be axially extended to avoid compressing the vertebrae while twisting. Breathing into the upper chest accommodates the abdominal compression.
*   **Hip Openers & Lotus**: Safe Lotus depends entirely on the external rotation of the femur. If the hip stops rotating, forcing the foot higher transfers dangerous torque to the knee (meniscus/MCL). **Never force the knee.**

### Safety & Modifications
*   **Ahimsa (Non-Harm)**: Pain is a signal. "Bad pain" (sharp, joint-focused) means stop immediately.
*   **Props**: Blocks and straps are tools for intelligence, not crutches. They help maintain functional alignment when soft tissue tension restricts movement.
