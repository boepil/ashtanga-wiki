# Diet and Digestion (Agni and Mitahara)

In the Ashtanga tradition, asana practice alone is considered insufficient if the diet is inappropriate. Proper nutrition is the foundation that allows the physical and energetic bodies to be purified.

## Understanding Agni (Digestive Fire)

A primary goal of Hatha Yoga is to stoke **Agni**—specifically *Jathara Agni* (the gastric fire). 
- **Purification:** A strong digestive fire burns away toxins (*Amā*) and ensures proper cellular renewal.
- **The Pranic Link:** *Samana-vayu*, the vital wind situated in the navel region, is responsible for stoking these fires.
- **Asana Integration:** Specific postures, such as *Paschimottanasana* (Seated Forward Bend) and *Matsyendrasana* (Spinal Twist), are designed specifically to "fan" this gastric fire.

## Mitahara: The Principle of Moderation

**Mitahara** (moderate diet) is a central tenet of yogic success. Over-eating leads to lethargy and dullness, while under-eating leaves the body too weak to sustain the intense heat of practice.

The traditional rule for consumption dictates how the stomach should be filled:
- **One-half (1/2)** filled with food.
- **One-quarter (1/4)** reserved for water.
- **One-quarter (1/4)** left empty for the free movement of air (*vayu sancharam*).

## Practice Timing and Fasting

- **Empty Stomach:** Asanas must always be practiced on an empty stomach, following the evacuation of the bladder and bowels.
- **Wait Times:** Practitioners must wait at least 3 hours after a meal before attempting head-down postures, and 6 hours before abdominal practices like *Nauli*.
- **Exceptions:** Physically weak practitioners may practice 1 hour after a very light liquid diet (e.g., a cup of warm milk).

## Post-Practice Care

According to K. Pattabhi Jois in *Yoga Mala*:
- **Sweat:** Do not wipe away perspiration immediately. Rub it back into the body to grant the body firmness and retain the generated energy.
- **Bathing:** Wait at least 30 minutes after practice so the internal temperature can cool naturally. Avoid cold baths during the early stages of practice.
- **Savasana:** Always conclude with 10–15 minutes of rest to remove fatigue and allow the nervous system to integrate the practice.
