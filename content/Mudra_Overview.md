# Mudra Overview

*Mudrā* (मुद्रा) comes from the Sanskrit root *mud* ("to delight" or "to please") and *rā* ("to give"). It is often translated as "that which bestows bliss," or more commonly in yoga, as a "seal," "gesture," or "energy lock." 

In the yogic tradition, mudras are symbolic gestures or energetic seals used to direct and trap *Prana* (vital energy) within the body, preventing its dissipation into the external world.

## The Types of Mudra

Mudras are not limited to hand positions. The tradition categorizes them into five main groups:

1. **Hasta Mudrā (Hand Gestures):** The most common type, used primarily in meditation and *pranayama* (e.g., [[Hand_Mudras|Chin Mudra]], [[Hand_Mudras|Anjali Mudra]]).
2. **Kaya Mudrā (Body Gestures):** Full-body postures practiced with specific energetic intent (e.g., [[Body_Mudras|Viparita Karani]], Yoga Mudrasana).
3. **Mana Mudrā (Mental/Gaze Gestures):** The use of the eyes, tongue, or mental focus to direct energy. This includes *Shambhavi Mudra* (third-eye gazing), *Khechari Mudra* (tongue to the palate), and the broader practice of [[Drishti]].
4. **Bandha Mudrā (Lock Gestures):** Combining mudras with the energetic locks (e.g., [[Maha_Bandha|Maha Mudra/Bandha]]).
5. **Adhara Mudrā (Pelvic/Root Gestures):** Targeted toward the lower body energy centers (e.g., *Ashvini Mudra*—rhythmic contraction of the anal sphincter).

## The Concept of Tristana

While Mudras "seal" the energy, the Ashtanga Vinyasa system specifically utilizes a three-pronged method to achieve this seal during active practice, known as **Tristana**:

1. **Ujjayi Pranayama:** The rhythmic, victorious breath that regulates prana and heat.
2. **Bandhas:** The internal muscle locks (Mula and Uddiyana) that stabilize the core and retain energy.
3. **Drishti:** The yogic gaze that directs the mind inward and prevents visual energy dissipation.

When these three elements are unified, the physical practice itself becomes a continuous, moving *Mudra*—a sealed vessel of meditative awareness.

## Traditional Sources

The extensive catalog of mudras comes from classical Hatha Yoga texts rather than Patanjali's Yoga Sutras:
- **Hatha Yoga Pradipika:** Lists 10 main mudras (such as Mahamudra, Khechari, and Vajroli) and states that mudras are the key to awakening Kundalini.
- **Gheranda Samhita:** Names 25 distinct mudras.
- **Shiva Samhita:** Describes the use of mudras for spiritual transformation and conquering death.
