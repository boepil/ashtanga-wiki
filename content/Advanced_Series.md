# Advanced Series (Sthira Bhaga)

## Summary
The Advanced Series, known as *Sthira Bhaga* (Sublime Serenity or Divine Stability), is the third major sequence in Ashtanga Yoga (often split into Advanced A, B, C, and D). It requires immense strength, extreme flexibility, and profound mental focus, building upon the foundations of the Primary and Intermediate series.

## Detail
Sthira Bhaga translates to "Strength and Grace." While Yoga Chikitsa (Primary) heals the body and Nadi Shodhana (Intermediate) purifies the nervous system, Sthira Bhaga aims to cultivate divine stability and supreme focus.

The series is characterized by:
1. **Extreme Arm Balances**: Poses like **Vasisthasana** variations, **Visvamitrasana**, **Galavasana**, and **Urdhva Kukkutasana** that demand extraordinary core and upper body strength.
2. **Deep Hip Opening & Leg-Behind-Head Postures**: Poses such as **Kasyapasana**, **Bhairavasana**, **Skandasana**, and **Durvasasana** take the leg-behind-head flexibility established in the Intermediate Series to its absolute limits, often combining it with arm balancing or standing.
3. **Advanced Inversions**: Deep backbends from inversions, such as **Viparita Chakrasana** (tick-tocks), and the pinnacle inversion, **Adho Mukha Vrksasana** (Handstand).
4. **Continuous Flow**: The Vinyasa between these advanced postures requires a mastery of floating and seamless transitions, relying heavily on the *[[Bandha_Overview|Bandhas]]* (internal locks).

The physical demands of Sthira Bhaga are so intense that it forces the practitioner to transcend physical effort and find absolute mental stillness. Only through complete surrender and structural integrity can these poses be sustained.
