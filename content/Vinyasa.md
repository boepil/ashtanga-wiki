# Vinyasa (Breath-Linked Movement)

## Summary
**Vinyasa** is the system of breathing and movement that defines the Ashtanga method. For every movement, there is one breath. For example, in [[Surya_Namaskar]], there are nine vinyasas. The purpose of vinyasa is internal cleansing and the generation of [[Tapas]] (heat).

## The Vinyasa Count
Each asana has a specific number of vinyasas assigned to it. This count acts as a mantra, focusing the mind and ensuring that the body moves in rhythm with the breath.
- **Entry**: The steps taken to enter a pose.
- **Stay**: The period of 5-10 breaths held in the state of the asana.
- **Exit**: The steps taken to return to a neutral position.

## Physiological Impact
- **Blood Purification**: The heat generated by vinyasa thins the blood, allowing it to move more freely through the joints and organs, removing toxins [ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, p. 1].
- **Respiratory Pump**: Rhythmic movement assists the venous return to the heart, reducing the cardiac workload [Kaminoff, p. 24].
- **Synovial Fluid Circulation**: The continuous movement ensures that the joints remain lubricated through the circulation of synovial fluid.
