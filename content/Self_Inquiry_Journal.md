# Self Inquiry Journal

In the Ashtanga tradition, the practice of asana is a laboratory for Self-Inquiry (**Ātma-Vicāra**)—an investigation into the real nature of one’s own Self. The goal is to separate the silent witness from the active world of ego and movement.

## Ātma-Vicāra Journal Template

**Date:** ____________________
**Sequence:** ____________________

### 1. The Observer (Puruṣa) vs. The Performer (Prakṛti)
Reflect on the duality of your practice today. Did you identify as the *Purusha* (the silent witness-consciousness), or were you entirely caught up in the fluctuations of *Prakriti* (the active world of body, pain, and movement)?
- **Observation:** Note moments where you successfully "withdrew" and observed sensations simply as "ripples on a pool."

### 2. Noticing the Five Vṛttis (Mental Fluctuations)
Identify which cognitive conditions dominated your mind during practice:
- **Pramāṇa (Valid Knowledge):** Direct, clear perception of your breath and alignment.
- **Viparyaya (Error):** Misperceiving limits, pushing into "wrong pain," or false assumptions.
- **Vikalpa (Conceptual Construction):** Daydreaming, mental chatter, or imagining future scenarios.
- **Nidrā (Lethargy):** Feelings of heaviness, dullness, or "spacing out."
- **Smṛti (Memory):** Past successes or failures distracting you from the present moment.

### 3. Identifying the Kleśas (Afflictions)
Pinpoint where mental "torments" arose during specific challenging asanas:
- **Asmitā (Egoism):** Pride in "nailing" a pose, or shame based on performance.
- **Rāga (Attachment):** "Thirsting" for a specific result or forcing the body to achieve a bind.
- **Dveṣa (Aversion):** Instinctive anger, fear, or avoidance toward a difficult posture.

### 4. Practicing Vairāgya (Detachment)
Reflect on your ability to see passing events securely without "grasping" for the fruits of your effort. How did letting go of the "thirst" for progress change the quality of your breath?

---

## Deep Inquiry Prompts (For Śavāsana)

1. **The Source of Thought:** When a thought arises, ask: *"To whom does this thought arise?"* and *"Who is the one who thinks?"*
2. **Neti-Neti (Not this, not that):** In repose, systematically eliminate identifications: *"I am not this breath, I am not this sensation, I am not this mind."* Observe what remains.
3. **The Fabrication of "I":** Reflect on the "I-maker" (*Ahaṃkāra*). Ask: *"Is the 'I' that is complaining about this posture a true self, or a temporary story?"*
