# Cardiovascular and Lymphatic Systems

## Summary
The physical mechanics of Ashtanga Vinyasa Yoga—specifically the continuous linking of movement (*vinyasa*) combined with resisted breathing (*Ujjayi*)—exert massive influence on the cardiovascular and lymphatic systems. The tradition claims this process "boils the blood" (*Tapas*) to remove toxins, a concept strongly supported by the anatomical mechanics of venous return and lymphatic drainage.

## Detail

### "Boiling the Blood" (Tapas and Detoxification)
A core tenet of Ashtanga is the generation of internal heat (*Tapas*).
- "The friction of the breath and continuous movement boils the blood, thinning it to circulate freely and remove toxins via sweat" (*ASHTANGA YOGA KNOWLEDGE BASE*, p. 24).
- B.K.S. Iyengar used a famous metaphor: "Just as a goldsmith heats gold in fire to burn out its impurities, similarly, asanas, by increasing the circulation of fresh blood through the body, purge it of the diseases and toxins" (*B.K.S. Iyengar Yoga*, p. 196).
- This is achieved mechanically via the breath: "Narrowing the opening of the glottis creates a resistance to airflow... giving this muscle [the diaphragm] a light workout" (Long, *Yoga Mat Companion 1*, p. 883).

### Cardiovascular Impact and Venous Return
The continuous muscular contraction of Vinyasa and the inversion of the body significantly aid the cardiovascular system.
- "Alternately contracting and stretching the muscles during Vinyasa augments blood flow by compressing and expanding the veins. Cardiac output also increases" (Long, *Yoga Mat Companion 1*, p. 873).
- "Inversions allow gravity to open the valves for you, encouraging venous return to your heart and improving circulation" (Swanson, *Science of Yoga*, p. 420).
- "Yoga can help to improve venous return in three main ways: (1) by improving the efficiency of the skeletal muscle pump; (2) by improving the efficiency of the respiratory pump; and (3) by inverting the body" (McGonigle and Huy, *The Physiology of Yoga*, p. 597).

### Lymphatic Drainage
The lymphatic system lacks a central pump (like the heart) and relies entirely on muscular contraction and gravity to move lymph fluid.
- "Movement in yoga asanas, particularly from sun salutations and inversions, can help facilitate lymph flow" (Swanson, *Science of Yoga*, p. 407).
- "Sarvangāsana [Shoulderstand] supports drainage of blood and lymph out of the legs, gut, and chest... Since lymph flows in little waves via peristaltic contractions, local pressure serves to squeeze or 'milk' the lymphatic ducts" (Little, *Yoga of the Subtle Body*, Chapter 7).
- The breath acts as an internal massage: "The rhythmic contraction and relaxation of the diaphragm during breathing massages the abdominal organs... The lymphatics that surround the intestines are massaged, stimulating the immune system" (Long, *Yoga Mat Companion 1*, pp. 874, 881).
- Hydration and movement allow the extracellular matrix to be cleansed: "Toxins and waste products can be escorted out of the matrix into the lymphatic system and removed from the body" (Clark, *Your Body, Your Yoga*, p. 1044).
