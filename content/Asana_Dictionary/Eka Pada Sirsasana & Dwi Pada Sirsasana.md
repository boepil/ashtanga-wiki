# Eka Pada Sirsasana & Dwi Pada Sirsasana

![Eka-Pada-Sirsasana](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Eka-Pada-Sirsasana.png)

## Sanskrit Meaning
"Eka means one. Pada means foot. Sirsa means head. Eka Pada Sirsasana is the one-leg-behind-head pose." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 293) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Dwi means two. Dwi Pada Sirsasana is the two-legs-behind-head pose." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 296) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"Eka Pada Sirsasana has seven vinyasas. The 3rd is the state of the asana." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1430) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Sit on the floor... bend the right leg and lift the foot. Hold the right ankle with both hands and lift the leg until the foot is behind the head. Place the right calf over the back of the neck. Join the palms in front of the chest." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 293–294) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"For Dwi Pada, place both legs behind the neck, crossing the feet. Balance on the hands and lift the torso (in some variations) or remain seated." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 296–297) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
Nose (nasagre)

## Movement
- **Eka Pada Sirsasana:** One leg behind the neck, torso upright, palms together.
- **Dwi Pada Sirsasana:** Both legs behind the neck, feet crossed, balancing on the hands (or seated).
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- **Hip External Rotators:** "Gluteus maximus, piriformis, and gemelli must be exceptionally mobile to allow the femur to rotate enough for the leg to clear the shoulder." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Adductors:** "The adductor group must lengthen intensely." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Erector Spinae:** "Active to prevent the spine from rounding too much under the weight of the leg(s)." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "Extreme hip external rotation and flexion. Knee flexion. Spinal flexion (mild to intense depending on flexibility)." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"Mula Bandha is essential to create a solid base in the pelvis, while Uddiyana Bandha helps to 'lift' the spine against the downward pressure of the leg on the neck." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 806) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- **Knee Torque:** "If the hip is not open enough, the practitioner often forces the leg back by twisting the knee, risking a meniscus tear." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 275) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Cervical Strain:** "The leg pressing on the neck can cause forward head posture and strain the cervical vertebrae." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 278) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Ensure the shoulder is moved forward in front of the calf to allow the leg to rest on the upper back/neck rather than the head." <div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>
- "Rule: The movement must come from the hip socket, not the knee joint." <div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Teaching Notes
"These postures develop tremendous flexibility in the hips and strengthen the spine. They are considered milestones in the Intermediate Series." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 295) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*
