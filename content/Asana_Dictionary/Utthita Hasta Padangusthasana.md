# Utthita Hasta Padangusthasana (Extended Hand to Big Toe Pose)

![Utthita-Hasta-Padangusthasana-a](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Utthita-Hasta-Padangusthasana-a.png)
![Utthita-Hasta-Padangusthasana-b](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Utthita-Hasta-Padangusthasana-b.png)
![Utthita-Hasta-Padangusthasana-c](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Utthita-Hasta-Padangusthasana-c.png)

## Sanskrit Meaning
"Utthita means extended. Hasta means the hand. Padangustha is the big toe. This pose is done by standing on one leg, extending the other in front, holding the toe of the extended leg and resting the head on the leg." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 199) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"There are fourteen vinyasas in this asana. The 2nd, 4th, 7th, 9th, 11th, and 14th vinyasas are the states of the asana." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1524) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"First, join the legs together... stand erect. Then, doing puraka [inhale], place the left hand on the waist, straighten the right arm, raise the right leg, catch hold of the big toe with the right hand, and straighten the knees, chest, and waist; this is the 1st vinyasa." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1524) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Next, doing rechaka [exhale], bring the right leg out to the right, hold the arm, leg, waist, and chest straight, and look to the left; this is the 4th vinyasa." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1524) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"In the 7th vinyasa ... stand straight, placing the hands on the waist and keeping the raised right leg extended straight, while breathing fully and deeply as much as possible." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1525) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Exhale, release the hands and lower the right leg to the floor to return to Tadasana." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 200) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
tip of the nose (for A), to the left (for B), tip of the nose (for C)
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Movement
A: right leg up, hold the big toe, left hand on the hip, bring the leg towards the face and the face towards the knee
B: right leg up, hold the big toe, left hand on the hip, bring the leg out to the right
C: right leg up, hands on hips, hold the leg extended straight, foot pointed.
Repeat on the left side.
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- "Standing leg: Your hip flexors are strongly engaging, while your extensors stretch. Your quadriceps engage to extend your knee, while your hamstrings engage and lengthen." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Utthita Hasta Padangusthasana B: All external rotators of the hip contract in this Asana. The piriformis also assists the lateral fibers of the gluteus medius, abducting the femur." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Single-Leg Balance: Maximum demand on hip stabilizers." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "The standing knee extends. The standing hip is in neutral. The raised knee extends. The raised hip flexes. The shoulder of the raised arm flexes." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Your ankle dorsiflexors help your ankle move into deeper dorsiflexion as you transition into the pose. Your fibularis muscles help to stabilize." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"Guide students into feeling the connection between pada bandha and mula bandha, encouraging them to maintain mula bandha throughout their asana practice." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 836) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"While in the state of this asana, the lower abdomen should be drawn in and held tightly." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1512) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Recaka kumbhaka [breath retention after exhale] must be done in this sthiti. That is, expel the breath completely from the body... pull in the stomach as much as one’s strength allows and hold it in." 
*(Yoga Makaranda, T. Krishnamacharya, p. 1462) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- "If they [gluteus medius and tensor fascia lata] didn't engage, the pelvis would shift over to the standing-leg side and we would lose balance." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Hyperextending or 'locking' the knees... can cause misalignment of the leg bones." *(The Physiology of Yoga, Andrew McGonigle and Matthew Huy) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Single-leg balance: Maximum demand on hip stabilizers." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Maintain a slight bend in joints, particularly weight-bearing ones... try microbending the knee in straight-legged poses." *(The Physiology of Yoga, Andrew McGonigle and Matthew Huy) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Press into the ball of the foot (metatarsals) while driving the shin forward slightly. This will require more effort but can contribute to building strength in the asana." *(The Physiology of Yoga, Andrew McGonigle and Matthew Huy) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Use a wall to balance at first, and train yourself to lift the leg in the air by activating the hip flexors." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Initially, when you first practise this without any support, you might fall down. In that case, after slowly exhaling the breath out, firmly hold the breath. This will prevent a fall." *(Yoga Makaranda, T. Krishnamacharya, p. 1464) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*
