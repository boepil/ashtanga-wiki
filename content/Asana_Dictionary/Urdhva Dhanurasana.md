# Urdhva Dhanurasana

"Urdhva means upwards. Dhanu means a bow. In this posture the body is arched back and supported on the palms and soles." 

![Urdhva-Dhanurasana](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Urdhva-Dhanurasana.png)


## Sanskrit Meaning
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 357) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"In the Ashtanga Primary Series, it is sequenced as the 36th posture, performed after Setu Bandhasana." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 753) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Lie flat on the back... Bend and raise the elbows over the head, and place the palms under the shoulders... fingers should point towards the feet. Bend and raise the knees... Exhale, raise the trunk and rest the crown of the head on the floor... exhale, lift the trunk and head and arch the back so that its weight is taken on the palms and the soles." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 363–364) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
Third eye or tip of the nose
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Movement
From lying down position bend the knees, feet close to the buttocks, feet parallel and firmly planted, palms by the ears close to shoulders, fingers towards the feet, lift the pelvis, straighten the arms, head loose. Repeat 3 times.
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- **Spinal Extensors:** "Erector spinae, longissimus, and iliocostalis contract powerfully to achieve the arch." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Gluteus Maximus:** "Contracts to extend the hips and lift the pelvis." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Quadriceps:** "Rectus femoris and vastus muscles extend the knees to push the weight toward the arms." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Shoulder Stabilizers:** "Triceps brachii and serratus anterior extend the arms and stabilize the scapulae." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "Spinal extension (total). Hip extension. Knee extension. Shoulder flexion and abduction. Wrist extension." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"Spiraling your inner thighs back... draw the outside of your hips in toward the core of your body, feeling an accentuated lift of your perineum [mula bandha]." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 692) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Extend the chest, stretch up the sacral region of the spine until the abdomen is taut as a drum." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 365) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- **Lumbar Compression:** "Lifting with the lower back instead of the hips and chest can cause impingement in the lumbar vertebrae." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 95) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Wrist Strain:** "Dumping weight into the wrists without engaging the fingers or the shoulders can lead to ligament strain." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 98) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Root the hands and feet equally. Ensure the knees stay parallel and do not splay outwards, which protects the lower back." *(A Little Book of Yoga, Orit Sen-Gupta, p. 47) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*
- "Rule: If the arms cannot straighten, focus on the lift from the legs and the crown of the head on the mat until the shoulders open sufficiently." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 165) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Teaching Notes
"In the Mysore Palace gymnastics tradition, this asana was referred to as valvarase, meaning 'exercise against the wall'." 
*(The Yoga Tradition of the Mysore Palace, N.E. Sjoman, p. 54) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*
