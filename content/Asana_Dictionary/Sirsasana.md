# Sirsasana

"Sirsa means the head. This is the Headstand pose, often called the 'King of Asanas'." 

![Sirsasana-A](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Sirsasana-A.png)


## Sanskrit Meaning
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 179) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Variations
### Sirsasana A
Crown of the head on the floor, hands clasped together behind the head, legs straight and up, forearm firmly engaged on the mat.
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

### Sirsasana B (Ardha Sirsasana)
From Sirsasana-A, bring straight legs down parallel to the ground (90 degrees). Rest other movement like Sirsasana – A.
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"Sirsasana has four vinyasas. The 3rd is the state of the asana." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1411) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Spread a blanket folded four times... Kneel down... Interlace the fingers up to the fingertips so that the palms form a cup. Rest the outer edges of the wrists on the blanket... Place the crown of the head on the blanket so that the back of the head touches the palms... After securing the head position, raise the knees from the floor by moving the toes closer to the head." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 180–181) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
Tip of the nose

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- **Shoulder Stabilizers:** "Serratus anterior, trapezius, and deltoids engage powerfully to push the floor away and protect the neck." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Deep Core:** "Transversus abdominis and psoas maintain the vertical alignment and prevent the back from arching." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Spinal Extensors:** "Engaged to keep the trunk upright and stable." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "Cervical neutral (ideally) with slight axial extension. Shoulder flexion and abduction. Elbow flexion. Hip extension (in A) or flexion (in B)." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"In the state of the asana, engage Mula Bandha and Uddiyana Bandha to stabilize the pelvis and maintain the vertical line." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 790) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"The chin should not press the chest; instead, the neck should be lengthened." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 182) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- **Cervical Overloading:** "If the forearms do not press firmly, too much weight is dumped into the crown of the head, risking compression of the cervical vertebrae." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 130) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Lumbar Arching:** "A weak core leads to a 'banana back,' which strains the lumbar spine and destabilizes the pose." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 132) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Ensure 80-90% of the weight is on the forearms and only 10-20% on the head." <div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>
- "Rule: If the neck feels any sharp pain or compression, come down immediately and rest in Balasana (Child's Pose)." <div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>
