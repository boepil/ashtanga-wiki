# Eka Pada Bakasana A

"Eka means one, Pada means foot, and Baka means a crane. This is a one-legged variation of the Crane Pose."

![Bakasana](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/Bakasana-copy-e1583305834400-300x268.png)

## Sanskrit Meaning
*(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, p. 11) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Detail
A one-legged arm balance where one knee is tucked into the armpit (as in Bakasana) and the other leg is extended straight back and upward.

## Anatomical Focus
- **Asymmetrical Core Strength**: Requires intense engagement of the transverse abdominis and obliques to balance the asymmetrical load.
- **Triceps & Shoulders**: High demand on the upper body to maintain the lift while the center of gravity shifts.
- **Hip Extension**: The back leg requires active engagement of the gluteus maximus and hamstrings to stay lifted.
