# Vatayanasana (Horse Pose)

![Vatayanasana](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Vatayanasana.png)

## Sanskrit Meaning
"Vatayana means a horse. The pose resembles a horse's face, hence the name." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 105) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"Vatayanasana has five vinyasas. The 3rd is the state of the asana." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1437) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Stand in Samasthiti. Place the left foot on the root of the right thigh in half-Padmasana. Bend the right knee and lower the hips until the left knee touches the floor. Join the palms in front of the chest or interlace the arms (Gomukhasana or Eagle arms variation)." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 105–106) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"In the Ashtanga sequence, the arms are often entwined like Garuda-asana." 
*(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
Third eye (nasagre/midbrow)
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Movement
One leg in half-lotus, kneeling on that knee, other foot flat on floor with knee bent at 90 degrees, arms entwined.
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- **Quadriceps (Right leg):** "Powerful eccentric and isometric engagement to maintain the 90-degree bend." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Hip Rotators (Left leg):** "Intense external rotation and flexion required for the half-lotus position while weight-bearing." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Core:** "Stabilizes the upright torso during the balance." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "Hip external rotation and flexion (lotus leg). Hip flexion and knee flexion (standing leg). Shoulder protraction (if using eagle arms)." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"Mula Bandha is essential to protect the knee of the lotus leg. By engaging the pelvic floor, you stabilize the hip joint, reducing the lateral torque on the knee." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 813) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- **Knee Strain:** "Lowering the weight onto the kneeling lotus leg without sufficient hip openness can cause acute knee pain or ligament damage." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 345) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Loss of Balance:** "Leaning forward shifts the center of gravity and increases pressure on the kneeling joint." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 348) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Push the pelvis forward to keep the torso vertical." <div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>
- "Rule: Use a wall for balance until the hip and knee are strong enough to support the full weight." <div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Teaching Notes
"This asana is unique in that it combines the hip opening of Padmasana with a balance that requires significant lower body strength." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 106) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*
