# Uttana Padasana

"Uttana means intense stretch. Pada means a foot. In this posture, the legs are intensely stretched and lifted while the body is arched." 

![Uttana-Padasana](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Uttana-Padasana.png)


## Sanskrit Meaning
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 118) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"Uttana Padasana is the vinyasa immediately following Matsyasana." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1410) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"From Matsyasana, exhale, lift the legs up to an angle of 45 degrees. Simultaneously lift the arms parallel to the legs, palms together." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 118) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"catvāri 4: (Transition from Matsyasana) inhale, lift the legs and arms to 45 degrees. Breathe eight times." 
*(Ashtanga Yoga Anusthana, Sharath Jois, p. 50) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
Third eye
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Movement
Legs straight and extended at 45 degrees, arms extended parallel to the legs, palms together. The crown of the head remains on the floor.
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- **Hip Flexors:** "Psoas and iliacus are intensely active to hold the weight of the legs at 45 degrees." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Deep Core:** "Transversus abdominis and rectus abdominis engage to stabilize the pelvis and protect the lower back." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Quadriceps:** "Engaged to keep the legs straight and the knees locked." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "Hip flexion (45 degrees). Knee extension. Shoulder flexion. Thoracic and cervical extension." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"Mula Bandha and Uddiyana Bandha are critical in this pose to support the weight of the legs and arms while the spine is in extension." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 789) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- **Lower Back Strain:** "If the core is weak, the weight of the legs can cause excessive lordosis and strain the lumbar spine." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 125) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Neck Instability:** "Lifting the legs and arms reduces the surface area of the foundation, potentially increasing pressure on the crown of the head." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 128) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Keep the palms pressed firmly together to help stabilize the upper body." <div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>
- "Rule: If the lower back feels strained, lower the legs slightly or return to Matsyasana." <div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Teaching Notes
"This asana intensifies the benefits of Matsyasana, further stimulating the thyroid and strengthening the core." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 119) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*
