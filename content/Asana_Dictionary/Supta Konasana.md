# Supta Konasana

"Supta means lying down and kona an angle." 

![Supta-Konasana](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Supta-Konasana.png)


## Sanskrit Meaning
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 247) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"Supta Konasana has fourteen vinyasas, the 9th of which is the asana sthiti." 
*(Yoga Makaranda, T. Krishnamacharya, p. 99) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"In the 8th vinyasa... lift both the legs up and... bring the legs over the head, open them out wide on the floor, and hold the big toes... this is the 8th vinyasa." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1404) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"From the 8th vinyasa, come up into the 9th vinyasa of Upavishta Konasana (balancing on the buttocks), then exhale and slowly lower into the 8th vinyasa of Upavishta Konasana (chest to floor)." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1404) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
Tip of the nose
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Movement
From lying down position with inhalation lift the legs over the head, rock back to the floor, legs wide apart, lengthen spine upwards, avoid pressure on neck. Roll forward into a seated position.
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- **Abdominals:** "Rectus abdominis and psoas work powerfully to control the descent and the rolling action." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Hamstrings:** "Intense lengthening as the legs are spread wide over the head." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Erector Spinae:** "Manage the spinal curve during the roll and the final fold." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "Hip abduction and flexion. Spinal flexion and then extension. Shoulder internal rotation (to catch toes)." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"While in the state, the abdomen should be drawn in completely, but without mula bandha, or even uddiyana bandha. Aspirants should remember this." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1405) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Remember that the stomach needs to be pulled in and held in." 
*(Yoga Makaranda, T. Krishnamacharya, p. 99) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- **Neck Compression:** "The most critical risk is putting weight on the cervical vertebrae during the 'legs over head' phase. Weight must remain on the shoulders." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 85) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Lower Back Strain:** "Rounding the lower back aggressively during the roll forward can strain the lumbar ligaments." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 88) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Avoid pressure on the neck. Use the strength of the arms to stabilize the roll." <div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>
- "Engage the core completely to control the momentum of the roll, ensuring a smooth transition to the seated position." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 145) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Teaching Notes
