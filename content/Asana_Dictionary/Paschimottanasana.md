## Sanskrit Meaning
"Paschima literally means the west. It implies the back of the whole body from the head to the heels. The anterior or eastern aspect is the front of the body from the face down to the toes. ... In this asana the back of the whole body is intensely stretched, hence the name." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 263) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"The classic forward bend, Paschimottanasana, translates from Sanskrit as 'west stretching pose,' signifying the sunset of a practice traditionally done facing the rising sun." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 765) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"Paschimattanasana has sixteen vinyasas. The 9th is its state." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1436) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Doing puraka [inhale] and with only the strength of the arms, jump the legs between the hands without allowing them to touch the floor, and stretch out the legs. Then press the hands to the floor on either side of the hips ... sit erect ... this constitutes the 7th vinyasa. Next, doing rechaka [exhale], grasp and hold the upper parts of the feet; this is the 8th vinyasa. Then, doing puraka slowly, then rechaka, straighten both legs, and place the head between the knees; this is the 9th vinyasa and the state of the asana." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1436) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"In the 10th vinyasa raise the head. In the 11th vinyasa, keeping the hands firmly pressed on the ground, raise the entire body off the ground and balance it in the air without touching the ground. The 11th vinyasa is called uthpluthi. The 12th vinyasa is caturanga dandasana. ... Afterwards, return to samasthiti." 
*(Yoga Makaranda, T. Krishnamacharya, p. 127-128) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Movement
Variations A, B, C: bend forward from the hips, feet flexed, hold onto big toes, bring the chest to the thighs, chin towards shin bone, root lock.
Variation D: bend forward from the hips, feet flexed, grab the right wrist with the left hand, bring the chest to the thighs, chin towards shin bone, root lock.

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- "Contracting the quadriceps, iliopsoas, and biceps during the forward bend Paschimottanasana is a form of active static stretching of the hamstrings through reciprocal inhibition." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "In this pose, the quadriceps contract concentrically, while the hamstrings lengthen eccentrically... The tibialis anterior contract concentrically, creating dorsiflexion of the ankle." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The pelvis tilts forwards when the iliopsoas shortens, creating a forwards flexion at the lumbar spine. The gluteus maximus and erector spinae then lengthen eccentrically..." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The biceps contract, flexing the elbows to draw the upper body forward... This force is transmitted through the posterior kinetic chain to the pelvis, tilting it forward." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "Joint Action: Deep hip flexion, ankle dorsiflexion." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The spine moves in a sagittal plane from a frontal axis and is in forwards flexion. The pelvis is in an anterior tilt." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"It is worth noting that, for this asana, one has to retract, or squeeze, and hold the anus tightly, as well as squeeze the lower abdomen and hold it in, and concentrate on the nadis related to the kanda ... the apana vayu ... moves upward and becomes one with prana vayu." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1438) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Then, pull in the stomach while doing recaka, lower the head and press the face down onto the knee. The knees should not rise from the ground in this sthiti either." 
*(Yoga Makaranda, T. Krishnamacharya, p. 127) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"This Paschima Tana carries the air from the front to the back part of the body (i.e., to the susumna). It kindles gastric fire, reduces obesity and cures all diseases of men." 
*(Hatha Yoga Pradipika, Swami Swatmarama, p. 83) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- "If hamstrings are tight, they pull the pelvis into Posterior Tilt (tucking under), forcing the lumbar spine to round. This loads the intervertebral discs safely." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Do not allow your thighs to lift off the floor, as the muscles at the back of your knees might rupture." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "If a beginner has a posterior pelvic tilt and over-straightens the knees... this will eventually intensify the lumbar flexion and can lead to back pain or a hamstrings injury." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "If the lower back rounds, bend the knees! This creates slack in the hamstrings, allowing the pelvis to tilt forward and the spine to neutralize." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Engage the Quadriceps (front of thighs) to signal the Hamstrings (back of thighs) to relax." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Avoid using arm strength to 'pull' yourself down. This often injures the lower back or hamstring attachments." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Sit on a folded blanket or block. This helps tilt the pelvis forward in folds." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
