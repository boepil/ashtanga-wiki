## Sanskrit Meaning
"This asana is dedicated to the sage Marichi, son of the Creator, Brahma." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 239) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Maricha Maharishi was known for bringing this asana to public knowledge and hence it is named for him." 
*(Yoga Makaranda, T. Krishnamacharya, p. 1499) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

**Sanskrit Breakdown:** *Marīci* (מארִיצ’י) means "Ray of Light" (of the sun). Marichi is one of the *Sapta-Rishi* (seven ancient sages).

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"The first and second forms of the asana (A & B) have twenty-two vinyasas, and the third and fourth (C & D) have eighteen vinyasas each." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1567) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

**Variation A (Forward Fold):**
"Stretch the left leg out... bend the right knee up, pulling the right heel in toward the right buttock, take the right arm around the knee of the bent leg, bring the left hand behind the back and take hold of the right wrist... this is the 7th vinyasa." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1568) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

**Variation B (Forward Fold with Half-Lotus):**
"In the 7th vinyasa, sit with the legs stretched out, bring the left heel toward the navel, as in Padmasana, bend the right knee... bring the right arm around the right shin, grasp hold of the left wrist with the right hand... touch the nose to the floor... this is the 8th vinyasa." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1569) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

**Variation C (Seated Twist):**
"Turn the waist toward the right, bring the left arm around the front of the right knee, twisting the left hand and arm around toward the back, bring the right arm around the back and grasp the left wrist with the right hand... this is the 7th vinyasa, and the asana’s state." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1570) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

**Variation D (Twist with Half-Lotus):**
"Sit, bending the arms and legs as in Marichyasana (B), turn the waist, bring the left arm around the front of the right knee and around toward the back, as in Marichyasana (C)... this is the 7th vinyasa." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1571) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
- **A:** Big toe of the extended leg
- **B:** Tip of the nose
- **C:** To the right side (or left when repeating)
- **D:** To the right side (or left when repeating)

## Movement
**Variation A:** Right knee bent, foot four fingers away from thigh, wrap right arm around bent leg, grab left wrist behind back, fold forward, chin to shin.
**Variation B:** Left leg in Padmasana, right knee bent, wrap right arm around right leg, grab left wrist, bend forward, chin to shin.
**Variation C:** Right knee bent, twist & wrap left arm around right leg, grab right wrist.
**Variation D:** Left leg in Padmasana, right knee bent, wrap left arm around right leg, grab right wrist.

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- **A/B (Folding):** "Iliopsoas: The primary hip flexor... Crucial for initiating the fold. Erector Spinae... Manage spinal extension and resist gravity." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 16) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **C/D (Twisting):** "The oblique muscles work on either side of the torso to create the rotation of the thoracic spine and ribcage." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Lotus component (B/D):** "Hip External Rotators position the hips for Padmasana. Gluteus medius contracts and abducts the bent leg." *(Key Muscles of Yoga, Ray Long) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "Forward folds with binding (shoulder internal rotation, scapular protraction)." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 54) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The spine rotates primarily due to the activation of the internal and external oblique muscles." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"With each exhale, renew the light lifting of the perineum, cultivating mula bandha while energizing up through the spine." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 839) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"The heel at the navel [in variations B/D] puts extra pressure on the abdomen so that the abdominal organs are toned better." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 244, 311) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- **Knee Torque:** "If the hip stops rotating (due to bone structure or tight ligaments) and you force the foot higher [in Lotus variations B/D], the torque transfers to the knee. This stresses the medial collateral ligament (MCL) and meniscus." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 17) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Lumbar Limitation:** "It is difficult to flex the lumbar spine in the Sage Marichi pose... consciously decrease hip flexion and increase lumbar flexion [to achieve balance]." *(Identifying Postural Imbalances, Vayu Jung Doohwa, p. 148) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Rule: Never force the knee. ... Full flexion of the knee (heel to glute) before rotating the hip minimizes the rotational torque on the knee ligaments." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 18) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Binding Modification: If hands don't meet... use a Strap or simply hold the shirt. Do not sacrifice spinal length to grab hands." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 32) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
