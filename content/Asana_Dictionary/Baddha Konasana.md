# Baddha Konasana (Bound Angle Pose)

![Baddha-Konasana-A](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Baddha-Konasana-A.png)
![Baddha-Konasana-b](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Baddha-Konasana-b.png)

## Sanskrit Meaning
"Baddha means caught, restrained. Kona means an angle. In this posture, sit on the floor, bring the heels near the perineum, catch the feet and widen the thighs until the knees touch the floor." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 177) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"Baddha Konasana has fifteen vinyasas, and the 8th vinyasa is the asana sthiti." 
*(Yoga Makaranda, T. Krishnamacharya, p. 93) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Join the feet, fold them open, press the heels to the sivani nadi [perineum], hold the feet open with both the hands... sit with knees on the floor; this is the 7th vinyasa." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1400) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

**Variation A:** "Doing rechaka, fold forward, place the head on the floor... this is the 8th vinyasa." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1400) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

**Variation B:** "Bend forward to bring forehead to sole of feet."

## Drishti
Tip of the nose or third eye
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Movement
Bring the feet together and close to the perineum, hold the feet with both hands and open the soles out like a book, knees towards the floor.
**Variation A:** Lean forward, chin to chest (neutral spine).
**Variation B:** Bend forward to bring forehead to sole of feet (rounded spine).
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- **Adductors:** "Pectineus, adductor brevis, longus, and magnus are intensely stretched in this position." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Hip External Rotators:** "Sartorius and gluteus maximus help to facilitate the external rotation and abduction of the thighs." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Erector Spinae:** "Engagement maintains the vertical lift of the spine before the fold." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "Hip abduction and external rotation. Knee flexion (close to 180°). Pelvic anterior tilt (Variation A) vs. Posterior tilt (Variation B)." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"While in the states of this asana, one should do rechaka and tighten the anus fully... pulling the stomach in completely." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1401) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Maintain a firm chin lock [Jalandhara Bandha] in Variation A." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1400) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- **Knee Strain:** "Forcing the knees down without sufficient hip mobility can put excessive load on the medial collateral ligament (MCL)." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 75) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **SI Joint Stress:** "Asymmetrical pressure on the feet or forcing the heels too close can destabilize the sacroiliac joints." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 78) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Ground through the sitting bones; keep cultivating pelvic neutrality... Never force the knees down." *(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 770) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*
- "Use the hands to 'open the feet like a book'—this external rotation of the feet helps to safely facilitate external rotation in the hip joint." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 135) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Teaching Notes
