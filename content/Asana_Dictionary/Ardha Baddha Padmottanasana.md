# Ardha Baddha Padmottanasana

"Ardha means half. Baddha means bound, restrained, caught, withheld. Padma is a lotus. Uttana is an intense stretch." 

![Ardha-Baddha-Padmottanasana](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Ardha-Baddha-Padmottanasana-1.png)


## Sanskrit Meaning
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 210) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"Ardha Baddha Padmottanasana has nine vinyasas..." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 210) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"This has 10 vinyasas." 
*(Yoga Makaranda, T. Krishnamacharya, p. 1427) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Stand in Tadasana. Inhale, raise the right leg from the floor, bend the right knee and rest the sole of the right foot on the left thigh. Hold the right foot with the left hand, bring the right arm round the back and catch hold of the big toe of the right foot... Release the left hand. Exhale, bend the trunk forward, place the left hand on the floor by the left foot, and keep the head up. Make the back as concave as possible." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 210-211) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Choose either leg and place its foot on top of the opposite thigh. Slowly, little by little, move the foot up until the back of the heel is pressed against the lower abdomen. ... Release the breath out completely, and without inhaling, practise kumbhaka and lower the head, placing it on top of the kneecap of the extended leg." 
*(Yoga Makaranda, T. Krishnamacharya, p. 1427) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"To come up, inhaling, lift to the Ardha positioning, stay to exhale and feel the belly draw to the spine, then use that support inhaling back up to standing." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 800) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
Tip of the nose

## Movement
Right leg in Padmasana, wrap the right arm around behind the back, hold the big toe, bend forward, left hand on the floor. Repeat on the left side.
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- "Complex Stabilization: Single-leg balance + hip external rotation + forward fold... Standing leg manages all previous balance demands with reduced base of support." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 77, 80) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The gluteus medius and tensor fascia lata... pull downward on the ilium bone of the pelvis when we stand on one leg... If they didn't engage, the pelvis would shift over to the standing-leg side and we would lose balance." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long, p. 445) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Binding creates proprioceptive challenge." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 77, 80) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "Introduces lotus prep (external rotation with knee flexion)." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 77, 80) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"From tadasana, do puraka kumbhaka. ... Whichever leg is raised, move the same hand behind the back and clasp the big toe... Keep the other hand in tadasana sthiti and do puraka kumbhaka. ... without inhaling, practise kumbhaka and lower the head." 
*(Yoga Makaranda, T. Krishnamacharya, p. 1427) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Stay to exhale and feel the belly draw to the spine, then use that support inhaling back up to standing." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 800) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- "If the hip stops rotating (due to bone structure or tight ligaments) and you force the foot higher, the torque transfers to the knee. This stresses the medial collateral ligament (MCL) and meniscus." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 27) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The attempt to adduct and twist the tibia 70° [for Lotus] will cause the medial femoral condyle to increasingly compress into the medial tibial condyle. Trapped between the two condyles is the medial meniscus." *(Your Body, Your Yoga, Bernie Clark, p. 573) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The load on the lumbar spine in a standing Forward Fold is significant." *(Science of Yoga, Ann Swanson, p. 305, 337) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Pregnant women should not do this asana. ... all other women can practise this regularly and freely." *(Yoga Makaranda, T. Krishnamacharya, p. 135, 1447) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "יש לכופף את הרגל בעליה כדי שלא יהיה עומס על הברך (Bend the [standing] leg on the way up to avoid load on the knee)." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 34, 111) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Rule: No rotation torque [in the knee]... In Hip Openers (Lotus...), keep the knee flexion closed (heel to butt)." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 47) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Never force the knee. If there is any pain in the knee, back off. The knee should remain closed (flexed) to protect itself." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 27) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Transition in and out of the pose with bent knees and an engaged core [to protect the lumbar spine]." *(Science of Yoga, Ann Swanson, p. 305, 337) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
