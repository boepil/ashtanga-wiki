# Karandavasana

"Karandava is a type of Himalayan duck. This asana resembles a duck landing or resting." 

![Karandavasana](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Karandavasana.png)


## Sanskrit Meaning
*(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"Karandavasana has twelve vinyasas. The 7th is the state of the asana." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1434) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"From Pincha Mayurasana, fold the legs into Padmasana (Lotus). Exhale, lower the lotus legs until they touch the back of the upper arms. Balance in this position, then lift the legs back up into Pincha Mayurasana." 
*(Ashtanga Yoga Anusthana, Sharath Jois, p. 62) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"sapta 7: inhale, fold legs into lotus and lower to the arms. Breathe five times." 
*(Ashtanga Yoga Anusthana, Sharath Jois, p. 62) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
Nose (nasagre)
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Movement
Forearm balance with Lotus legs, lowering the lotus to rest on the upper arms, then lifting back up.
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- **Serratus Anterior:** "Extremely active to maintain the 'lift' while the weight of the legs is lowered forward." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Hip Rotators:** "Active to maintain the Lotus position during the inversion." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Abdominals:** "Intense engagement to control the descent and the powerful lift back up." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "Shoulder flexion. Hip external rotation and flexion (in lotus). Knee flexion. Spinal flexion (mild to control the descent)." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"Uddiyana Bandha is the 'jack' that lifts the lotus back up. The suction of the belly creates the upward force needed to return to Pincha Mayurasana from the arm-rest position." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 810) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- **Shoulder Instability:** "The shifting center of gravity puts massive load on the rotator cuff tendons." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 315) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Knee Torque:** "Maintaining Lotus while inverted and moving can stress the knee if the hips are not open." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 318) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Keep the elbows narrow; if they spread, the stability of the pose is lost." <div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>
- "Rule: Focus on the 'suction' of the belly to lift the legs, rather than just pushing with the arms." <div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Teaching Notes
"Karandavasana is one of the most physically demanding poses in the Second Series, requiring advanced strength, balance, and hip mobility." 
*(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*
