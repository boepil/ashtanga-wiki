# Supta Kurmasana

"Supta means reclined or sleeping. Kurma means a tortoise." 

![Supta-Kurmasana](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Supta-Kurmasana.png)


## Sanskrit Meaning
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1573) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"Supta Kurmasana continues from the Kurmasana sequence; the state is the 9th vinyasa." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1573) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"From the 8th vinyasa, cross the legs over each other behind the head, place the forehead on the floor, and grasp the hands behind the back... this is the 9th vinyasa." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1573) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
Tip of the nose or third eye
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Movement
Jump over upper arm from downward dog then sit down, legs extended to the sides, arms extended to the sides underneath the legs (knees over arms), chin and chest to the floor, cross the right foot over the left, place over neck, clasp the hands together behind the back.
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- **Hip External Rotators:** "Gluteus maximus and deep rotators (piriformis, gemelli) are intensely stretched to allow the feet to cross behind the head." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Shoulder Internal Rotators:** "Subscapularis and teres major engage to bring the arms behind the back for the bind." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "Extreme hip flexion, abduction, and external rotation. Cervical flexion (to place feet behind head). Shoulder internal rotation and extension." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"Use the strength of the waist and legs to maintain the position while doing rechaka and puraka." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1573) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"The compression of the abdomen against the thighs naturally encourages a deep uddiyana bandha." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 890) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- **Cervical Spine Strain:** "Forcing the feet behind the head without sufficient hip mobility can put dangerous pressure on the cervical vertebrae." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 60) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Sacroiliac (SI) Joint Instability:** "The extreme rotation and flexion of the hips can stress the SI joints if the core is not engaged." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 62) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Rule: Hips first, head second. Only attempt to cross the feet behind the head if the knees are comfortably behind the shoulders." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 120) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Engage the neck muscles (sternocleidomastoid) to resist the weight of the legs and maintain a neutral cervical curve where possible." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 122) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
