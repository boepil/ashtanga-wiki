# Bhujapidasana (Arm Pressure Pose)

![Bhujapidasana-A](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Bhujapidasana-A.png)
![Bhujapidasana-b](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Bhujapidasana-b.png)

## Sanskrit Meaning
"Bhuja means an arm or shoulder. Pida means pain or pressure." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 263) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"Bhujapidasana has fifteen vinyasas, and the 8th vinyasa is the state of the asana." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1572) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"From Adho Mukha Svanasana (6th vinyasa), jump the legs around the shoulders without touching the floor; squeeze the shoulders forcefully with the thighs and straighten the arms... this is the 7th vinyasa." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1572) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"In the 8th vinyasa, bend the elbows and lean forward until the chin touches the floor, while crossing the feet. This is the state of the asana." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1572) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
Tip of the nose
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Movement
**Variation A:** Jump over upper arm from downward dog, wrap legs around arm, fingers engaged & spread, straight arms.
**Variation B:** From Variation A, exhale, bend elbows & lean forward to place chin on ground.
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- **Shoulder Stabilizers:** "Serratus anterior and pectoralis minor protract the scapulae to create a stable base for the lift." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Arm Strength:** "Triceps brachii and anconeus extend the elbows to support the bodyweight." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Inner Thighs:** "Adductor magnus, longus, and brevis squeeze the thighs against the upper arms to maintain the bind." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "Deep hip flexion and adduction. Shoulder protraction and depression." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Wrist extension (neutral to 90°) supports the vertical load." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"The state of the asana must be performed only in rechaka (exhale)." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1572) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Maintain a strong uddiyana bandha to lift the center of gravity." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 890) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- **Wrist Strain:** "Dumping weight into the wrists without engaging the fingers (Hasta Bandha) can lead to carpal tunnel compression or ligament strain." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 45) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Shoulder Impingement:** "Collapsing into the shoulders instead of protracting the scapulae can stress the rotator cuff." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 48) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Engage the fingertips into the mat like claws to distribute weight off the wrists." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 110) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Ensure the thighs are high enough on the arms (above the elbows) before attempting the chin-to-floor transition." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 112) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Teaching Notes
"To exit, take the legs back without touching the floor and balance on the arms (Tittibhasana transition - 10th vinyasa) before jumping back to Bakasana or directly to Chaturanga." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1573) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*
