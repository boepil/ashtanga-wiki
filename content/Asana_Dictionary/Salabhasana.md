# Salabhasana

"Salabha means a locust. The pose resembles that of a locust resting on the ground, hence the name." 

![Salabhasana](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/Salabhasana-e1583308268509-300x170.png)


## Sanskrit Meaning
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 173) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Variations
### Salabhasana A
Lie on the stomach, arms back, palms up. Exhale, lift the head, chest, and legs off the floor as high as possible. Only the abdomen bears the weight.
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

### Salabhasana B
Same as A, but with hands under the shoulders (as if for Bhujangasana) or with fingers interlaced behind the back.
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"Salabhasana is ranked third in Jois’s Intermediate Series list." 
*(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Lie full length on the floor on the stomach, face downwards. Stretch the arms back. Exhale, lift the head, chest and legs off the floor as high as possible... Keep the ribs off the floor." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 173–175) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- **Erector Spinae:** "Intensely active to lift the torso and maintain spinal extension." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Gluteus Maximus & Hamstrings:** "Engaged to lift the legs and maintain hip extension." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Trapezius & Rhomboids:** "Retract the scapulae to open the chest." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "Spinal extension. Hip extension. Shoulder extension (in A) or neutral/flexion (in B)." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"Mula Bandha is essential to stabilize the pelvis and prevent excessive compression in the lumbar spine during the lift." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 796) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- **Lumbar Compression:** "Lifting the legs too high without enough abdominal support can cause impingement in the lower back." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 182) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Neck Strain:** "Cranking the head back too aggressively can compress the cervical vertebrae." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 185) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Think about lengthening the body forward and back rather than just lifting up." <div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>
- "Rule: Keep the neck long and gaze at the tip of the nose to avoid neck strain." <div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Teaching Notes
"This asana aids digestion, relieves gastric troubles and flatulence, and strengthens the entire back." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 175) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*
