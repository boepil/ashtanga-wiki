# Dandasana (Staff Pose)

![dandasana](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/dandasana.png)

## Sanskrit Meaning
"Danda means a staff or rod. In this asana, the body remains straight like a stick (Danda) and strengthens the spine, hands and legs." 
*(Krishnamacharya's Yogasanagalu, T. Krishnamacharya, p. 189, 250) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"dandasana (staff). ... When one sits like a staff, with the legs extended and the ankles, big toes and knees together, that is the 'stick' posture." 
*(Roots of Yoga, James Mallinson and Mark Singleton, p. 621, 622) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"Dandasana is the first posture among the sitting asanas." 
*(Krishnamacharya's Yogasanagalu, T. Krishnamacharya, p. 188) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Sit on the floor with the legs stretched straight in front. Place the palms on the floor by the hips, the fingers pointing to the feet. Stretch the hands straight and keep the back erect." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 250) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"The student then jumps through his or her arms to a sitting position with the legs stretched out straight in front. ... This movement ... corresponds to the 'jump through' to daṇḍāsana in Ashtanga Vinyasa. From here, the student assumes the asana itself, which is held for five breaths." 
*(Yoga Body: The Origins of Modern Posture Practice, Mark Singleton, p. 204-205) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"With practice, move fluidly from Dandasana to Tolasana to Lolasana to Chaturanga Dandasana." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 736) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Movement
Extend the legs to the front, feet flexed, hands on the floor by the hips, spine straight, Use root, abdomen & throat lock, pull the scapulas down.

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- "Erector Spinae: (Spinalis, Longissimus, Iliocostalis) Manage spinal extension and resist gravity in forward folds." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 21) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Hip flexor strength [is required] to maintain 90° hip flexion... Spinal extensors counter gravitational flexion." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 68) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Tighten your quadriceps and pull them towards your groin. Press your thighs down on the floor, and counter that pressure by lifting your waist." *(B. K. S. Iyengar Yoga: The Path to Holistic Health, B. K. S. Iyengar, p. 125) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Contract the erector spinae to straighten the spine... Draw the scapulae toward the midline by contracting the rhomboids... Contract the latissimus dorsi to further open the chest... Lift the spine by gently contracting the triceps." *(Key Muscles of Yoga, Ray Long, p. 292) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"After exhalation, pull in the region of abdomen in all the way into the navel. During inhalation, the chest is to be expanded. Rechaka has to be longer than Puraka." 
*(Krishnamacharya's Yogasanagalu, T. Krishnamacharya, p. 188, 189) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"[Mula Bandha] should be learnt while doing various asanas, especially Tadasana, Sirsasana, Sarvangasana, Urdhva Dhanurasana, Ustrasana and Paschimottanasana [Dandasana is foundational to this group]." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 471) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"The chin is lowered into the chest by bending the neck." 
*(Krishnamacharya's Yogasanagalu, T. Krishnamacharya, p. 188) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*
