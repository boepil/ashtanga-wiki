"Parsva means side or flank. Uttana (ut = intense, and tan = to extend, stretch, lengthen) means an intense stretch. The name implies a pose in which the side of the chest is stretched intensely." 

![Uthitha-Parsvottanasana-A](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Uthitha-Parsvottanasana-A.png)


## Sanskrit Meaning
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 192) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"Parshvottanasana has only five vinyasas, the 2nd and 4th of which are the states of the asana." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1516) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Jump to the right, standing with the legs three feet apart... bring hands together behind the back in a prayer position and, doing puraka [inhale], turn the right foot and waist to the right, and lift the chest; this is the 1st vinyasa. Then, doing rechaka [exhale] slowly, touch the nose to the knee without bending the knees... this is the 2nd vinyasa." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1517) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Then, doing puraka, lift the head up and turn to face the left... this is the 3rd vinyasa. Then, doing rechaka, touch the knee with the nose and hold... this is the 4th vinyasa." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1517) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
Tip of the nose

## Movement
Feet 3ft apart, right foot 90, left foot 45, hands in Paschim namaskar mudra (in prayer behind the back), fold forward towards the right knee, bring the chin to the shin. Repeat on the left side.

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- "The front leg hamstring [is in] eccentric control [and the] back leg hip flexor [is] lengthening under load." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 73) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The quadriceps are concentrically contracted, extending the knee joints, while the sartorius hip flexor muscle, and other hip external rotators, externally rotates the left leg." *(The Manual of Yoga Anatomy, Sally Parkes, p. 348) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The forwards flexion of the spine is caused initially by a concentric shortening of the iliopsoas... The erector spinae, quadratus lumborum, and hamstrings lengthen eccentrically to allow this movement." *(The Manual of Yoga Anatomy, Sally Parkes, p. 348) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "The back foot rotates inward 30 degrees and supinates. The front foot rotates out 90 degrees. The trunk flexes. The front hip flexes and externally rotates. The back hip internally rotates. The knees extend. The shoulders internally rotate. The wrists extend. The cervical spine flexes slightly." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long, p. 420) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The shoulder rotators inwardly rotate the humerus bones and the biceps brachii shorten, while the triceps lengthen to allow flexion at the elbow. This allows the hands to be positioned behind the middle spine with the wrists extended and the palms together." *(The Manual of Yoga Anatomy, Sally Parkes, p. 348) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- "For those predisposed to femoral acetabular impingement syndrome (FAIS), adding internal rotation before commencing [the fold] is not a good idea." *(Your Body, Your Yoga, Bernie Clark, p. 510) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Tightness of the infraspinatus limits internal rotation of the humerus, especially in postures such as Parsvottanasana." *(Key Muscles of Yoga, Ray Long, p. 240 / 273) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "If you have high blood pressure or a cardiac condition, omit Step 4 [looking up/tilting back]. If you have dysentery or an abdominal hernia, practise this asana up to Step 4." *(B. K. S. Iyengar Yoga: The Path to Holistic Health, B. K. S. Iyengar, p. 122) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "To reduce or eliminate hyperextension of the knee... co-contract the quadriceps and the hamstrings by simultaneously hugging the thighs together or simulating sliding the feet together." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long, p. 422) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "There is a tendency to shift the weight onto the outside of the front foot in this pose, inverting the ankle. Counteract this by pressing into the base of the big toe." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long, p. 422) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Roll the shoulders forward to release the external rotators [before entering Reverse Namaste]. Do not force your hands into this position, as you can injure your wrists." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long, p. 421) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
