# Kapotasana

"Kapota means a pigeon. In this posture, the chest is puffed out like that of a pigeon, hence the name." 

![Kapotasana](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/Untitled-design-9-e1583821052402.png)


## Sanskrit Meaning
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 168) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"Kapotasana has fifteen vinyasas. The 8th is the state of the asana." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1425) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Kneel on the floor... arch the back and lower the head to the floor. Reach back with the arms and hold the heels or the soles of the feet. Press the elbows on the floor and puff the chest forward." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 168–169) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"aṣṭau 8: inhale, arch back and hold the heels. Breathe five times." 
*(Ashtanga Yoga Anusthana, Sharath Jois, p. 57) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
Nose (nasagre)

## Movement
Kneeling, deep backbend, holding heels or toes, elbows on floor.
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- **Quadriceps:** "Powerful eccentric load to manage the depth of the backbend and to provide the 'lift' needed to reach the feet." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Iliopsoas:** "Intense stretch in the hip flexors as the hips are pushed forward." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Pectoralis Major:** "Stretched as the chest is expanded and the elbows are grounded." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "Extreme spinal extension (thoracic and lumbar). Hip extension. Knee flexion. Shoulder extension and external rotation." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"Mula Bandha and Uddiyana Bandha are absolutely critical in Kapotasana to prevent 'collapsing' into the lower back. The core must remain active to support the structural integrity of the spine in this extreme extension." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 801) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- **Lumbar Impingement:** "The intensity of the arch can easily lead to compression of the lumbar vertebrae if the hips are not pushed forward." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 225) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Shoulder Girdle Tension:** "Forcing the elbows to the floor without sufficient thoracic mobility can strain the rotator cuff." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 228) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Focus on moving the hips forward and the chest up before reaching for the feet." <div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>
- "Rule: If you cannot breathe freely, you have gone too deep. Back off until the breath is steady." <div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Teaching Notes
"Kapotasana is the 'peak' backbend of the Intermediate Series. It requires a combination of quad strength, hip flexibility, and deep core control." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 170) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*
