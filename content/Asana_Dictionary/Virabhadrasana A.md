# Virabhadrasana A (Warrior I Pose)

![Virabhadrasana-a](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Virabhadrasana-a.png)

## Sanskrit Meaning
"Asanas are named after legendary heroes like Vfrabhadra... Daksa once celebrated a great sacrifice, but he did not invite his daughter Sat! nor her husband Siva... Sat!... threw herself into the fire and perished. When Siva heard this he was gravely provoked, tore a hair from his matted locks and threw it to the ground. A powerful hero named Vlrabhadra rose up... This asana is dedicated to the powerful hero created by Siva from his matted hair." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 140, 157-158) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Virabhadra—the fierce spiritual warrior... Approach the asanas named for Virabhadra—Virabhadrasana I, II, and III—we can encourage students to cultivate the mind of the spiritual warrior, aware of all sides, unattached to attainment, centered in one’s being." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 778) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"There are sixteen vinyasas to this asana, of which the 7th, 8th, 9th, and 10th are the states of the asana." [Note: In the Ashtanga system of Pattabhi Jois, both Warrior I and Warrior II are practiced within a single sixteen-vinyasa sequence]. 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1512) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Begin this asana in the same way as the first Surya Namaskara and continue through to the end of the 6th vinyasa... stand as in the 7th vinyasa of the second Surya Namaskara... doing rechaka [exhale], turn to the left, bend the left knee, keeping the arms raised over the head, with the palms together, the chest lifted up... this is the 8th vinyasa." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1513) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"In transitioning from Adho Mukha Svanasana to Virabhadrasana I... in traditional Ashtanga Vinyasa yoga, the left heel is turned in about halfway and rooted down before stepping the right foot forward." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 817) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Stand in Tadasana. Raise both arms above the head; stretch up and join the palms. Take a deep inhalation and with a jump spread the legs apart sideways 4 to 4½ feet. Exhale, turn to the right. Simultaneously turn the right foot 90 degrees to the right and the left foot slightly to the right. Flex the right knee till the right thigh is parallel to the floor and the right shin perpendicular to the floor... gaze at the joined palms." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 158-159) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Exhale and jump back to Tadasana." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 159) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Movement
(This continues from Utkatasana) hands on the floor, lift the body, jump back, Chaturanga, Urdhava mukha, adho mukha. Step the right foot forward between the hands, left foot 45, arms up, palms together. Repeat on the left side.

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- "The front leg involves eccentric quadriceps control at depth and hip stabilization, while the back leg utilizes hip extension with knee extension to prevent anterior pelvic tilt." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 42, 46) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The spine is in a slight backbending position, so it strengthens the extensors of the spine—mainly the erector spinae—and stretches the front of the upper body." *(The Manual of Yoga Anatomy, Sally Parkes, p. 345) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "In the front leg, the hip flexors and the hamstrings concentrically contract to move the knee into a flexed position, with the quadriceps lengthening eccentrically." *(The Manual of Yoga Anatomy, Sally Parkes, p. 347) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The gluteals extend the hip and tilt the pelvis down and under (retroversion); cues for engaging these muscles include squeezing the buttocks and tucking the tailbone." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long, p. 25) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Engagement of the serratus anterior is used to stabilize the shoulder blades as the arms reach overhead." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 29) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "The back foot turns inward 30 degrees and supinates, while the front foot turns out 90 degrees." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long, p. 122) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The spine is in extension with slight external rotation of the left hip and left lumbar region, moving primarily in the sagittal plane." *(The Manual of Yoga Anatomy, Sally Parkes, p. 348) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"To deepen the experience of Virabhadrasana I, emphasize the steady grounding of the feet, internal rotation of the back leg... pada bandha in both feet, mula bandha, and steady energetic lifting through the spine, through the heart center, and out through the fingertips." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 820) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Lift the front of your hips away from your front leg while lightly drawing your lower belly and sacrum toward each other." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 783) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"When in the states of Utkatasana and Virabhadrasana, there is no need to do rechaka and puraka more than five times. However, aspirants should not forget to do rechaka and puraka while performing the vinyasas." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1512) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- "Because of anterior pelvic tilt and lumbar lordosis, it is extremely difficult to correct the pelvis to a neutral position, often leading to 'crunching' in the lower back." *(Identifying Postural Imbalances, Vayu Jung Doohwa, p. 132) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "To avoid straining your right knee, ensure the kneecap does not project forward past the ankle and is turned slightly toward the little toe." *(B. K. S. Iyengar Yoga: The Path to Holistic Health, B. K. S. Iyengar, p. 98) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Practitioners with lower back injuries, neck injuries, or high blood pressure should exercise caution or modify the pose." *(The Manual of Yoga Anatomy, Sally Parkes, p. 346) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Lengthen before you deepen and root down to rise up." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 18) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "If the hips are inflexible, move the front foot 5–10 centimeters to the side to allow the pelvis and lower back to move more freely." *(The Manual of Yoga Anatomy, Sally Parkes, p. 346) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Maintain the lift of the back knee and adjust shoulder blades by pushing them in and then lifting them upward." *(B. K. S. Iyengar Yoga: The Path to Holistic Health, B. K. S. Iyengar, p. 97) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
