# Kukkutasana

"Kukkuta means a cock, which this posture resembles." 

![Kukkutasana](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Kukkutasana.png)


## Sanskrit Meaning
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 140) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"Kukkutasana has fourteen vinyasas, the 8th of which is the state of the asana." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1570) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Do Garbha Pindasana above until the 7th vinyasa... pressing the palms to the floor and doing puraka, lift up the Padmasana, and stand on the strength of the palms; this is the 8th vinyasa." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1570) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
Tip of the nose
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Movement
Legs in Padmasana, arms between the calves and the thighs, palms flat on the floor, lift the body up and off the mat with root lock.
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- **Shoulder Stabilizers:** "Serratus anterior and pectoralis minor protract the scapulae to lift the pelvis." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Arm Strength:** "Triceps brachii extend the elbows to support the lift." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Core engagement:** "Deep engagement of the transversus abdominis and pelvic floor (Mula Bandha) is essential to maintain the height of the lift." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "Wrist extension (supporting weight). Shoulder protraction and depression. Hip external rotation (Lotus)." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"When in the state of this asana... one should do uddiyana bandha and nauli. There is no mula bandha in this asana." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1571) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"In the state of this asana, one should do rechaka and puraka deeply while keeping the chest, waist, and back completely straight." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1571) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- **Wrist Compression:** "The vertical load on the wrists is significant. Failing to engage the fingertips can lead to joint strain." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 70) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Elbow Strain:** "Hyperextending the elbows under the load of the body can strain the ligaments of the inner elbow." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 72) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Press through the base of the index finger and thumb to stabilize the wrist joint." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 130) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "If the lift is not possible, focus on the downward pressure of the hands to build the necessary strength in the serratus anterior." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 132) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Teaching Notes
"In this position, revolve the stomach (nauli), lift the back and chest fully." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1570) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*
