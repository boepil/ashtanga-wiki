# Upavistha Konasana (Seated Wide Angle Pose)

![Upavistha-Konasana-A](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Upavistha-Konasana-A.png)
![Upavistha-Konasana-b](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Upavistha-Konasana-b.png)

## Sanskrit Meaning
"Upavistha means seated. Kona means an angle." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 206) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"Upavishta Konasana has fifteen vinyasas, the 8th and 9th of which are the states of the asana." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1402) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Jump the legs through the arms... spread the legs as wide as possible, sit... take hold of the sides of the feet, and lift the head and chest; this forms the 7th vinyasa." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1402) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

**Variation A:** "Doing rechaka, pull in the stomach, slowly place the head and chest on the floor... this is the 8th vinyasa." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1402) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

**Variation B:** "Come up to sit straight on the buttocks... hold the raised legs wide apart and straight... balance on the tailbone... this is the 9th vinyasa." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1402) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
- **A:** Tip of the nose or third eye
- **B:** Towards the sky (or third eye)
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Movement
**Variation A:** Sit with legs wide apart, hold the outside edges of the feet, bend forward, bring the chin and the chest to the floor.
**Variation B:** From Variation A lift straight legs, hold the outside edges of the feet, balance on the tailbone.
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- **Hamstrings:** "Intense lengthening of the medial hamstrings (semitendinosus and semimembranosus)." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Adductors:** "Gracilis and adductor magnus are stretched in the wide-legged position." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Quadriceps:** "Engagement is crucial to facilitate reciprocal inhibition of the hamstrings." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Spinal Extensors:** "Manage the descent and maintain length in the forward fold." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "Hip abduction (90°+). Hip flexion. Pelvic anterior tilt." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"While in the states of Upavishta Konasana, holding mula bandha and uddiyana bandha is very important." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1403) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Press the hands into the floor behind the hips [to enter]... increasingly flex the front foot, engaging the quadriceps." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 768-769) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- **Hamstring Tear:** "Forcing the chest to the floor without sufficient hamstring length can cause micro-tears at the ischial tuberosity (sit bone)." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 80) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Knee Instability:** "Allowing the knees to roll inward (internal rotation) can strain the medial collateral ligament." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 82) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Keep the toes and kneecaps pointing straight up (or slightly back) to ensure the rotation is coming from the hip joint, not the knee." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 140) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "If the back is rounding significantly, sit on a block or keep the hands behind you for support to maintain pelvic tilt." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 142) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
