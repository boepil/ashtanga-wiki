# Salamba Sarvangasana (Supported Shoulderstand)

![Salamba-Sarvangasana](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Salamba-Sarvangasana.png)

## Sanskrit Meaning
"Alamba means a prop, a support and sa together with or accompanied by. Salamba, therefore, means supported or propped up. Sarvanga (Sarva =all, whole, entire, complete; anga = limb or body) means the entire body or all the limbs." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 205–206) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"Sarvangasana has thirteen vinyasas, of which the 8th is its state." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1410) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Lie flat on the back... Raise the hips from the floor with an exhalation and rest the hands on them... raise the trunk up perpendicularly supported by the hands until the chest touches the chin." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 206–207) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"aṣṭau 8: inhale, lift the legs straight up. Breathe ten times [or more]." 
*(Ashtanga Yoga Anusthana, Sharath Jois, p. 77) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
Toes (or navel)
*(raw PDF)*

## Movement
Lift the legs up directly over the head, support the back with your hands, elbows close to the trunk. The goal is to stack the hips directly over the shoulders.
*(raw PDF)*

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- **Deep Core:** "Transversus abdominis and psoas work to maintain the vertical orientation of the legs and pelvis." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Back Extensors:** "Erector spinae contract to maintain the lift of the trunk." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Shoulder Stabilizers:** "Serratus anterior and rhomboids engage to maintain the platform of the shoulders." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "Cervical flexion (to maximum). Shoulder extension and abduction (at the foundation). Hip extension. Knee extension." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"In the 8th vinyasa, the chin should press into the chest properly [Jalandhara Bandha], the legs be held straight, and the big toes and tip of the nose should form a single line." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1410) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Engage mula bandha and gaze to the nose or belly." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 738) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- **Neck (C7) Overloading:** "Weight must rest on the shoulders and upper arms, not the neck. Overloading the C7 vertebra can lead to disc compression." *(raw PDF)*
- **Hyperflexion of the Cervical Spine:** "Turning the head side-to-side in this pose can cause acute ligament or joint injury." *(raw PDF)*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Use folded blankets under the shoulders to preserve the natural curve of the cervical spine." *(raw PDF)*
- "Walk hands down the back (towards shoulder blades) to lift the chest and move the weight off the neck." *(raw PDF)*

## Teaching Notes
"The 'firm chinlock' increases the blood supply to the thyroid and parathyroid glands situated in the neck." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 213) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Known as the 'Queen of Asanas' for its calming effect on the nervous system." 
*(raw PDF)*
