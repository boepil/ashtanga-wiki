# Utthita Parsvakonasana

"Parsva means side or flank. Kona is an angle. This is the extended lateral angle pose." 

![Utthita-Parsvakonasana-A](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Uthitha-Parsvakonasana-A.png)


## Sanskrit Meaning
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 177) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Utthita means extended, stretched." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 701) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"This asana has five vinyasas, of which the 2nd and 4th constitute the states of the asana." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1514) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Jump the legs open to the right, doing puraka [inhaling], and stand with the legs as much as five feet apart... turn the right foot out, bend the knee completely, place the right hand by the side of the right foot, stretch the left arm straight out over the ear, and gaze at the fingertips; this is the 2nd vinyasa." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1514) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Press through the feet to release." 
*(Teaching Yoga: Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 847) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
Finger tip of the left hand
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Movement
Feet 4ft apart, bend the right knee over the ankle, bring right hand to the floor outside the right foot, chest open, left arm extended in line with the ear, back foot firm on the mat. Repeat on the left side.
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- "The psoas is the agonist muscle that flexes the hip and tilts the pelvis forward (anteversion). When the psoas contracts, the brain signals its antagonist muscle, the gluteus maximus... to relax into the stretch." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long, p. 426) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "To allow for the deep flexion of the right knee... the hamstrings shorten and the quadriceps lengthen, while the adductors assist to fix the leg in position." *(The Manual of Yoga Anatomy, Sally Parkes, p. 365) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Strong engagement of the gluteus medius/minimus to stabilize the pelvis; serratus anterior for the extended arm." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 60) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "Hip flexion and external rotation (front leg), shoulder abduction and rotation (top arm)." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 60) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The back foot turns in 30 degrees and supinates. The front foot turns out 90 degrees. The back knee extends. The back hip extends and externally rotates. The trunk laterally flexes and rotates up." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long, p. 458) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"Rooting evenly through your feet while lifting your inner arches, spiral your inner thighs back while drawing your sitting bones toward each other and lifting steadily from the front floor of your pelvis with mula bandha." 
*(Teaching Yoga: Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 813) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"In the 2nd and 4th vinyasas, which are the states of this asana, the body should be held tightly, and rechaka [exhalation] and puraka [inhalation] done slowly and as much as possible." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1515) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- "If you have high blood pressure, avoid this asana. If you have cervical spondylosis, do not turn your neck or look up." *(B. K. S. Iyengar Yoga: The Path to Holistic Health, B. K. S. Iyengar, p. 121) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Ensure the knee does not track past the ankle in poses like Parsvakonasana." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 65) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "For those predisposed to femoral acetabular impingement syndrome (FAIS), adding internal rotation before commencing [the fold] is not a good idea." *(Your Body, Your Yoga, Bernie Clark, p. 565) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Rest on the back of the right heel and do not allow dead weight to fall on your right thigh or palm." *(B. K. S. Iyengar Yoga: The Path to Holistic Health, B. K. S. Iyengar, p. 122) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Elongate the spine to minimise pressure in the lower back, and keep the arms level, as this will help to strengthen the shoulders." *(The Manual of Yoga Anatomy, Sally Parkes, p. 364) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "For lower back injuries and high blood pressure, shorten the stance and place the right forearm on the right thigh." *(The Manual of Yoga Anatomy, Sally Parkes, p. 364) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
