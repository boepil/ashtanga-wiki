# Garbha Pindasana (Embryo in the Womb Pose)

![Garbha-Pindasana](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Garbha-Pindasana.png)

## Sanskrit Meaning
"Garbha Pinda means an embryo in the womb (garbha = womb; pinda = embryo). This variation of Padmasana... resembles that of a human foetus in the womb." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 141) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"There are fourteen vinyasas in Garbha Pindasana, the 8th of which is its state." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1569) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Do Padmasana... insert both arms past the elbow through the openings between the thigh and calf, hold the ears with the hands, sitting purely on the buttocks, straighten the chest and spinal column, press both heels on either side of the navel." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1569) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"In the 8th vinyasa, roll on the spine in a clockwise circle for nine rotations." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1569) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
Tip of the nose
*(raw PDF)*

## Movement
Legs in Padmasana, slide the hands between the calves and the thighs, bend the arms, place palms over cheeks. Rock and roll in a full circle on around 5-9 times (clockwise).
*(raw PDF)*

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- **Hip External Rotators:** "Intense engagement to maintain the Lotus bind under the load of the arms." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Core Stabilizers:** "Rectus abdominis and obliques work to maintain the rounded spine during the rolling action." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Shoulder Stabilizers:** "Serratus anterior and rotator cuff protect the shoulder joint as the arms are threaded through the legs." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "Extreme hip external rotation and flexion (Lotus). Spinal flexion (during the roll). Shoulder internal rotation and protraction." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"While in the state of this asana, the lower abdomen should be drawn in and held tightly." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1536) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"In this posture the abdominal organs are contracted completely and the blood is made to circulate well round the organs." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 141) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- **Knee Torque:** "The most common injury in Garbha Pindasana occurs during the threading of the arms, where the knees are forced apart. If the hips are tight, the torque transfers directly to the medial meniscus." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 65) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Spinal Impact:** "Rolling on a hard floor or with a flat back can cause bruising or strain to the spinous processes." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 68) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Rule: Use water or oil on the arms to help them slide through if the bind is tight, but never force the legs apart." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 125) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Ensure the spine is perfectly rounded (C-curve) before beginning the rolling sequence to protect the vertebrae." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 127) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Teaching Notes
"Garbha Pindasana dissolves the fat of the lower abdomen, purifies the manipura, or third, chakra, and wards off diseases of the liver and spleen." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1570) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*
