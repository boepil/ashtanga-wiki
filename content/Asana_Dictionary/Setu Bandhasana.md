# Setu Bandhasana (Bridge Pose)

![Setu-Bandhasana](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Setu-Bandhasana.png)

## Sanskrit Meaning
"Setu means a bridge. Setu bandha means the formation or construction of a bridge. In this posture, the whole body forms an arch and is supported at one end by the crown of the head and at the other by the feet." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 255) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Setu Bandhasana: Bound Bridge Pose, or Charlie Chaplin Pose." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 736) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"Setu Bandhasana has fifteen vinyasas, of which the 9th is the state." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1396) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Lie down... this is the 7th vinyasa. Next, do puraka [inhale]. Then, doing rechaka [exhale], bend the knees a little, join the heels together, placing the little toes firmly on the floor, bend the head back, place the top of the head on the floor, lift the chest a little... then, crossing the arms over the chest and doing puraka, lift up the waist and back, and stand straightly on the head and feet alone." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1396) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
Tip of the nose or third eye
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Movement
Heels together, toes pointed outwards, lift the chest off the floor, roll head to bring crown of head to floor, with balance of the head and the feet lift hip upwards. Arms are crossed over the chest.
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- **Spinal Extensors:** "Longissimus and iliocostalis engage powerfully to maintain the arch." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Gluteus Maximus:** "Contracts to lift the pelvis and stabilize the hips." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Quadriceps:** "Rectus femoris and vastus muscles extend the knees to support the bridge." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Neck Extensors:** "Semispinalis capitis and splenius capitis support the weight on the crown of the head." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "Spinal extension (intense). Hip extension. Knee extension. Cervical extension (crown of head on floor)." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"This pose is specifically noted for purifying the muladhara chakra (root chakra) and increasing the jathara agni (digestive fire)." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1397) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"During the transition out of the pose (11th vinyasa), pull in the stomach completely [Uddiyana Bandha]." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1408) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- **Cervical Spine Impact:** "Placing too much weight on the head without sufficient leg and back strength can compress the cervical vertebrae." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 90) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Knee Strain:** "If the heels are not joined properly, the internal rotation of the knees can strain the ligaments." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 92) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Root down through the feet and elbows while arching the spine, emphasizing pelvic neutrality." *(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 714) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*
- "Rule: The weight should be distributed between the head and the feet; if the neck feels compressed, lower the hips and re-engage the legs." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 160) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Teaching Notes
"The arching movement ensures the pineal, pituitary, thyroid, and adrenal glands are 'bathed in blood' and function properly." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 257) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*
