# Parivrtta Parsvakonasana (Revolved Side Angle Pose)

![Utthita-Parsvakonasana-b](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Uthitha-Parsvakonasana-b.png)

## Sanskrit Meaning
"Parivrtta means revolved, turned round or back. Parsva means side or flank. Kona is an angle. This is the revolving lateral angle posture." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 170) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"Parivrtta Parsvakonāsana Eight*" 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 170) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Utthitaparsvakonasana a, b... (It has 5 vinyasas)." [Note: In the Krishnamacharya/Ashtanga tradition, "b" refers to the revolved version]. 
*(Yoga Makaranda, T. Krishnamacharya, p. 97) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Stand in Tadasana. Take a deep inhalation, and with a jump spread the legs apart sideways 4 to 4½ feet... Bend the right leg at the knee until the thigh and the calf form a right angle and the right thigh is parallel to the floor." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 170) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Exhale, and rotate the trunk and the left leg so as to bring the left arm over the right knee. Rest the left armpit on the outer side of the right knee, and place the left palm on the floor by the outer side of the right foot. Give a good twist to the spine (to the right), turn the trunk and bring the right arm over the right ear and gaze up at the outstretched right arm." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 171) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Inhale, and lift the left palm from the floor. Raise the trunk and come back to position 2, by straightening the right leg and raising the arms. ... Exhale and jump back to Tadasana." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 172) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
Finger tip of the right hand
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Movement
Feet 4ft apart, bend the right knee over the ankle, revolve around, bring left hand to the floor outside the right foot, right arm extended in line with the ear. Repeat on the left side.
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- "The entire spine, but particularly the thoracic spine, deeply rotates due to a strong concentric contraction of the latissimus dorsi and internal and external obliques." *(The Manual of Yoga Anatomy, Sally Parkes, p. 400) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Contracting the adductor magnus assists the gluteus maximus, extending and externally rotating the back leg in Parivrtta Parsvakonasana." *(Key Muscles of Yoga, Ray Long, p. 260) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The triceps extend the arms from the elbows, while the deltoids and serratus anterior work to extend the left arm over the head." *(The Manual of Yoga Anatomy, Sally Parkes, p. 400) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "The back foot rotates inward 90 degrees. The front foot turns out 90 degrees. The front hip and knee flex to 90 degrees. The back hip extends and externally rotates. The trunk laterally flexes and rotates." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long, p. 515) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The spine is in extension, moving in a sagittal plane from a frontal axis. The pelvis is in an anterior tilt." *(The Manual of Yoga Anatomy, Sally Parkes, p. 401) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"In an effort to find pelvic neutrality... guide students to create a feeling of their pubic bone leading the way in pulling the hips up and back." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 804) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"With an exhale use light uddiyana bandha, floating to Chaturanga." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 837) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- "Practitioners with injuries to the vertebral discs should not practise this asana." *(The Manual of Yoga Anatomy, Sally Parkes, p. 399) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "This [external rotation of the shoulder] can be too intense for practitioners with injuries and should be practised with caution." *(The Manual of Yoga Anatomy, Sally Parkes, p. 398) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Ensure the knee does not track past the ankle in poses like Parsvakonasana." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 65) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Ensure the right arm is parallel to the ground and try to create as much length through the spine as possible before rotating the upper body." *(The Manual of Yoga Anatomy, Sally Parkes, p. 399) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Anchor the back foot into the ground [to] create a stretch of the entire upper side of the body." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long, p. 495) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Use a block [under the hand]... to decrease the range of motion." *(The Manual of Yoga Anatomy, Sally Parkes, p. 399) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
