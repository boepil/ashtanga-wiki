# Navasana (Boat Pose)

![Navasana](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Navasana.png)

## Sanskrit Meaning
"Paripurna means entire or complete. The posture here resembles that of a boat with oars, hence the name." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 207) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Navasana... keep our bodies like a boat." 
*(Krishnamacharya's Yogasanagalu, T. Krishnamacharya, p. 121) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Navasana: Boat Pose." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 920) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"There are thirteen vinyasas in Navasana and the 7th one is the state of the asana." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1501) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Jump the legs in between the arms, doing puraka [inhale] and using only the strength of the arms... sit squarely on the buttocks, raise and straighten the legs... hold the arms out straight on either side of the knees." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1501) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"From Dandasana, slide one heel back toward the hip... leverage the anterior rotation of the pelvis while sitting taller... lift the feet off the floor, eventually straightening the legs and bringing the toes level with the eyes without slumping in the spine." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 781) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
Toes
*(raw PDF)*

## Movement
Sit in, extend legs up, both legs straight, trunk up, angle between trunk & legs 90 around, arms parallel to the floor, engage abdominal muscle. Repeat 5 times (lifting up between sets).
*(raw PDF)*

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- "Abdominal Group: The rectus and transversus abdominis work powerfully as fixators to support the position of the spine." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Hip Flexors: The legs are fully extended and raised from the ground by a strong contraction of the iliopsoas, quadriceps, and hip flexors." *(B. K. S. Iyengar Yoga, B. K. S. Iyengar) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Spinal Stabilizers: The erector spinae and latissimus dorsi work powerfully as fixators." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "Joint action involves deep hip flexion. The spine is in a slightly extended position and angled backwards, so that the spine and legs make a ‘V’ shape." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The shoulders flex to approximately 90 degrees, arms parallel to the floor." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"While coming into the state of this asana, never do kumbhaka, that is, never hold your breath." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1501) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"One feels the grip on the muscles of the abdomen and the lower back." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 210) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Refer to pada bandha and mula bandha as key energetic actions for awakening this energetic awareness." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 780) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- "Sacral Pressure: Many students... are resting on the top of their sacrum, not on the ischial tuberosities (the sitting bones). This puts a lot of stress into the fully flexed lower spine." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The load on the lumbar spine is significant if compensation through lumbar flexion occurs." *(Science of Yoga, Ann Swanson, p. 301) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Maintain a Long Spine: Keep the back long and straight; do not collapse." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Engage the Abdominal Air Bag: Contract the rectus abdominis to draw the ribcage down... this increases intra-abdominal pressure and creates an 'air bag' effect to stabilize the spine." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Modify for Tightness: Tightness in the hip extensors (hamstrings) can stop hip flexion. Bending the knees reduces this resistance." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Teaching Notes
"Then cross the legs without touching the floor, and use the strength of the arms and hands to lift the body up off the floor [Lolasana]; this is the 8th vinyasa. Then, doing rechaka, come back to the 7th vinyasa. In this way, repeat the asana three to six times." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1501) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*
