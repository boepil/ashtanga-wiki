# Dhanurasana & Parsva Dhanurasana

![Dhanurasana](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Dhanurasana.png)

## Sanskrit Meaning
"Dhanu means a bow. Dhanurasana is the Bow Pose, where the body resembles a bow with the arms as the string." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 175) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Parsva means side. In Parsva Dhanurasana, the practitioner rolls onto the side while maintaining the bow shape." 
*(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"Dhanurasana has three vinyasas. The 2nd is the state of the asana." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1422) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Lie full length on the floor on the stomach... Exhale, bend the knees and hold the ankles with the hands. Exhale, lift the knees and chest off the floor, pulling the legs with the arms so the body is arched like a bow." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 175) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"From Dhanurasana, exhale and roll the entire body onto the right side. Stay for 5 breaths, then roll back to the center and roll onto the left side." 
*(Ashtanga Yoga Anusthana, Sharath Jois, p. 56) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
Nose (nasagre)

## Movement
- **Dhanurasana:** Lying on stomach, holding ankles, body arched.
- **Parsva Dhanurasana:** Rolling onto the side while maintaining the grip on the ankles.
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- **Spinal Extensors:** "Longissimus and iliocostalis are active to lift the torso." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Quadriceps:** "Lengthened intensely as the legs are pulled back." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Pectorals:** "Stretched as the shoulders are pulled back by the weight of the legs." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "Spinal extension. Hip extension. Shoulder extension and retraction. Knee flexion." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"Uddiyana Bandha is essential during the roll in Parsva Dhanurasana to stabilize the core and prevent the spine from collapsing." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 798) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- **Lumbar Pinched Nerve:** "Exaggerated lordosis without abdominal support can pinch the lower back." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 195) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Sacroiliac (SI) Joint Strain:** "Uneven pulling in Parsva Dhanurasana can strain the SI joint." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 198) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Keep the big toes touching to prevent the knees from splaying too wide, which protects the lower back." <div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>
- "Rule: Use the strength of the legs pushing into the hands to create the lift, rather than just pulling with the arms." <div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Teaching Notes
"This asana brings back flexibility to the spine and tones the abdominal organs. It is more intense than Salabhasana because the arms provide leverage." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 176) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*
