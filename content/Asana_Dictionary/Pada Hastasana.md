# Pada Hastasana (Hands to Feet Pose)

![Pada-Hastasana-a](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Pada-Hastasana-a.png)
![Pada-Hastasana-b](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Pada-Hastasana-b.png)

## Sanskrit Meaning
"Pada means the foot. Hasta means the hand. This posture is done by bending forward and standing on one's hands." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 91) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"Padahastasana has three vinyasas. The 2nd vinyasa is the state of the asana." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1576) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Stand with the legs as much as half a foot apart... and doing puraka and then rechaka, place palms under the feet and, doing puraka, lift the head up and stay in position; this is the 1st vinyasa." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1576) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Then, doing rechaka, place the head in the region between the knees, keeping knees straight, and hold position, doing rechaka and puraka fully as much as possible; this is the 2nd vinyasa." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1576) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Then, taking the breath in, lift only the head; this is the 3rd vinyasa. Then Samasthiti." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1576) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
third eye or tip of the nose (A) / tip of the nose (B)
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Movement
A: feet hip distance apart, spine straight, chest open, slide hand under feet.
B: slide hands under feet (the rest as in Padangusthasana-B).
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- "The hip flexors, mainly the iliopsoas and rectus femoris, shorten via a concentric contraction... The abdominal muscles isometrically contract to support the lumbar region, while the trapezius muscles contract concentrically, moving the scapulae away from the head." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The biceps contract, flexing the elbows to draw the upper body forward... The force produced by this action ultimately affects the position of the pelvis, tilting it forward." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The quadriceps contract in this forward bend, lifting the patella, straightening the knee, and stretching their antagonists (the hamstrings)." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "The wrist is flexed (also called palmar flexion), such as when we step on the hands in Padahastasana." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The spine is in forwards flexion, in a sagittal plane. The pelvis is in an anterior tilt." *(The Manual of Yoga Anatomy, Sally Parkes) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Deep hip flexion, knee extension." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"In the state of this asana, the lower abdomen should be drawn in tightly, and rechaka and puraka done as much as possible." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1576) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"With pada bandha in both feet... offer the same cues [as Padangusthasana] except with placement of the palms under the feet, toes to the wrists and fingertips to the heels." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 880) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Radiate down through the legs to ground the feet firmly and activate the legs; internally rotate the femurs, pitch the pubic bone back and up, and stretch the sternum toward the floor." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 881) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- "The load on the lumbar spine in a standing Forward Fold is significant. The lower back is particularly vulnerable during the transition in and out of the pose." *(Science of Yoga, Ann Swanson) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "In deep hip flexion postures, such as Standing Forward Bend, the proximal hamstring tendon stretches over the insertional tuberosity, causing transverse compression forces on the tendon." *(Yoga of the Subtle Body, Tias Little) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Most [disc] herniations occur posterior-laterally due to spinal flexion; if you currently have a disk issue, move slowly or avoid flexion by not going into the pose as deeply." *(Your Body, Your Yoga, Bernie Clark) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "If the lower back rounds, bend the knees! This creates slack in the hamstrings, allowing the pelvis to tilt forward and the spine to neutralize." *(Your Body, Your Yoga, Bernie Clark) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "To lengthen from the lumbar spine, try to tilt the pelvis forwards so the coccyx draws upwards." *(Yoga of the Subtle Body, Tias Little) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Engage the Quadriceps (front of thighs) to signal the Hamstrings (back of thighs) to relax [through reciprocal inhibition]." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Caution with lumbar disc herniations: Avoid rounding; keep spine straight." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
