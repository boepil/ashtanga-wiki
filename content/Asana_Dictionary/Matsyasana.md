# Matsyasana (Fish Pose)

![Matsyasana](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Matsyasana.png)

## Sanskrit Meaning
"Matsya means a fish. In this variation, the body is arched like a fish in the water." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 116) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"Matsyasana has nine vinyasas. The 4th is the state of the asana." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1409) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Lie flat on the back... bend the knees and cross the legs in Padmasana. Exhale, arch the back, lifting the chest, and rest the crown of the head on the floor. Hold the big toes with the hands." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 116) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"catvāri 4: inhale, arch the back and place the top of the head on the floor. Hold the toes. Breathe eight times." 
*(Ashtanga Yoga Anusthana, Sharath Jois, p. 50) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
Third eye
*(raw PDF)*

## Movement
Legs in Padmasana, chest lifted, the crown of the head on the floor, hold the big toes.
*(raw PDF)*

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- **Spinal Extensors:** "Erector spinae muscles are active to lift the thoracic spine and maintain the arch." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Deep Neck Flexors:** "Longus colli and longus capitis are engaged to stabilize the neck while in extension." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Pectorals:** "Stretched as the chest expands." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "Thoracic extension. Cervical extension. Hip external rotation (in lotus). Knee flexion." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"While in this state, the stomach should be drawn in and held [Uddiyana Bandha], and the chest expanded fully." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1409) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- **Cervical Compression:** "Too much weight on the head without enough lift from the chest can compress the cervical discs." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 120) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Knee Strain:** "If Padmasana is forced, the internal rotation required can strain the knee ligaments." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 122) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Ensure the lift comes from the thoracic spine and the shoulder blades moving together, rather than just tilting the head back." *(raw PDF)*
- "Rule: Keep the weight distributed between the hips and the crown of the head, with minimal pressure on the neck." *(raw PDF)*

## Teaching Notes
"This asana is the counter-pose to the deep flexion of the shoulderstand sequence, helping to reset the cervical spine and expand the lungs." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 789) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*
