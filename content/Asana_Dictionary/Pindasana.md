# Pindasana

"Piṇḍa means embryo or foetus.... the bent crossed legs are brought down until they rest on the head. The posture resembles that of an embryo in the womb, hence the name." 

![Pindasana](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Pindasana.png)


## Sanskrit Meaning
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 270) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"Pindasana has fourteen vinyasas. The 9th is the state of the asana." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1407) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"From Urdhva Padmasana in Sarvangasana, exhale, bend and lower the crossed legs from the hips towards the head. Rest the legs over the head. Release the hands from the back, and clasp the legs." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 270) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"nava 9: inhale, wrap the arms around padmāsana and lock the hands. Breathe eight times." 
*(Ashtanga Yoga Anusthana, Sharath Jois, p. 49) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
Tip of the nose
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Movement
Legs in Padmasana, bring the knees to the chest, wrap the arms around, clasp the hands together, avoid pressure on neck.
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- **Abdominals:** "Deep core muscles work to maintain the tight tuck of the embryo position." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Paraspinals:** "Intensely stretched along the entire length of the spine, especially the thoracic region." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Adductors:** "Engage to keep the lotus tight against the torso." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "Maximum spinal flexion. Cervical flexion. Hip flexion and external rotation. Knee flexion." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"While in the state of this asana, the lower abdomen should be drawn in and held tightly [Uddiyana Bandha]." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1391) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"envelop the thighs with the arms, embrace the Padmasana, hold the wrists tightly, balance the body on the shoulders only." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1407) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- **Neck Compression:** "Dropping the weight of the legs onto the head or neck can cause severe cervical impingement." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 115) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Chest Restriction:** "The intense compression of the torso can restrict breathing if the student is not calm." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 118) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Clasp the hands or wrists around the legs to maintain the balance on the shoulders, not the neck." <div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>
- "Rule: Focus on keeping the weight centered over the shoulder blades throughout the hold." <div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Teaching Notes
"This posture massages the abdominal organs and helps to quiet the mind through the physical sensation of containment." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 788) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*
