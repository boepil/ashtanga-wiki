# Savasana

"Sava means a corpse. In this posture, one mimics the stillness of a corpse." 

![Savasana](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Savasana.png)


## Sanskrit Meaning
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 422) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"Savasana has no vinyasas. It is the final rest at the end of every practice." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1415) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Lie flat on the back... keep the arms a little away from the thighs and the palms facing upwards. Close the eyes. Stay as still as possible." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 422) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
Inwards (closed eyes)
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Movement
Relax the whole body, witness the movement of your breath. Stay for 10 to 15 minutes.
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Action:**
- **Conscious Relaxation:** "The goal is to inhibit all voluntary muscle activity and shift the nervous system into the parasympathetic (rest and digest) state." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Physical Alignment:**
- "Legs slightly apart, feet falling open. Arms away from the body, palms up. Spine neutral. Head centered." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"In Savasana, all bandhas are released. The body is completely surrendered." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 793) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- **Lower Back Discomfort:** "If the lower back feels tight, placing a bolster under the knees can help flatten the lumbar spine." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 160) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Falling Asleep:** "The goal is 'sleep with awareness.' If you fall asleep, the conscious benefits of the relaxation are reduced." <div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Ensure the body is warm (use a blanket if needed) to prevent muscle tension during the rest." <div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>
- "Rule: Let go of all effort, including the control of the breath." <div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Teaching Notes
"This is the most difficult asana to master because it requires the complete stillness of both the body and the mind." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 424) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*
