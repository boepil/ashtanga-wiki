# Pada Angusthasana (Big Toe Pose)

![Pada-Angusthasana](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Pada-Angusthasana.png)
![Pada-Angusthasana-b](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Pada-Angusthasana-b.png)

## Sanskrit Meaning
"Pada means the foot. Angustha is the big toe. This posture is done by standing and catching the big toes." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 210-211) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"Padangushtasana has three vinyasas, of which the 2nd is the state of the asana." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1538) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"First, stand up straight, inhaling through the nostrils deeply, jump the legs apart as much as half a foot, slowly let the breath out, reach down and take hold of the big toes, lift the head and chest up completely without bending the knees, and stay in position while inhaling; this is the 1st vinyasa." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1538) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Then, letting the breath out, take in the lower abdomen, place the head in the region between the two knees, straighten the knees, and stay in position, doing puraka and rechaka as much as possible; this is the 2nd vinyasa." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1538) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Next, inhaling, slowly lift the head, remaining in position with the fingers holding the big toes; this is the 3rd vinyasa. Then exhale and return to Samasthiti." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1538) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
third eye or tip of the nose (A) / tip of the nose (B)
*(raw PDF)*

"padāgre dṛṣṭi tip of the big toe." 
*(Ashtanga Yoga Anusthana, R. Sharath Jois, p. 64) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Movement
A: feet hip distance apart, lets & spine straight, chest open, hold big toes.
B: feet hip distance apart, bend forward, hold big toes, crown of the head towards the floor
*(raw PDF)*

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- "Quadriceps: The quadriceps are active to protect the knees and facilitate the fold while the hamstrings are lengthening." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Iliopsoas and Hip Flexors: The psoas major, iliacus, and pectineus work in combination to tilt the pelvis forward (anteversion), which draws the sitting bones backward and upward to augment the hamstring stretch." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Biceps Brachii: The biceps contract to flex the elbows, providing the force necessary to draw the upper body forward and deeper into the fold." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "Hip Flexion: The primary action is deep hip flexion, where the pelvis hinges forward over the femur." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Spinal Flexion: The spine moves into forward flexion, particularly in the 'B' phase of the posture where the crown of the head reaches toward the floor." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"While in the state of this asana, the lower abdomen should be drawn in and held tightly, and rechaka and puraka should be done slowly and as much as possible." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1538) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Internally rotate the femurs, pitch the pubic bone back and up, and stretch the sternum toward the floor." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 849) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- "Lumbar Spine Load: The load on the lumbar spine in a standing forward fold is significant, making the lower back particularly vulnerable during the transitions in and out of the pose." *(Science of Yoga, Ann Swanson) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Hamstring Tendon Compression: In deep hip flexion, the proximal hamstring tendon stretches over the ischial tuberosity... which causes potentially aggravating transverse compression forces." *(Yoga of the Subtle Body, Tias Little) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Disc Herniation: Most intervertebral disk herniations occur posterior-laterally due to spinal flexion; students with existing disk issues must avoid deep rounding." *(Your Body, Your Yoga, Bernie Clark) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Hinge from the Hips, Not the Waist: Focusing on anterior pelvic tilt ensures that the fold begins at the hip joints rather than through excessive rounding of the lumbar spine." *(Your Body, Your Yoga, Bernie Clark) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Engage Your Quads: Firmly engaging the front of the thighs protects the knee joint and utilizes reciprocal inhibition to safely lengthen the hamstrings." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Bend the Knees if the Back Rounds: If the hamstrings are tight, bending the knees creates slack that allows the pelvis to tilt forward, protecting the intervertebral disks." *(Your Body, Your Yoga, Bernie Clark) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Lengthen Before You Deepen: This cue encourages axial extension, creating space between the vertebrae before entering the flexed position to minimize compressive forces." *(Science of Yoga, Ann Swanson) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
