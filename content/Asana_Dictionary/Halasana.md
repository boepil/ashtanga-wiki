# Halasana (Plow Pose)

![halasana](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/halasana.png)

## Sanskrit Meaning
"Hala means a plough, the shape of which this posture resembles, hence the name." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 253) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"Halasana has thirteen vinyasas, the 8th of which is the state of the asana." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1404) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Do Salamba Sarvangasana I with a firm chinlock.... Release the chinlock, lower the trunk slightly, moving the arms and legs over the head and resting the toes on the floor.... Tighten the knees by pulling up the hamstring muscles at the back of the thighs and raise the trunk." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 253) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"aṣṭau 8: exhale, lower the legs over the head and touch the toes to the floor. Breathe eight times." 
*(Ashtanga Yoga Anusthana, Sharath Jois, p. 48) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
Tip of the nose
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Movement
Bring the legs over the head to the floor, feet together and pointed, arms on the floor, fingers interlaced.
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- **Spinal Extensors:** "Engaged to lengthen the back body while the spine is in deep flexion." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Hamstrings:** "Actively stretched while the quadriceps engage to keep the knees straight." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Shoulder Stabilizers:** "The rhomboids and trapezius work to pull the shoulder blades together, protecting the neck." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "Cervical flexion (maximum). Spinal flexion. Hip flexion. Knee extension. Shoulder extension." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
Practice begins with a "firm chinlock" (Jalandhara Bandha) established in Sarvangasana. 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 253) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Aspirants should note that rechaka and puraka both occur in the 8th vinyasa." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1404) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- **Cervical Strain:** "Weight must remain on the shoulders. If the toes do not touch the floor comfortably, the weight can shift onto the cervical vertebrae, causing injury." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 100) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Lumbar Tension:** "Collapsing the back instead of lifting the sit bones can cause excessive pressure in the lumbar spine." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 102) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Interlace the fingers and press the arms into the floor to lift the trunk and create space for the neck." <div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>
- "Rule: Keep the gaze fixed on the nose and never turn the head side-to-side while in the pose." <div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Teaching Notes
"This asana rejuvenates the abdominal organs and helps relieve backaches by stretching the entire posterior chain." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 255) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*
