# Urdhva Padmasana (Upward Lotus Pose)

![Urdhva-Padmasana](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Urdhva-Padmasana.png)

## Sanskrit Meaning
"Ūrdhva means above or high. Padmāsana is the lotus pose. In this variation, Padmasana is done in the shoulderstand." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 266) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"Urdhva Padmasana has fourteen vinyasas, of which the 9th vinyasa is its state." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1406) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"From Salamba Sarvangasana, bend the legs at the knees and cross them. First place the right foot over the left thigh, and then the left foot over the right thigh. Stretch the crossed legs vertically up, move the knees closer to each other and the legs as far back as possible from the pelvic region." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 266–267) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"nava 9: inhale, lift the legs up and take padmāsana. Place the hands on the knees. Breathe eight times." 
*(Ashtanga Yoga Anusthana, Sharath Jois, p. 49) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
Tip of the nose
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Movement
From shoulderstand bring the legs into Padmasana, hands support the back, push the knees with the hands, straighten the arms & spine.
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- **Hip Rotators:** "Deep external rotators (obturators, piriformis) are engaged to facilitate the lotus position." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Serratus Anterior:** "Engages to stabilize the scapulae and provide a firm base for the shoulders." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Abdominals:** "Rectus abdominis and obliques work to stabilize the inverted lotus." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "Cervical flexion. Shoulder extension and abduction. Deep hip external rotation. Knee flexion. Ankle inversion (in lotus)." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"While in this state, tighten the anus [Mula Bandha], draw in the stomach fully [Uddiyana Bandha], hold the knees with the hands, straighten the arms, and slowly do puraka and rechaka as much as possible." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1406) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- **Knee Strain:** "Forcing the lotus position while inverted can cause lateral meniscus compression if the hips are not sufficiently open." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 110) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Loss of Balance:** "Leaning too far back without core engagement can cause the student to roll out of the pose onto the neck." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 112) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Ensure the weight is firmly on the shoulders before releasing the hands from the back to the knees." <div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>
- "Rule: If lotus is not accessible, maintain the shoulderstand or a simple cross-legged position to avoid knee injury." <div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Teaching Notes
"This asana improves balance and concentration while providing the benefits of an inversion coupled with hip opening." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 788) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*
