# Purvottanasana (Intense East Stretch Pose)

![purvottanasana](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/purvottanasana.png)

## Sanskrit Meaning
"Purva literally means the East. It means the front of the whole body from the forehead to the toes. Uttana means an intense stretch. In this posture, the whole front of the body is stretched intensely." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 291) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"pursvo. east; the front of the body... pursvottana. the intense stretch of the front side of the body" 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 898) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"Purvottanasana represents Paschimattanasana’s opposite. It has fifteen vinyasas, of which the 8th is the state of the asana." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1498) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"To begin, follow the method for Paschimattanasana until the 7th vinyasa. Next, place the hands on the floor at a distance of twelve inches behind the waist, lift the legs and torso entirely off the floor doing puraka [inhale], press the soles of the feet firmly on the floor, lower the head back, making the body firm, and hold, while slowly performing rechaka and puraka; this is the 8th vinyasa." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1498) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"From Paschimattanasana position, inhale and lift both hands straight up and while exhaling deeply take the shoulders slowly behind the back and place the palm of the hands on the floor about 1 foot distance from the hips with the fingers facing forward. ... push the chest forward and do a deep puraka kumbhaka. Pressing the heels and the palms tightly against the ground, lift the entire body in a straight line and drop the neck backwards." 
*(Yogasanagalu, T. Krishnamacharya, p. 190) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Then, doing rechaka slowly, return to the state of the 7th vinyasa and sit; this is the 9th vinyasa." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1498) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
Third eye or tip of nose
*(raw PDF)*

## Movement
Hands behind hips (20 to 30 cm) fingers pointing towards the feet, lift the hips up, weight on the hands and feet, feet together, bring the toes to the floor.
*(raw PDF)*

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- "Erector Spinae & Posterior Chain: The erector spinae muscles are the prime movers in the back bend Purvottanasana. Combine contraction of the erector spinae with the synergists of this pose, including the quadriceps, gluteus maximus, and triceps." *(Key Muscles of Yoga, Ray Long, p. 132) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The gluteus maximus contracts in this Asana. Its external rotation component is counteracted by contraction of the gluteus medius (anterior fibers), tensor fascia lata, and adductor group. (Accentuate this by pressing down the ball of the foot.)" *(Key Muscles of Yoga, Ray Long, p. 118) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The posterior section of the deltoids contracts, extending the arms in Purvottanasana and stretching the anterior deltoids, biceps brachii, and pectoralis major muscles." *(Key Muscles of Yoga, Ray Long, p. 139) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Contract the quadriceps and tensor fascia lata to extend the knees. Because the soles of the feet are planted on the mat and cannot move, the force of this contraction lifts the pelvis." *(Yoga Mat Companion 3, Ray Long, p. 104-105) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "The shoulders extend and externally rotate. The elbows extend. The forearms pronate. The trunk and cervical spine extend. The hips extend, internally rotate, and adduct. The knees extend. The ankles plantar flex." *(Yoga Mat Companion 3, Ray Long, p. 103) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"Try to press the balls of the feet firmly down, internally rotate the thighs, press the tailbone toward the heels, and expand across the chest by pressing the tips of the shoulder blades up into the chest." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 831) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"While in the state, do puraka and rechaka slowly and deeply, as much as possible." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1496) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Similar to the second step in dandasana, push the chest forward and do a deep puraka kumbhaka." 
*(Yogasanagalu, T. Krishnamacharya, p. 190) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- "Upward Plank Pose (Purvottanasana) with the hands pointing forward helps to maximize internal rotation at the shoulder... However, this position may be too stressful for many students’ wrists." *(Your Upper Body, Your Yoga, Bernie Clark, p. 604) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Tightness in the rectus abdominis limits the depth of back bends such as Urdhva Danurasana and Purvottanasana. (Overcoming this tightness without core engagement can lead to lower back crunching.)" *(Key Muscles of Yoga, Ray Long, p. 129) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Gently engage the abdominals during this backbend to produce the abdominal 'air bag' effect, protecting the lumbar spine from hyperextension." *(Yoga Mat Companion 3, Ray Long, p. 106) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "To take pressure off the wrist may require the hands pointing backwards... Another option, to try to get the best of both worlds, is to place a wedge or folded towel under the heel of the hand." *(Your Upper Body, Your Yoga, Bernie Clark, p. 604) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Roll the pelvis back and down. The force that tilts the pelvis also helps to lift the back." *(Yoga Mat Companion 3, Ray Long, p. 108) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Engaging the tensor fascia lata and gluteus medius... helps to maintain the kneecaps facing upward, the desired position in the pose." *(Yoga Mat Companion 3, Ray Long, p. 105) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
