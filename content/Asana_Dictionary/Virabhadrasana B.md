# Virabhadrasana B

"Asanas are named after legendary heroes like Vīrabhadra... When Śiva heard [of the death of Satī] he was gravely provoked, tore a hair from his matted locks and threw it to the ground. A powerful hero named Vīrabhadra rose up... This asana is dedicated to the powerful hero created by Śiva from his matted hair." 

![Virabhadrasana-b](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Virbhadrasana-b.png)


## Sanskrit Meaning
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 212-213) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Virabhadra—the fierce spiritual warrior... Like Shiva, Virabhadra’s aim in destruction is not revenge but to destroy the real enemy, which is the ego standing in the way of humility." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 767) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"There are sixteen vinyasas to this asana, of which the 7th, 8th, 9th, and 10th are the states of the asana." [Note: In the Ashtanga system, Warrior I and II are combined into a single 16-count sequence]. 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1556) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Keeping the legs in the same position [after the 8th vinyasa/Warrior I Left] and doing puraka [inhale], bring arms down to shoulder level, and stand gazing at the fingertips of the left hand... this is the 9th vinyasa... The 10th vinyasa is just like the 9th vinyasa, but on the right side." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1557-1558) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Stand in Tadasana. Take a deep inhalation, and with a jump spread the legs apart sideways 4 to 4½ feet. Raise the arms sideways in line with the shoulders, palms facing down. Turn the right foot sideways 90 degrees to the right and the left foot slightly to the right... Exhale and bend the right knee till the right thigh is parallel to the floor... the bent knee should not extend beyond the ankle, but should be in line with the heel." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 215-216) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Press the hands to the floor... lift the body up off the floor; this is the 11th vinyasa. Then, doing rechaka, throw the body back... the 12th, 13th, 14th, 15th, and 16th vinyasas should be performed in the same manner as the 4th, 5th, 6th, 3rd, and 2nd vinyasas of the first Surya Namaskara." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1557-1558) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Movement
(Continuing from Virabhadrasana A), from the left side open the hip to the side, open the arms parallel to the floor, bend the knee over the ankle. Repeat on the right side. hands to the floor, lift the body, jump back...

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- "Warrior II adds frontal plane shoulder endurance (deltoid isometric hold)." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 48) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Contract the rhomboids to draw the scapulae toward the spine while engaging the serratus anterior to spread the arms apart. Co-activation of these muscles stabilizes the shoulder blades and opens the chest." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long, p. 470–471) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The front knee is abducted by the middle deltoid and supraspinatus... all the deltoid heads engage to stabilize your shoulders in place... anterior deltoid helps your latissimus dorsi to internally rotate the joints." *(Science of Yoga, Ann Swanson, p. 272) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "The back foot rotates inward 30 degrees and supinates. The back knee extends. The back hip extends and externally rotates. The front hip and knee flex to 90 degrees. Both shoulders abduct and externally rotate. The elbows extend. The forearms pronate. The cervical spine rotates to turn the head." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long, p. 468) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The spine is in extension, moving in a frontal plane from a sagittal axis. The pelvis is in a neutral position." *(The Manual of Yoga Anatomy, Sally Parkes, p. 364) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"Without actually moving them, create an energetic action of drawing your feet toward each other. Feel this more fully awaken the muscles in the inside of your legs, and from there feel a more natural and full awakening of mula bandha." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 776) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Allow your shoulder blades to release down your back while spreading across your collarbones." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 769) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"When in the states of Utkatasana and Virabhadrasana, there is no need to do rechaka and puraka more than five times. However, aspirants should not forget to do rechaka and puraka while performing the vinyasas." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1556) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- "The instruction to draw the shoulder blades down and back pulls the clavicle backward... and downward... so much that the brachial plexus or subclavian artery may become trapped between the clavicle and first rib... causing pain, numbness, coldness or tingling in the [arm]." *(Your Upper Body, Your Yoga, Bernie Clark, p. 624) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The knees may fall inward towards each other (called valgum or abduction) or move away from each other (called varum or adduction)." *(Your Body, Your Yoga, Bernie Clark, p. 581) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Do not practise if you have a cardiac condition, palpitations, heartburn, diarrhoea, or dysentery. Women with menorrhagia and metrorrhagia should avoid this asana." *(B. K. S. Iyengar Yoga: The Path to Holistic Health, B. K. S. Iyengar, p. 83) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Ensure that your right thigh is parallel to the floor. Keep the shin perpendicular to the floor, in line with your right heel... Pull the muscles of your right calf upward." *(B. K. S. Iyengar Yoga: The Path to Holistic Health, B. K. S. Iyengar, p. 83) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Your front knee is over or just behind your ankle, distributing the load on your joint evenly." *(Science of Yoga, Ann Swanson, p. 272) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "For knee injuries, decrease the bend of the right knee. For lower back injuries and high blood pressure, shorten the stance and place the right forearm on the right thigh." *(The Manual of Yoga Anatomy, Sally Parkes, p. 365) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
