# Kurmasana

"Kurma means a tortoise." 

![Kurmasana](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Kurmasana.png)


## Sanskrit Meaning
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 271) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"Kurmasana has sixteen vinyasas, and the 7th vinyasa is the state of the asana." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1573) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Jump the legs around the shoulders as in Bhujapidasana, lower to the floor, stretch the arms under the thighs, and place the chin on the floor... this is the 7th vinyasa." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1573) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
Tip of the nose or third eye
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Movement
Jump over upper arm from downward dog then sit down, legs extended to the sides, arms extended to the sides underneath the legs (knees over upper arms), chin and chest to the floor.
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- **Hamstrings:** "Intense lengthening of the semitendinosus, semimembranosus, and biceps femoris." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Spinal Extensors:** "Longissimus and iliocostalis work eccentrically to manage the deep forward fold." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Inner Thighs:** "Adductor magnus is stretched as the legs move into abduction and flexion." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "Maximum hip flexion with abduction. Shoulder internal rotation and extension (under the legs)." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Knee extension (in the full state) stretches the posterior joint capsule." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"This pose is said to clean the apana vayu." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1573) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Exhale fully to sink the chest towards the floor, engaging uddiyana bandha to create space in the pelvic floor." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 890) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- **Hamstring Attachment Strain:** "Forcing the heels wide while the chest is on the floor can overstretch the proximal hamstring tendons (sitting bone area)." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 55) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Shoulder Pressure:** "If the knees are too low on the arms, the weight of the legs can compress the brachial plexus or strain the shoulder joint." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 58) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Keep the feet flexed to engage the calves and protect the knee ligaments." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 115) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Ensure the thighs are high on the shoulders before sliding the arms underneath." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 117) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
