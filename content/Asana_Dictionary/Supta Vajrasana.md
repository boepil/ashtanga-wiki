# Supta Vajrasana (Reclining Thunderbolt Pose)

![Supta-Vajrasana](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Supta-Vajrasana.png)

## Sanskrit Meaning
"Supta means reclining. Vajra means a thunderbolt. Supta Vajrasana is the reclining version of the Thunderbolt pose, performed in Lotus (Padmasana) in the Ashtanga system." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 125) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"Supta Vajrasana has sixteen vinyasas. The 9th is the state of the asana." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1426) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Sit in Padmasana. Exhale, lie back on the floor. Arch the back and place the crown of the head on the floor. Cross the arms behind the back and hold the big toes (as in Baddha Padmasana)." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 125) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"nava 9: inhale, arch back and hold the toes. Breathe five times." 
*(Ashtanga Yoga Anusthana, Sharath Jois, p. 58) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
Nose (nasagre)
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Movement
Seated in Lotus, reclining back, arching the spine with the crown of the head on the floor, holding the big toes with crossed arms behind the back.
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- **Quadriceps:** "Intense stretch in the lotus position while the torso is in extension." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Pectorals:** "Expanded as the arms bind behind the back and the chest arches." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Spinal Extensors:** "Active to maintain the arch and lift the chest off the floor." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "Spinal extension. Hip external rotation and flexion (in lotus). Knee flexion. Shoulder internal rotation and extension (during the bind)." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"Mula Bandha is essential to protect the knees in this pose; by engaging the pelvic floor, you reduce the 'twist' in the knee joint during the reclining phase." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 802) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- **Knee Torque:** "The combination of Lotus and spinal extension puts extreme pressure on the knee ligaments." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 235) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Neck Compression:** "Putting too much weight on the head without enough lift from the chest can compress the cervical spine." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 238) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "If the knees feel any pain, do not perform the bind; instead, keep the hands on the floor for support." <div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>
- "Rule: The knees must stay grounded. If they lift, the arch is too deep for the current flexibility of the hips." <div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Teaching Notes
"This asana is a powerful counter-pose to the intense forward folds. It opens the chest and stretches the pelvic region deeply." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 126) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*
