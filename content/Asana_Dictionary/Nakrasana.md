# Nakrasana (Crocodile Pose)

![Nakrasana](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Nakrasana.png)

## Sanskrit Meaning
"Nakra means a crocodile. This asana resembles a crocodile pouncing on its prey." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 75) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"Nakrasana has five vinyasas. The 3rd is the state of the asana." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1436) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Perform Chaturanga Dandasana. With an inhalation, jump the whole body forward without lifting the hands or feet off the floor more than an inch. Exhale and land. Repeat this five times forward, then five times backward." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 75–76) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"In the Ashtanga tradition, this is a dynamic hopping movement performed in the low plank position." 
*(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
Nose (nasagre)
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Movement
Dynamic hops forward and backward in Chaturanga Dandasana.
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- **Pectoralis Major & Minor:** "Intensely active to stabilize the torso during the ballistic movement." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Triceps Brachii:** "Maintain the low plank height while generating the forward/backward momentum." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Core (Rectus Abdominis/Obliques):** "Essential for maintaining the 'plank' shape and preventing the hips from sagging during the hops." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "Elbow flexion. Shoulder protraction and flexion. Wrist extension. Hip and knee extension." *(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"Uddiyana Bandha must be held extremely firm to protect the spine from the impact of the landings. The core stability allows the body to move as a single unit." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 812) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- **Wrist Impact Trauma:** "The repetitive jumping and landing in extension can lead to acute wrist pain or stress fractures if not performed with control." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 335) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Lumbar Jolting:** "If the core is not engaged, the landing impact can travel into the lower back." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 338) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Focus on light landings; the sound of the feet and hands hitting the floor should be minimal." <div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>
- "Rule: The body must remain stiff as a board throughout the movement." <div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Teaching Notes
"This asana develops powerful explosive strength in the arms and core. It is one of the few truly 'ballistic' movements in the Ashtanga sequence." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 76) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*
