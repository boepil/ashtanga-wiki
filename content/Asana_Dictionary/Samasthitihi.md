# Samasthitihi (Equal Standing)

![Surya-Namaskar](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/Surya-Namaskar.png)

## Sanskrit Meaning
"Tada means a mountain. Sama means upright, straight, unmoved. Sthiti is standing still, steadiness. Tadasana [also called Samasthiti] therefore implies a pose where one stands firm and erect as a mountain." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 338) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Samasthiti, which means standing up in a straight line." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1600) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Samasthihi (equal standing), which invokes an attitude and awareness of equanimity as they feel the connection of body, breath, mind, and spirit." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 963) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"The āsanas described in this chapter all originate and finish with the fundamental standing position known as 'Husshyar' or 'attention'... just as the full Ashtanga Vinyasa sequence begins and ends each pose in samasthiti." 
*(Yoga Body: The Origins of Modern Posture Practice, Mark Singleton, p. 204) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Vinyasas from 1 to 7 are equal in all asanas... Afterwards, return to samasthiti." 
*(Yoga Makaranda, T. Krishnamacharya, p. 263, 270) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"After each practice, one should stand in samasthiti with hands folded together in front of the chest and pray to their ishtadeva (preferred God)." 
*(קרישנמצריה מלמד סדרה ראשונה (Yogasanagalu), T. Krishnamacharya, p. 316) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Movement
Feet engaged with surface awareness, toes stretched & heel pressed, pull knee-cap up, gentle root & abdomen lock, open chest, shoulder depressed, arms to the sides.
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- "Focus on the four corners of the feet, active quadriceps, and neutral pelvis." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 24) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Turn the foot in and lift the arch by contracting the tibialis posterior... then engage its antagonist muscle group, the peroneus longus and brevis... [to stabilize the foundation]." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long, p. 11) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Drawing the shoulder blades toward the midline [engages] the rhomboid, pectoralis minor, and serratus anterior muscles to lift and expand the chest." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long, p. 19) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "The standing knee extends. The standing hip is in neutral." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long, p. 75) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The spine is in a neutral position, moving in a sagittal plane from a frontal axis." *(The Manual of Yoga Anatomy, Sally Parkes, p. 164) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"Mula Bandha: Base of pelvis (pelvic floor lift)... Uddiyana Bandha: Abdominal lock (pull belly in and up)." 
*(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 45, 53) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

"Emphasize pada bandha and encourage students to maintain this energetic action whenever their feet are on the floor... Guide students into feeling the connection between pada bandha and mula bandha." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 958, 965) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- "When you slouch, you have limited lung capacity, as well as restricted movement of your diaphragm." *(Science of Yoga, Ann Swanson, p. 86) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "If the arches of the feet collapse or fall, then the lower back may compress." *(Yoga of the Subtle Body, Tias Little, Chapter 1) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The common cue to lift the kneecap... does not stop hyperextension and may actually increase it... creating more stress on the ACL." *(Your Body, Your Yoga, Bernie Clark, p. 183) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "The Tripod Foot: Weight distributed between: 1. Center of Heel 2. Base of Big Toe 3. Base of Little Toe." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 48) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "To reduce the stress on the ACL [in hyperextension], we need to pull the tibia posteriorly... by engaging the hamstrings... this co-contracts the quadriceps and the hamstrings, firmly stabilizing the knee joint." *(Your Body, Your Yoga, Bernie Clark, p. 184) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Shoulder Alignment: Keep the humerus integrated in the socket; depression of the scapula facilitates stability." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 24, 45) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
