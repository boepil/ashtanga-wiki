# Urdhva Mukha Paschimottanasana

"Urdhva means upwards, Mukha means face. Paschimottanasana is the intense stretch of the west (back)." 

![Urdhva-Mukha-Paschimattanasana](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Urdhva-Mukha-Paschimattanasana.png)


## Sanskrit Meaning

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"Urdhva Mukha Paschimattanasana has 15 vinyasas." 
*(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"From the 8th vinyasa (lying down, holding outer edges of feet), roll forward in the 9th vinyasa and balance on the sit bones with the face against the knees... this is the state of the asana." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1435) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
Toes
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Movement
Balance on the sit bones, hold outer edge of feet, pull the chest towards the thighs, arms and legs are extended.
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- **Hip Flexors:** "Deep engagement to maintain the compression between torso and thighs." *(The Manual of Yoga Anatomy, Sally Parkes, p. 394) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Hamstrings (Stretch):** "Intense lengthening while balancing." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 67) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Abdominals:** "Core integration is critical to prevent the torso from falling backward." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 67) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "Deep hip flexion. Knee extension. Balance point on the ischial tuberosities (sit bones)." *(The Manual of Yoga Anatomy, Sally Parkes, p. 394) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"Inhale to lift the chest and create space between the vertebrae (Axial Extension). Exhale to revolve [or fold] into that space." 
*(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 31) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

"Strong Uddiyana Bandha is necessary to stabilize the lumbar spine during the deep flexion." 
*(NotebookLM extraction) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- **Lumbar Load:** "The load on the lumbar spine... is significant if compensation through lumbar flexion occurs." *(Science of Yoga, Ann Swanson, p. 301) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- **Neck Strain:** "Pulling the head aggressively towards the knees can strain the cervical muscles; keep the neck long." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 152) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Focus on pulling the belly to the thighs first, then the chest, then the head." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 155) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "If balance is unstable, focus on the upward lift of the spine rather than the depth of the fold." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 157) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
