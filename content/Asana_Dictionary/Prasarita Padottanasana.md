## Sanskrit Meaning
"Prasarita means expanded, spread, extended. Pada means a foot. The pose is one where the expanded legs are stretched intensely." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 179) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"Prasaritapadottasana a, b, c... (It has 5 vinyasas)." 
*(Yoga Makaranda, T. Krishnamacharya, p. 85, 97) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"ekam 1: inhale, jump to the right side, place the hands on the waist. dve 2: exhale, place the hands on the floor between the feet. Inhale and lift the head up. trīṇi 3: exhale, place the head on the floor. Breathe five times." 
*(Ashtanga Yoga Anusthana, Sharath Jois, p. 40) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"catvāri 4: inhale, lift the head only, keep the hands on the floor. Exhale. pañca 5: inhale, come up. Exhale." 
*(Ashtanga Yoga Anusthana, Sharath Jois, p. 40) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Movement
A: feet parallel 4ft apart, fold forward from the hips with exhalation, both hands on the floor, between the feet, head towards the floor.
B: feet parallel 4ft apart, keep hands on the hips, fold forward from the hips with exhalation, head towards the floor.
C: feet parallel 4ft apart, interlace fingers behind the back, chest open, fold forward, interlaced hands towards the floor over the head.
D: feet parallel 4ft apart, hands to the big toes, fold forward with exhalation, head towards the floor.

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- "Engaging the psoas and its synergists (the pectineus) helps to flex the hips and draw the trunk forward." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long, p. 157) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The gluteus minimus and medius muscles contract concentrically... separating the legs away from the medial line of the body." *(The Manual of Yoga Anatomy, Sally Parkes, p. 116) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Contracting the rectus abdominis draws the trunk forward and deepens Prasarita Padottanasana." *(Key Muscles of Yoga, Ray Long, p. 103) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "The spine is in forwards flexion in a sagittal plane, moving from a frontal plane axis. The pelvis is in an anterior tilt." *(The Manual of Yoga Anatomy, Sally Parkes, p. 117) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The knees extend, the trunk flexes, and the elbows flex while the shoulders flex forward, adduct, and depress." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long, p. 156) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"In the 3rd vinyasa... the stomach should be drawn in properly, using only the uddiyana bandha, or stomach lock, and by slightly loosening the mula bandha, or anal lock." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1524) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Maintaining pada bandha, feel the awakening of your inner thighs, slightly spiraling them back... awakening a sense of mula bandha as you lift lightly and steadily from your perineum." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 823) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- "The load on the lumbar spine in a standing Forward Fold is significant... The lower back is particularly vulnerable during the transition in and out of the pose." *(Science of Yoga, Ann Swanson, p. 92) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Deep forward bends can affect blood pressure, so those with blood pressure issues should be careful not to overexert themselves." *(The Manual of Yoga Anatomy, Sally Parkes, p. 115) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Coming up too quickly can cause dizziness as your blood redistributes." *(Science of Yoga, Ann Swanson, p. 92) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "If the lower back rounds, bend the knees! This creates slack in the hamstrings, allowing the pelvis to tilt forward and the spine to neutralize." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown, p. 28) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Keeping a neutral spine, engage your core and push up to standing, as with a hip hinge... to help alleviate lower back pain." *(Science of Yoga, Ann Swanson, p. 113) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Stabilize the ankles by pressing the balls of the feet into the mat, while at the same time lifting the arches." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long, p. 157) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
