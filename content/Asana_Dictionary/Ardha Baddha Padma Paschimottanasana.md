# Ardha Baddha Padma Paschimottanasana (Half Bound Lotus Seated Forward Bend)

![Ardha-Baddha-Padma-paschimattanasana](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Ardha-Baddha-Padma-paschimattanasana.png)

## Sanskrit Meaning
"Ardha means half, baddha means caught, restrained and padma a lotus. Paschimottanasana is the posture where the back of the whole body is intensely stretched." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 248) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Ardha Baddha Padma Paschimottanasana: Half Bound Lotus West Intense Stretch Pose." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 941) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"This has 22 vinyasas. The 8th and 15th vinyasas are the asana sthiti." 
*(Yoga Makaranda, T. Krishnamacharya, p. 1429) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Up to the 7th vinyasa, practise according to the pascimottanasana vinyasa krama. But in the 7th vinyasa, extend the left leg out in front. Place the right foot on top of the left thigh, such that the right heel touches the left lower abdomen. Take the right hand behind the back and clasp the right big toe with the fingers. Hold the big toe of the extended left leg with the fingers of the left hand." 
*(Yoga Makaranda, T. Krishnamacharya, p. 1429) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Bend the left leg at the knee, and place the left foot over the right thigh. The left heel should press the navel and the toes should be stretched and pointing... Bring the left arm round the back from behind and with an exhalation catch the big toe of the left foot." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 248) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Inhale, stretch the back and gaze up for a few seconds... Exhale, move the trunk forward by bending the right elbow outwards. Rest the forehead, then the nose, then the lips and lastly the chin on the right knee." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 248) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Inhale, raise the head and trunk, release the hands, straighten the left leg... Repeat the pose on the other side." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 249) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
Foot
*(raw PDF)*

## Movement
Right leg in Padmasana, wrap the right arm around behind the back, hold the big toe, bend forward, chin to shin or knee. Repeat on the left side.
*(raw PDF)*

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- "Asymmetric Hip Mobility + Hamstring Loading: Lotus leg: hip external rotation flexibility; Extended leg: hamstring lengthening." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The seated asymmetrical poses... the purpose is the bent leg opening the pelvis for Lotus (Padmasana)." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Iliopsoas: The primary hip flexor, connecting the spine to the legs. Crucial for... lifting the legs [and folding]." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Erector Spinae: (Spinalis, Longissimus, Iliocostalis) Manage spinal extension and resist gravity in forward folds." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "Challenges symmetric spinal alignment with asymmetric hip position." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Joint Action: Deep hip flexion, ankle dorsiflexion [for the extended leg]." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The pelvis tips forward, drawing rotation at the hip joint. This allows the spine to extend long over the legs." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"Do the 8th and the 15th vinyasas with recaka, pulling in the stomach and extending the legs straight out." 
*(Yoga Makaranda, T. Krishnamacharya, p. 1429-1430) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"While placing the chin on the knee of the extended leg, the bent knee is brought close to the stretched leg. This gives a good pull to the navel and abdominal organs. Blood is made to flow round the navel and the genital organs." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 250) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- "If the hip stops rotating (due to bone structure or tight ligaments) and you force the foot higher, the torque transfers to the knee. This stresses the medial collateral ligament (MCL) and meniscus." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Trapped between the two condyles [of the knee] is the medial meniscus. If a student persists... the medial meniscus will first complain, then tear." *(Your Body, Your Yoga, Bernie Clark) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "If hamstrings are tight, they pull the pelvis into Posterior Tilt (tucking under), forcing the lumbar spine to round. This loads the intervertebral discs safely." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Rule: Never force the knee. If there is any pain in the knee, back off. The knee should remain closed (flexed) to protect itself." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "In Hip Openers (Lotus...), keep the knee flexion closed (heel to butt) [before rotating the hip to minimize rotational torque]." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Elevate Hips: Sit on a folded blanket or block. This helps tilt the pelvis forward in folds and keeps the spine straight." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
