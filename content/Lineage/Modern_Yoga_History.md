# Modern Yoga History

The modern yoga movement is the result of a complex dialogical exchange between Indian spiritual traditions and Western physical culture that began in the late 19th century. While today’s global practice is often presented as an ancient, unbroken lineage, it is more accurately described as a 20th-century synthesis that medicalized, secularized, and physicalized a once meditatively-focused discipline. For a broader view of the various traditions and their relationships, see the [[Genealogy_of_Modern_Yoga]].

## Early Modern Transition: The Sanitizers (1890s–1920s)
In the late 1800s, hatha yoga was widely stigmatized as the domain of "fakirs"—mendicant street performers who used physical contortions for alms. Swami Vivekananda, the first major international guru, explicitly shunned asanas (postures) in his 1896 work *Raja Yoga*, focusing instead on meditation and Neo-Vedanta philosophy to make Hinduism palatable to Westerners.

The reclamation of physical yoga was led by two primary disciples of the sage Paramahansa Madhavadasji:
* **Shri Yogendra**: Founded the Yoga Institute in 1918, arguably creating the first "yoga class" for householders. He stripped yoga of its mystical associations to create a secular health regime, heavily influenced by Western calisthenics (like J.P. Müller’s system).
* **Swami Kuvalayananda**: Founded Kaivalyadhama in 1924 to scientifically investigate the physiological effects of yoga. His "medicalization" of yoga helped legitimate it as a therapeutic science, influencing almost all subsequent teachers, including B.K.S. Iyengar.

## T. Krishnamacharya: The Mysore Crucible (1930s–1950s)
Commonly called the "Father of Modern Yoga," [[T_Krishnamacharya]] developed a radically innovative style at the Mysore Palace under the patronage of the Maharaja. Tasked with training young boys and performing spectacular demonstrations, he synthesized hatha techniques with Western gymnastics, wrestling, and circus contortion.

* **Innovations**: He pioneered *Vinyasa Krama*—the systematic linking of breath and movement—and incorporated Surya Namaskar (Sun Salutations) into the practice, though at the time they were considered separate physical culture exercises.
* **Lineage Impact**: His students (Jois, Iyengar, Devi, and his son Desikachar) became the primary vectors for yoga's global dissemination.

## The Lineages of the Krishnamacharya Tradition
Each of Krishnamacharya's disciples evolved his Mysore teachings into distinct global styles:

* **K. Pattabhi Jois (Ashtanga Vinyasa Yoga)**: Codified Krishnamacharya’s vigorous Mysore sequences into a fixed series of increasingly difficult postures. He emphasized Ujjayi breathing, Bandhas (energy locks), and Drishti (gaze points). This system birthed the modern "Power Yoga" and "Vinyasa Flow" movements. See [[K_Pattabhi_Jois]].
* **B.K.S. Iyengar (Iyengar Yoga)**: Initially a student of Krishnamacharya’s aerobic style, Iyengar later abandoned vinyasa for extreme precision, alignment, and long holds. His 1966 book *Light on Yoga* became the global "Bible" of the movement, introducing the extensive use of props (blocks, straps) to make yoga accessible to all bodies. He framed the physical practice as an embodied meditation containing all eight limbs of classical yoga. See [[BKS_Iyengar]].
* **Indra Devi**: The "First Lady of Yoga," she was the first Western woman to study with Krishnamacharya. She moved to Hollywood in 1947, teaching stars like Greta Garbo and Gloria Swanson. She reframed yoga as stress relief and relaxation, a focus that remains a cornerstone of Western practice.
* **T.K.V. Desikachar**: Krishnamacharya’s son, he focused on the therapeutic application of yoga (Viniyoga), emphasizing one-on-one instruction tailored to the individual's needs, a shift away from the mass-drill style of the early Mysore period.

## The Sivananda Diaspora and Global Expansion
* **Swami Sivananda**: A former physician, he promoted the "Yoga of Synthesis," integrating the four classical paths (Action, Knowledge, Devotion, Meditation). His prolific English-language writings helped frame yoga as a universal, spiritual science.
* **Swami Vishnudevananda**: Sivananda’s disciple who founded the International Sivananda Yoga Vedanta Centers. His 1960 work, *The Complete Illustrated Book of Yoga*, was a landmark illustrated guide that preceded Iyengar's success.
* **Paramahansa Yogananda**: His 1946 *Autobiography of a Yogi* remains one of the most influential spiritual texts, focusing on Kriya Yoga and meditation rather than postural practice.

## Contemporary Global Legacy
By the late 20th century, these lineages merged with New Age spirituality and psychology. Figures like Bishnu Ghosh (Yogananda’s brother) influenced the creation of Bikram Yoga, while Yogi Bhajan synthesized hatha and Sikh traditions to create Kundalini Yoga. Today, approximately 90% of global yoga stems from the mainstream legacy of Krishnamacharya, evolving into a multi-billion dollar industry that continues to balance its roots in Indian spirituality with modern athletic and therapeutic demands.
