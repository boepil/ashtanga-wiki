## Summary
Tirumalai Krishnamacharya (1888–1989) is commonly referred to as the "Father of Modern Yoga." He synthesized Western and Indian modes of physical culture into traditional Haṭha Yoga, fundamentally shaping the vinyāsa (movement) and krama (sequence) oriented approaches that dominate modern postural practice.

## Detail
### Early Life and Studies
Born in Muchukundapuram, Karnataka State, Krishnamacharya was the eldest child of a distinguished Vaiṣṇava Brahmin family. His great-grandfather had been head of the Śrī Parakālamatḥa in Mysore. In 1915, he journeyed to Tibet to study under Rāmmohan Brahmacāri. After seven years at Lake Mansarovar, he had "absorbed 'all of the philosophy and mental science of Yoga; its use in diagnosing and treating the ill; and the practice and perfection of asana and pranayama'" (Singleton, *Yoga Body*, p. 177). 

Despite his orthodox Brahmin heritage, circumstances later forced him to work in a coffee plantation in Karnataka, during which time he adapted his attire to "half-pants and half-sleeved shirt, socks and shoes, a hat on his head and a stick in his hand" (Singleton, *Yoga Body*, p. 197). 

### Teaching Methodology and Vinyasa Krama
Krishnamacharya transformed Haṭha yoga—"once an obscure backwater of yoga—into its central current" (Goldberg, *The Path of Modern Yoga*, p. 357). He elaborated a system where the "central component was a rigorous (and oftentimes aerobic) series of āsanas, joined by a repetitive linking sequence" (Singleton, *Yoga Body*, p. 175).

However, his teaching was not monolithic. "The essence of [Krishnamacharya’s] teaching is 'not that the person needs to accommodate him- or herself to yoga, but rather the yoga practice must be tailored to fit each person'" (Stephens, *Teaching Yoga*, p. 214).
*   **With youth (like K. Pattabhi Jois):** He taught vigorous asanas and dynamic sequences smoothly linked together.
*   **With healthy adults:** He focused on *raksana krama* (maintenance of health and relief of stress), teaching simpler asanas, pranayama, and meditative practices (Goldberg, *The Path of Modern Yoga*, p. 358).

### Vedic Chanting
Beyond posture, he was a master of the Vedas. Srivatsa Ramaswami recalls: "My guru taught several other sections of Vedic chanting... I think I have spent more than 1,500 hours learning and chanting these mantras with Śrī Krishnamacharya" (Ramaswami, *My Studies With Śrī Krishnamacharya*, p. 1).

### Personality
Historically, he was known as a demanding figure. "Krishnamacharya's manner was intimidating even while he was standing still... his straight back was telling of his rigidity and arrogance... he belittled the views of those who disagreed with him" (Goldberg, *The Path of Modern Yoga*, p. 208).
