# K. Pattabhi Jois

## Summary
Sri Krishna Pattabhi Jois (1915–2009) was the primary architect and global popularizer of Ashtanga Vinyasa Yoga. A devoted student of T. Krishnamacharya, Jois formalized his guru's vigorous, linking sequences into a standardized, set series of postures that revolutionized modern yoga in the West.

## Detail
### Early Life and Studies
Sri K. Pattabhi Jois was born on the full moon day of July in the small village of Kowshika. "From the age of five he was instructed in Sanskrit and rituals by his father... He began what was to be a twenty-five-year period of study with the great yogi Sri T. Krishnamacharya" (Jois, *Yoga Mala*, p. 1). 

Jois remained at the Sanskrit College in Mysore from 1930 to 1956, "studying Sanskrit and then the Vedas, and eventually earning a professorship in Advaita Vedanta. He taught there until 1973, when he left to devote himself fully to teaching yoga at his yoga shala" (Jois, *Yoga Mala*, p. 2).

### The Ashtanga Vinyasa System
Jois claimed his vinyāsa-based system was reconstructed by Krishnamacharya from a now-lost text called the *Yoga Korunta* by Vāmanarṣi. "The vigorous nature of the Ashtanga Vinyasa system is said to cure physical illness and purify the channels (nāḍī), or 'nerves,' in which prāṇa moves in the body" (Sarbacker, *Tracing the Path of Yoga*, pp. 211-212).

His defining phrase was: *"Yoga is ninety-nine percent practice and one percent theory."* He meant that practitioners "cannot be mechanical in our approach or resort to being only philosophical about yoga; we must engage it practically" (Jois, *Yoga Mala*, p. 1).

### Teaching Methodology
Jois institutionalized two primary modes of practice at the Ashtanga Yoga Nilayam in Mysore (established in 1948):
1. **Led Practice:** "Teacher-guided group practice following verbal commands" (the traditional Sanskrit count).
2. **Mysore Style:** "Self-guided practice often facilitated by forceful or aggressive hands-on adjustments by the teacher" (Sarbacker, *Tracing the Path of Yoga*, p. 211).

### Historical Impact
Jois was "discovered" by the Belgian yogin André Van Lysebeth, who published *Pranayama—la dynamique du souffle* in 1971, identifying Jois as a "yoga master in South India" (Goldberg, *The Path of Modern Yoga*, p. 357). 

The highly fashionable Ashtanga Vinyasa yoga is a direct development of a specific, unrepeated phase of Krishnamacharya’s teaching. The style that came to prominence in the West since the late 1980s through Pattabhi Jois "represents a unique and unrepeated phase of Krishnamacharya’s teaching" (Singleton, *Yoga Body*, pp. 175-176).

### The Transition from Palace to Global Practice
The Ashtanga system underwent a significant pedagogical and anatomical shift as it moved from preadolescent boys at the Mysore Palace to a global adult population:
- **Anatomical Adaptation**: Originally designed for young boys with narrow hips, the practice has adapted for adult practitioners (predominantly women). This includes modifications like standing with feet hip-distance in certain poses to manage joint stress [3].
- **From Gymnastics to Therapy**: What began as a vigorous, athletic training method influenced by Indian martial arts and Western gymnastics was formalized by Jois as "Yoga Chikitsa" (Yoga Therapy), emphasizing internal cleansing and purification [1][6].
- **The Fixed Sequence as a Mirror**: Jois utilized the constancy of the sequence as a "mirror" to reflect the practitioner's changing internal state, moving the focus from physical performance to self-observation [30].
- **Management of the "Yoga Industrial Complex"**: Jois preserved the practice's intensity while making it a "premium lifestyle," using the "teacher as gatekeeper" model to ensure stability before progression [21][28].
