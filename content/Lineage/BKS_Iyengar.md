# B.K.S. Iyengar

## Summary
Belur Krishnamachar Sundararaja (B.K.S.) Iyengar (1918–2014) was a seminal figure in modern yoga, renowned for his emphasis on structural alignment and the invention of yoga props. A student of T. Krishnamacharya, Iyengar's methodical approach made yoga accessible to a global audience and profoundly influenced how physical postures are taught today.

## Detail
### Early Life and Hardship
B.K.S. Iyengar's early life was marked by severe illness. "Throughout his childhood he suffered from general malnutrition as well as tuberculosis, malaria, and typhoid fever" (Stephens, *Teaching Yoga*, p. 211). In 1932, he was invited to Mysore to live with his sister and her husband, T. Krishnamacharya, who introduced him to yoga to improve his health.

His early teaching career was equally difficult. "On August 30, 1937, eighteen-year-old Iyengar arrived in Pune for the teaching job... He was practically broke. His worldly goods consisted of a pair of shirts and dhotis... he shaved and bathed without soap" (Goldberg, *The Path of Modern Yoga*, p. 376).

### Teaching Methodology: Alignment and Props
Unlike the continuous flow of Ashtanga Vinyasa, Iyengar's system focuses on static holds and exact anatomical alignment. "In Iyengar yoga poses are typically held for a much longer duration. It is in holding asanas that alignment is perfected along with energetic actions that refine the asana" (Stephens, *Teaching Yoga*, p. 211).

To achieve this, "Iyengar developed the extensive use of yoga props, including mats, ropes, blankets, and cushions, in order to assist active yoga practice and to provide support during passive and restorative practice" (Sarbacker, *Tracing the Path of Yoga*, p. 210).

He viewed this structural perfection not just as physical therapy, but as a spiritual practice. "The awareness from within of every pore of the body in the various asanas is itself meditation... [Conditioning asanas] are themselves vehicles of meditative action" (Goldberg, *The Path of Modern Yoga*, p. 423).

### Historical Impact and Global Reach
A major turning point occurred when classical violinist Yehudi Menuhin requested a meeting. Menuhin, suffering from exhaustion, "fell into a deep sleep" after a short relaxation exercise guided by Iyengar. Menuhin's subsequent patronage helped bring Iyengar to the West (Goldberg, *The Path of Modern Yoga*, p. 380).

Iyengar authored the groundbreaking book *Light on Yoga: Yoga Dipika* (1966), which "was hailed early on as the 'Bible of yogāsana' and later as the 'Bible of Modern Yoga'" (Sarbacker, *Tracing the Path of Yoga*, p. 209). His demonstrations and manual "created in the Western public an awareness of the... artistic potential of postural practice" (Goldberg, *The Path of Modern Yoga*, p. 140).

His influence penetrated public institutions; in 1969, Iyengar’s method was officially introduced into the adult education curriculum in London, marking the first yoga classes provided by the Inner London Education Authority (Goldberg, *The Path of Modern Yoga*, p. 381).
