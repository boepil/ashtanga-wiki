# The Skeletal System

## Summary
The skeletal system is the foundational framework of the body, consisting of approximately 206 bones that act as levers for movement. In yoga, the health of the skeleton is maintained through proper alignment, joint congruency, and understanding the specific mechanics of the spine and major joints.

## Detail

### The Spine: The Central Pillar
The spine is the central axis of the body, serving both as structural support and as the physical channel for energy (Sushumna Nadi).
- **The Four Natural Curves**:
    1. **Cervical (Lordosis)**: Neck region (C1-C7).
    2. **Thoracic (Kyphosis)**: Upper/mid-back region (T1-T12), anchors the ribs.
    3. **Lumbar (Lordosis)**: Lower back region (L1-L5), bears significant weight.
    4. **Sacral (Kyphosis)**: Fused base of the spine.
- **Intervertebral Disks**: The shock absorbers of the spine. Movement must be mindful to avoid uneven compression (e.g., in deep forward folds or twists).
- **Spinal Patterns**: Practice involves moving the spine through flexion, extension, rotation, and lateral flexion, ideally preceded by **Axial Extension** (elongation) to create space.

### Major Joints and Structural Stability
- **Joint Congruency**: Articular surfaces fitting perfectly together ensures even pressure distribution and protects cartilage.
- **Skeletal Variance**: Individual differences in bone morphology (e.g., femoral neck-shaft angle) dictate the ultimate range of motion in poses like Trikonasana or Padmasana.

### Functional Anatomy of Regional Segments
- **Pelvic Bowl**: Capable of anterior tilt (increasing lumbar curve) and posterior tilt (stabilizing the lower back).
- **Hips**: Ball-and-socket joints that are the mechanical center of movement.
- **Shoulder Girdle**: Highly mobile complex requiring dynamic stabilization from the rotator cuff.
- **Knees and Ankles**: Foundational for stability in standing poses.
