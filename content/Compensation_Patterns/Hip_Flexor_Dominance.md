# Hip Flexor Dominance in Forward Folds

![[ChatGPT Image May 5, 2026, 04_48_15 PM.png]]
### 1. Pelvic Tilt and Spinal Integrity

**Lack**  
Eccentric hamstring length and the capacity for the pelvis to rotate anteriorly (anterior pelvic tilt) at the hip joint.

**Compensation**  
Excessive rounding (flexion) of the lumbar spine (posterior pelvic tilt) and over-recruitment of the psoas and rectus femoris to "pull" the torso down. This creates a "hinge" in the lower back rather than a fold at the hips.

**Where It Shows Up**  
- **Movement:** Touching toes, sitting on the floor with straight legs.  
- **Yoga:** [[Paschimottanasana]], [[Surya_Namaskar]] (Uttanasana), [[Janu Sirsasana]].

**What to Train**  
- **Eccentric Gluteal Control:** Maintaining a degree of engagement in the glutes to protect the hamstring attachments during the descent.
- **Reciprocal Inhibition:** Actively engaging the quadriceps to signals the hamstrings to release.
- **Axial Extension:** Creating length in the spine before initiating the fold to ensure the discs are not compressed unevenly.

**Yoga Expression**  
A prominent "hump" in the lumbar region; shallow or restricted breath as the diaphragm is compressed by the forward-pulling psoas; and potential strain at the ischial tuberosity (sitting bones).

**Meta Insight**  
"Mobility (spinal) substituting for mobility (hip)." The system uses the more mobile segment (lumbar spine) to complete a shape the less mobile segment (hip) cannot achieve.

---

## Diagnostic Cue
While in Paschimottanasana, can you find the space to lift your chest forward and flatten your lower back? If the lower back remains rounded regardless of effort, the hip flexors are dominating and the hamstrings are at their limit.

## Common Misinterpretation
Practitioners often think they need to "pull harder" with their arms to get deeper. This only increases the lumbar rounding and hip flexor dominance. The focus should shift to "tilting the pelvis" rather than "reaching the head."
