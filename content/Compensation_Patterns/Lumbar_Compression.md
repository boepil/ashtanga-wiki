# Lumbar Compression

**Lack**  

![[ChatGPT Image May 6, 2026, 08_14_08 PM.png]]

### 1. Thoracic Mobility and Hip Extension

Lack of hip extension (tight hip flexors) and insufficient thoracic spine mobility.

**Compensation**  
"Dumping" into the more mobile lumbar segment (L4-L5/S1); excessive squeezing of the upper fibers of the gluteus maximus causing the femurs to rotate externally (knees splaying out), which "pinches" the sacroiliac (SI) joint.

**Where It Shows Up**  
- **Movement:** Reaching backward, deep spinal arches.  
- **Yoga:** [[Urdhva Dhanurasana]], [[Surya_Namaskar]] (Upward Dog), [[Kapotasana]].

**What to Train**  
- **Internal Thigh Rotation:** Emphasizing internal rotation while extending the hips to broaden the sacrum and protect the SI joint.
- **Hip Flexor (Psoas) Lengthening:** To allow the pelvis to stay neutral while the spine arches.
- **Thoracic Extension:** Building mobility in the mid-back to distribute the arch evenly across the entire spine.

**Yoga Expression**  
Sharp, localized pain in the lower back; restricted, labored breath; and a feeling of being "stuck" or "pinched" in the peak of the arch. This is a loss of *Sukha* (ease) due to structural compression.

**Meta Insight**  
"Stability (lumbar) substituting for mobility (hip/thoracic)." The spine sacrifices its stabilizing role to provide the mobility the hips and mid-back are lacking.

---

## Diagnostic Cue
In Urdhva Dhanurasana, can you keep your feet and knees parallel? If the toes turn out and the knees splay, you are using the external rotators of the glutes to "force" the lift, which is compressing your lower back.

## Common Misinterpretation
Practitioners are often told to "squeeze the glutes" to lift higher. While the lower fibers are needed for extension, squeezing the whole glute maximus causes rotation that leads directly to SI joint pain. The cue should be "lift from the tailbone and hug the inner thighs together."
