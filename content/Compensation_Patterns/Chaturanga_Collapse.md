# Collapse in Chaturanga

![[Chaturanga_Collapse.png]]
### 1. Serratus Anterior and Core Integration

**Lack**  
Lack of eccentric control in the triceps and failure of the serratus anterior to maintain scapular protraction (preventing winging) combined with poor intra-abdominal pressure (IAP).

**Compensation**  
Shoulders "dipping" below the level of the elbows (stressing the anterior capsule); "winging" of the scapulae (blades poking out); lumbar hyperextension (sinking the hips) or excessive kyphosis (rounding the upper back) to hide core weakness.

**Where It Shows Up**  
- **Movement:** Push-up, plank, burpee.  
- **Yoga:** [[Surya_Namaskar]] (Chaturanga), transitions in vinyasa (jump backs).

**What to Train**  
- **Serratus Anterior:** "Scapular push-ups" to build protraction strength and stability.
- **TVA/Core:** Building IAP (Bandha engagement) to prevent the "sagging" of the midsection and support the spine from the inside out.
- **Triceps:** Eccentric loading (slow lowers) to control the descent.

**Yoga Expression**  
Breath becomes "stuck" or strained as the ribcage collapses; "hard" landing in jump-backs; shoulder pain in the anterior deltoid region. This is a lack of *Sthira* (steadiness) leading to a loss of structural integrity.

**Meta Insight**  
"Mobility substituting for stability" (sinking into the joint capsule rather than supporting with muscular and energetic tension).

---

## Progression Path
- **Modified:** Knees-down Chaturanga to master the 90-degree elbow bend without the shoulders dipping.
- **Support:** Using a block under the sternum to gauge depth.
- **Dynamic:** Master the "plank to chaturanga" transition before adding the jump-back.

## Diagnostic Cue
If you stop halfway down, can you maintain the lift of your belly and the width of your shoulder blades? If your shoulder blades "pinch" together, the serratus anterior has yielded.

## Common Misinterpretation
Practitioners often think Chaturanga is a "tricep exercise." While the triceps are active, it is primarily a **core and scapular stability pose**. If the foundation (core/shoulder girdle) is weak, the triceps cannot effectively hold the weight.
