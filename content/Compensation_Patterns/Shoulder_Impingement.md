### 1. Scapular Stability and Upward Rotation

Underdeveloped capacity for the scapula to rotate upward and remain stable against the ribcage (Serratus Anterior insufficiency) and a lack of humeral external rotation (Rotator Cuff weakness).

**Compensation**  
Upper trapezius dominance causing "shrugging" of the shoulders toward the ears; humeral internal rotation (elbows splaying out); rib flare and lumbar hyperextension to bypass shoulder range limitations.

**Where It Shows Up**  
- **Movement:** Overhead press, push-up (scapular winging), plank.  
- **Yoga:** [[Surya_Namaskar]] (Downward Dog), [[Surya_Namaskar]] (Chaturanga), [[Urdhva Dhanurasana]] (restriction in the armpits).

**What to Train**  
- **Serratus Anterior:** Protraction and upward rotation exercises (e.g., plank protraction / "scapular push-ups").
- **External Rotators (Infraspinatus/Teres Minor):** Strengthening to keep the humeral head centered in the socket.
- **Lower Trapezius:** To counteract upper trap dominance without pinning the shoulders down (which inhibits rhythm).

**Yoga Expression**  
Instability in inversions like [[Pincha Mayurasana]], restricted/shallow breath in the upper lobes due to neck tension, and "crunching" sensations in the subacromial space. This is a failure of *Sukha* (ease) due to excessive *Sthira* (steadiness) becoming rigidity.

**Meta Insight**  
"Global effort (Upper Traps/Pecs) replacing local control (Serratus/Rotator Cuff)."

## Progression Path
- **Awareness:** Detect the "shrug" in Tadasana when arms are raised.
- **Integration:** Focus on the "wing" of the shoulder blade moving toward the armpit in Downward Dog.
- **Load:** Gradual weight-bearing in Plank with a focus on protraction.

## Diagnostic Cue
In Downward Facing Dog, can you rotate your elbow pits toward the front of the mat without the ribcage dropping or the shoulders shrugging? If the neck disappears, the upper traps have taken over.

## Common Misinterpretation
Practitioners often try to "pull the shoulders down and back" during overhead reaching. This is an anatomical error; the scapula *must* rotate upward to allow the humerus to move without impingement. The "shoulders down" cue actually *causes* impingement in overhead poses.
