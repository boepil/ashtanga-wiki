# Hatha Yoga Pradipika

The **Hatha Yoga Pradipika** (Light on Hatha Yoga) is one of the most influential surviving texts on classical Hatha yoga. Compiled in the 15th century by Svatmarama, it draws upon earlier, now-lost tantric and yogic sources to create a comprehensive manual of physical and energetic practices.

## Core Purpose

Unlike the strictly meditative focus of the [[Yoga_Sutras]], the HYP approaches spiritual liberation through the purification and manipulation of the physical and subtle bodies. Its explicit goal is to prepare the practitioner for the higher meditative states of Raja Yoga (the yoga of Patañjali).

## Key Practices

The text outlines four main chapters (upadeshas):
1. **Asana**: Describes 15 physical postures (mostly seated, though some non-seated poses appear) aimed at bringing health, lightness, and steadiness to the body.
2. **Shatkarmas and Pranayama**: Details six cleansing techniques (*shatkarmas*) to clear excess phlegm and fat, followed by extensive instructions on breath retention ([[Pranayama]]) to purify the energetic channels (*nadis*).
3. **Mudras and Bandhas**: Describes physical and energetic "seals" (such as *Mula Bandha*, *Uddiyana Bandha*, and *Jalandhara Bandha*) used to direct the vital energy ([[Prana_Vayus]]) into the central channel, the [[Sushumna]], to awaken Kundalini.
4. **Samadhi**: Explores the ultimate state of absorption, particularly focusing on *Nadanusandhana* (listening to the internal sound).

## Significance in Modern Yoga

The HYP is a critical link between ancient contemplative traditions and modern postural yoga. It demonstrates that the physical practices were traditionally deeply intertwined with energetic regulation and spiritual liberation, providing the historical basis for the use of breath and locks in the Ashtanga method.
