# Bhagavad Gita

The **Bhagavad Gita** ("Song of the Lord") is a 700-verse Hindu scripture that is part of the epic Mahabharata. It takes the form of a dialogue between the warrior prince Arjuna and his charioteer, Krishna (an avatar of the deity Vishnu), on the battlefield of Kurukshetra, just as a great fratricidal war is about to begin.

## The Yoga of Action and Devotion

Facing a moral crisis about fighting his own kin, Arjuna refuses to fight. Krishna responds by imparting profound spiritual wisdom, outlining several paths (yogas) to liberation:
- **Karma Yoga (The Yoga of Action)**: Krishna teaches that one must perform their duty (*dharma*) without attachment to the results. "You have a right to your actions, but never to your actions' fruits." This echoes the concept of *Isvara Pranidhana* in the [[Niyamas]].
- **Jnana Yoga (The Yoga of Knowledge)**: Understanding the eternal, indestructible nature of the true Self (the Atman), distinguishing it from the temporary physical body.
- **Bhakti Yoga (The Yoga of Devotion)**: Unconditional love and surrender to the Supreme Divine as the ultimate path to salvation.

## Philosophical Significance

The Gita provides a practical philosophy for living in the world while remaining spiritually unattached. It redefines yoga not just as ascetic withdrawal from society, but as skill in action and equanimity of mind amidst the chaos of life. Its teachings on duty, detachment, and devotion deeply inform the broader philosophical context within which physical practices like [[Asana]] are meant to be understood.
