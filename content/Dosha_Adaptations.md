# Dosha Adaptations

In Ayurveda, the body is governed by three primary energetic constitutions known as **Doshas**: **Vata** (air/ether), **Pitta** (fire/water), and **Kapha** (earth/water). 

Health is maintained when these doshas are in harmony. Ashtanga Yoga practice must often be adapted to restore equilibrium when a practitioner presents with a doshic imbalance.

## 1. Vata (Air/Ether)

**The Imbalance:** Manifests as a scattered mind, restlessness, irregular breathing, and physical coldness. Vata types are prone to joint pain, stiffness, and cracking joints as they age.

**Practice Adaptations:**
- **Tempo:** Move gradually and steadily. Perform Sun Salutations slowly to build internal heat without causing nervous agitation.
- **Focus:** Emphasize grounding. Focus heavily on standing poses and utilize **Pada Bandha** (foot lock) to anchor the body.
- **Duration:** Linger longer in deep, seated asanas to calm the nervous system.

## 2. Pitta (Fire/Water)

**The Imbalance:** Characterized by excess heat, irritability, and a competitive, ego-driven attitude toward practice. Prone to inflammation or "wrong pain" from pushing too hard.

**Practice Adaptations:**
- **Tempo:** Go slow and move consciously. Focus on deep relaxation rather than achieving a "sweaty workout."
- **Focus:** Practice strictly with **Ahimsa** (non-harming). Surrender the ego-driven desire for "the perfect pose" and prioritize *sukham* (ease).
- **Pranayama:** Incorporate cooling breathing techniques like *Sitali*.

## 3. Kapha (Earth/Water)

**The Imbalance:** Shows up as heaviness in the limbs, lethargy, boredom, or a lack of motivation. Manifests physically as excess mucus and slow circulation.

**Practice Adaptations:**
- **Tempo:** Kapha types benefit greatly from a warming, vigorous, and fast-flowing Vinyasa practice to stimulate metabolism.
- **Focus:** Prioritize heart-opening postures and sustained backbending to clear mucus and combat lethargy.
- **Pranayama:** Begin practice with *Kapalabhati* to stoke the inner fire (*Agni*).

## Prishta-Vata (Toxic Wind in the Spine)

A specific yogic concept is **Prishta-Vata**, referring to the accumulation of "bad Vata" or "toxic wind" in the spinal region and joints.

- **Effects:** It obstructs flexibility and causes joint pain. If the movement of vital wind (*Vayu Sancharam*) is obstructed, the body trembles during practice, significantly increasing the risk of injury.
- **The Remedy:** Proper **Vinyasa Krama** is essential to "thin the blood" and remove these toxic winds through sweat. Asanas like *Paschimottanasana* ensure that energy (*Prana Vayu*) flows correctly up the spine, clearing the blockages.
