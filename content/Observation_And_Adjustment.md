# Observation And Adjustment

Observation and adjustment are the primary pedagogical tools in the Ashtanga yoga room. The goal is to inform the student's body and internal awareness, not to force a specific aesthetic shape.

## Visual Assessment Skills

Visual assessment is the art of seeing beyond the aesthetic shape to understand the student's internal functional reality.

1. **Scanning the Room**
   - **The Forest Before the Trees:** Soften your gaze to take in the whole room first. Sense the collective energy.
   - **Microscopic Eyes:** Look for subtle signs of energy dissipation—a wandering gaze, fidgeting, or shallow breath—which often precede physical misalignment.

2. **Key Misalignments to Watch For**
   - **Foundation (Feet):** Check for collapsing arches or bowing knees. The feet dictate the chain of alignment.
   - **Spine:** Watch for hyperlordosis (swayback) in the lumbar, or kyphosis (rounding) in the thoracic. Ensure the spine moves as a cohesive curve.
   - **Joint Stacking:** Monitor for hyperextension ("locking out") in elbows and knees.

3. **Tension vs. Compression**
   - **Tension:** Muscular resistance (felt in the belly of the muscle). Adaptable; changes with breath. Sensation is "stretching."
   - **Compression:** Skeletal limit (bone hitting bone). Unchangeable. Sensation is "pinching" or a hard stop.
   - **The Golden Rule:** You cannot see sensation. Ask: *"What are you feeling?"*

## Safe Physical Adjustments

Modern adjustments prioritize functional alignment and stability over aesthetic perfection.

### Types of Adjustments
1. **Supportive/Stabilizing:** Using hands or props to provide a foundation and a sense of safety.
2. **Deepening:** Using resistance (e.g., PNF stretching) where the student contracts against the teacher's touch to safely access a deeper range.
3. **Corrective:** Guiding the body toward functional alignment to alleviate structural compression.

### Safety Protocols
- **For the Teacher:** Use your legs and body weight, not just arms/back. Conserve energy and do not over-adjust. Never use aggressive force (e.g., stepping on students).
- **For the Student:** Distinguish between healthy tension and hard compression. Any sharp, shooting, or electrical pain is a stop signal.
- **Consent:** Always ask permission before adjusting. Ask "What are you feeling?" during the adjustment.

## Verbal Cueing Techniques

Effective cueing acts as a bridge between the teacher's understanding and the student's embodied experience.

1. **Types of Cues**
   - **Anatomical & Biomechanical:** Isolate prime movers (e.g., "Squeeze the glute to extend the hip").
   - **Imagery & Metaphor:** Evoke a felt sense (e.g., "Root down like a tree").
   - **Directional:** Orienting the body in space (offer options based on individual skeletal reality).
   - **Energetic:** Direct *prana* or focus on internal locks (*bandhas*) and gaze (*drishti*).

2. **Economy of Words**
   - **Say Less, Teach More:** Over-cueing creates mental noise. Silence allows students to process.
   - **Inquiry over Correction:** Ask "What do you feel here?" or "What stops you?" to foster self-study (*svadhyaya*).
   - **Empowering Language:** Avoid "Nocebos" (fear-based language). Use positive, explorative language.
