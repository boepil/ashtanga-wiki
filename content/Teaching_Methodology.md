# Teaching Methodology

Teaching Ashtanga Yoga requires a delicate balance between upholding the rigid traditional sequences (**Paramparā**) and employing responsive pedagogy tailored to the individual (**Viniyoga**). The role of the teacher is not merely to instruct physical shapes, but to facilitate a process of self-discovery, utilizing the mat as a laboratory for the student's internal transformation.

## Lineage and Transmission

Knowledge in yoga is passed through a direct transmission, a relationship that is fundamentally sacred.
- **Paramparā (Lineage):** Honoring the transmission of knowledge from teacher to student. The tradition reveals that revered masters like T. Krishnamacharya were innovators who modified postures to suit an individual's age, constitution (**deha**), and capability (**śakti**).
- **The Teacher as Student:** An effective teacher must first be a constant student, embodying the practice they transmit. As Pattabhi Jois famously stated, "Yoga is ninety-nine percent practice and one percent theory".
- **Empowerment:** The teacher's goal should be to interfere as little as possible, allowing students to develop strength of character and independent mastery.

## Energy Management in Teaching

Effective teaching requires managing the subtle energy (*prana*) of both the individual student and the collective class environment.

1. **Reading Student Energy**
   - **Microscopic Observation:** Look beyond physical alignment to observe how energy flows through breath, skin tone, and nervous system state.
   - **Breath Indicators:** Shallow or erratic breathing signals overexertion; guide students back to a slow, steady rhythm to stabilize energy.
   - **Mental Focus (*Drishti*):** A wandering gaze indicates dissipating energy. Cueing specific gaze points re-channels mental force.

2. **Managing Classroom Atmosphere**
   - **Active vs. Passive:** Balance dynamic effort with passive engagement to prevent exhaustion (*tamas*) or agitation (*rajas*).
   - **Sequencing:** Structure the class to cultivate *sattva* (equilibrium)—energizing without depleting, calming without dulling.

3. **Teacher Self-Regulation**
   - **Preserve Reserves:** Avoid physical burnout by using verbal cues and props rather than physically supporting every student.
   - **Compassionate Detachment:** Maintain *samatvam* (equanimous balance). Care deeply for the student's progress without becoming emotionally entangled.
   - **Internal Equilibrium:** Keep a "passive inward gaze" to remain grounded, allowing you to hold space for the class.

## The Scope of Practice
- **Educator vs. Clinician:** A yoga teacher is primarily a guide offering a pathway for self-discovery through movement and breath. A yoga teacher is **not** a clinician or therapist.
- **Handling Injuries:** Explicitly remind students that you are a yoga teacher, not a medical professional. If an injury is beyond your expertise, refer the student out.
- **Limiting Claims:** Be legally and ethically cautious about curative claims.

## Ethical Foundation: Yamas and Niyamas
- **Ahimsa (Non-harming):** Creating a safe space and respecting physical limitations. Brute force is explicitly ruled out.
- **Satya (Truthfulness):** Being truthful about your own knowledge and limitations.
- **Brahmacharya (Right Use of Energy):** Maintaining clear professional boundaries and disciplined use of energy.
- **Aparigraha (Non-grasping):** Remaining unattached to a student's "progress" or the financial "fruits" of your labor, treating teaching as *Karma Yoga* (selfless service).

## Continuing Education
- **Returning to the Source:** Studying in Mysore (Mysuru) or with senior authorized teachers.
- **Specialized Workshops:** Training in hands-on adjustments, functional anatomy, and scriptural study (*Yoga Sutras*, *Bhagavad Gita*).
- **Svādhyāya (Self-Study):** Scholarly study is the "education of the self," internalizing ennobling thoughts to manifest in character.
