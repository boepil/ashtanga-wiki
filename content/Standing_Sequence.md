# Standing Sequence

## Summary
The Standing Sequence is the foundational block of asanas following the Sun Salutations in all Ashtanga series. It builds leg strength, balance, and pelvic stability while opening the hips and hamstrings.

## Detail
The Standing Sequence grounds the practitioner, developing the roots necessary for the more complex seated and inverted postures. It consists of balancing poses, deep forward folds, and lateral stretches.

Key postures in the sequence include:
1. **Padangusthasana & Pada Hastasana**: Initial forward folds that stretch the back body and establish the mind-muscle connection with the hamstrings.
2. **Trikonasana (Triangle) & Parivrtta Trikonasana (Revolved Triangle)**: Lateral extension and spinal rotation.
3. **Parsvakonasana (Side Angle) & Parivrtta Parsvakonasana**: Deeper lateral extension with hip opening and spinal twisting.
4. **Prasarita Padottanasana (A, B, C, D)**: Wide-legged forward folds that open the hips, stretch the hamstrings, and mobilize the shoulders.
5. **Parsvottanasana**: Intense side stretch testing balance and hamstring flexibility.
6. **Utthita Hasta Padangusthasana**: Standing balance, leg extension, and hip opening.
7. **Ardha Baddha Padmottanasana**: Standing half-bound lotus forward fold, introducing intense knee and hip rotation.
8. **Utkatasana**: Chair pose, building quadriceps strength and generating intense heat.
9. **Virabhadrasana A & B**: Warrior poses A and B, cultivating power, focus, and hip stability.

This sequence establishes the core alignment principles that will be heavily relied upon during the seated sequence.
