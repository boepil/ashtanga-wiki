# Muscular System

## Summary
The muscular system provides the force for movement and the support for stability in Ashtanga Yoga. Beyond the major muscle groups, specific focus is placed on deep core stabilizers, the iliopsoas (the structural intermediary), and refined gluteal differentiation to ensure safety in backbends and standing balances.

## Detail

### Core Stabilizers
The core is not just the superficial abdominal muscles; it is a complex system of deep stabilizers:
- **Transversus Abdominis (TVA)**: The deepest abdominal muscle, acting like a corset to stabilize the trunk and maintain **Intra-Abdominal Pressure (IAP)**.
- **Multifidus**: Deep back muscles that increase spinal stability.
- **Diaphragm**: The "roof" of the core, working with the pelvic floor to regulate pressure.
- **Pelvic Floor**: The base of the core, essential for **Mula Bandha**.

### The Iliopsoas: "The Flying Buttress"
Composed of the Psoas Major and Iliacus, it is the strongest hip flexor and a critical core muscle.
- **Structural Role**: Props up the spine from the legs.
- **Ashtanga Applications**: Vital for jump-throughs, lifts (Lolasana), and maintainnig an upright spine in seated postures.
- **Fascial Connection**: Interweaves with the diaphragm, creating a direct link between the core/pelvis and the respiratory system.

### Gluteal Differentiation
- **Gluteus Maximus**: Prime mover in hip extension. In backbends, "squeezing" the glutes can cause external rotation and SI joint compression. Safe backbending requires emphasizing internal rotation of the thighs to broaden the sacrum.
- **Gluteus Medius & Minimus**: Primary abductors and stabilizers for the pelvis in standing balances (e.g., Vrksasana).

| :--- | :--- |
