# Drishti

*Dṛṣṭi* (gaze) is the third element of [[Tristana]], the three-fold focus of Ashtanga Yoga. It involves directing the physical gaze to a specific point during each asana to stabilize the mind, prevent distraction, and manage the flow of vital energy (*Prana*).

## The Nine Traditional Drishtis

| Sanskrit | English | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nāsāgre dṛṣṭi** | Nose-tip gaze | The tip of the nose (stimulates the vagus nerve/parasympathetic system). |
| **Brūmadhye dṛṣṭi** | Third-eye gaze | Between the eyebrows (Ajna Chakra). |
| **Nābhau dṛṣṭi** | Navel gaze | The navel (belly button). |
| **Hastāgre dṛṣṭi** | Hand gaze | The tip of the middle finger or the hand. |
| **Aṅguṣṭhāgre dṛṣṭi** | Thumb gaze | The tip of the thumb. |
| **Pādāgre dṛṣṭi** | Toe gaze | The tip of the big toe. |
| **Pārśvayoḥ dṛṣṭi** (Right) | Right-side gaze | Far to the right side (over the shoulder). |
| **Pārśvayoḥ dṛṣṭi** (Left) | Left-side gaze | Far to the left side (over the shoulder). |
| **Ūrdhva dṛṣṭi** | Upward gaze | Upward toward space or the horizon. |

## Integration with Postures

The assigned Drishti is rarely arbitrary; it usually supports the anatomical alignment of the cervical spine.

- **Forward Folds:** The gaze is directed toward the big toes (*Pādāgre*) or the navel (*Nābhau*), encouraging the elongation of the spine and deep internal focus.
- **Backbends:** Postures that open the chest utilize the third-eye (*Brūmadhye*) or upward gaze (*Ūrdhva*), facilitating the extension of the upper spine and the lifting of the heart.
- **Twists:** The gaze follows the direction of the spinal rotation (*Pārśvayoḥ* - side gaze), supporting the cervical spine's participation in the twist.
- **Inversions (e.g., Downward Dog):** The gaze is directed at the navel (*Nābhau*), which encourages a slight chin tuck (*Jalandhara Bandha*) and the activation of the core locks.
