# Seasonal Routines (Ritucharya)

**Ritucharya** (seasonal routine) is the Ayurvedic practice of aligning one’s lifestyle, diet, and yoga discipline with the rhythms of nature. Because seasonal shifts directly impact the balance of the body's *Doshas*, the practice must adapt accordingly.

Traditional texts suggest starting a new yoga practice during seasons of equable climate, like Spring or Autumn. *Yoga Mala* notes that Winter is also a superior time to begin, provided one practices early in the morning (*Brahma Muhurta*).

## 1. Summer (Grishma): The Pitta Season

Heat and intensity aggravate the fire element, potentially leading to inflammation, exhaustion, and irritability.

- **Practice:** Move slowly and consciously. Avoid "hot and sweaty" overexertion. Include cooling pranayama like *Sitali*.
- **Diet:** Favor cooling, juicy, and soothing foods: milk, ghee, coconut, and sweet fruits. Strictly avoid overly spicy, salty, or sour foods (like tomatoes or lemons).

## 2. Winter/Spring (Hemanta/Vasanta): The Kapha Season

Marked by heaviness, coldness, and the accumulation of mucus.

- **Practice:** Engage in warming, vigorous, and flowing Vinyasa. Emphasize **Tapas** (disciplined heat). Postures like *Mayurasana* (Peacock Pose) are highly recommended to stoke the gastric fire (*Agni*). Utilize *Kapalabhati* to clear respiratory passages.
- **Diet:** Favor warming, easily digestible grains and *mung dal*. Use warming spices like ginger, cumin, and cardamom. Avoid heavy, cold dairy and excessive sweets.

## 3. Autumn (Sharad): The Vata Season

The season of wind and rapid change, often leading to mental restlessness, physical dryness, and joint stiffness.

- **Practice:** Move gradually and steadily. Emphasize grounding through standing postures and strict regularity in the practice schedule to calm the nervous system.
- **Diet:** Favor warm, unctuous (oily) foods. Use plenty of **ghee** to lubricate the joints and soothe the nerves. Avoid dry, raw, or cold foods.

## Ritu-Sandhi (The Junction of Seasons)

**Ritu-Sandhi** refers to the delicate two-week period between seasons. During this transition, immunity lowers, and the body and breath may feel unsettled.

- **Moderation:** Strictly follow *Mitahara* (moderate diet).
- **Gradual Shift:** Change practice intensity gradually (*shanaih shanaih*). Do not abruptly switch from a vigorous winter practice to a slow summer practice.
- **Purity:** Maintain *Saucha* (cleanliness) and devotion to keep the mind steady through the environmental transition.
