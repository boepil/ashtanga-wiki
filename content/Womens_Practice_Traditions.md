# Womens Practice Traditions

## Summary
The Ashtanga and Krishnamacharya lineages maintain specific traditional guidelines regarding women's practice. These guidelines dictate periods of rest aligned with natural cycles—specifically Moon Days (full and new moons), the "Ladies Holiday" (menstruation), and precise modifications or suspensions of practice during and after pregnancy.

## Detail

### Moon Days (Full and New Moons)
In the Ashtanga tradition, physical practice is suspended on the days of the new and full moons. "The [Ashtanga Vinyasa] practice is done every day except Saturdays, new moons, and full moons" (Stephens, *Teaching Yoga*, p. 529).

T. Krishnamacharya historically aligned these days with periods of required restraint. He noted that "on the days of the new and full moons, the transitory day of the sun... sexual intercourse and the like are not related to brahmacharya," indicating these are days set aside for conservation of energy and rest from intense discipline (Krishnamacharya, *Yoga Makaranda*, p. 1634).

### Menstruation (The "Ladies Holiday")
Resting during the first few days of the menstrual cycle is a strict traditional mandate, though therapeutic postures are recommended for pain relief.

"During period, women should not practice yoga" (Krishnamacharya, *Yoga Makaranda*, p. 206). Similarly, Stuart Ray Sarbacker notes that Pattabhi Jois "recommends that women suspend practice during menstruation" (*Tracing the Path of Yoga*, p. 1309).

**Therapeutic Recommendations for Pain:**
- "If they have abdominal pain, if they practice mahamudra, baddhakonasana and upavishtakonasana along with moolabandha, they will get relief from pain" (Krishnamacharya, *Yoga Makaranda*, p. 206).
- "Ladies suffering from severe pain in the back during the menstrual period will obtain relief in this pose [Malasana II] and the back will feel soothed" (Iyengar, *Light on Yoga*, p. 258).
- Pattabhi Jois noted regarding the four Marichyasanas: "Some women suffer from abdominal pain during menstruation. This is removed by the practice of these asanas" (*Yoga Mala*, p. 1611).

**Strict Contraindications:**
All sources universally forbid inversions during menstruation.
- "Women should avoid headstand and shoulderbalance during their menstrual period" (Sen-Gupta, *A Little Book of Yoga*, p. 123).
- "On no account stand on your head during the menstrual period" (Iyengar, *Light on Yoga*, p. 231).

### Pregnancy Modifications

**First Trimester:**
- "All the asanas can be practised during the first three months of pregnancy" (Iyengar, *Light on Yoga*, p. 232).
- However, specific poses like Baddhapadmasana and deep twists are cautioned early on: "Pregnant women should not practice this asana [Marichyasana] after the second month" (Jois, *Yoga Mala*, p. 1611).

**Second and Third Trimesters:**
- After the third month, inversions like Sirsasana (Headstand) and Sarvangasana (Shoulderstand) "should not be done" (Krishnamacharya, *Yoga Makaranda Part II*, pp. 1510-1511).
- Deep forward folds must be stopped: "Pregnant women should not do this asana [Pascimottanasana]. But this can be done up to the third month of pregnancy" (Krishnamacharya, *Yoga Makaranda*, p. 172).
- "Women who are 5 month pregnant must not do asanas involving extreme bending" (Krishnamacharya, *Yoga Makaranda*, p. 205).
- Jois strictly warns against intense abdominal compression: "The asanas that should not be practiced by those who are pregnant are Marichyasana D and Garbha Pindasana" (*Yoga Mala*, p. 1649).

**Suspension of Practice:**
- Jois recommended that women "suspend practice after the eighth month of pregnancy, resuming it one or more months after childbirth" (Sarbacker, *Tracing the Path of Yoga*, p. 1309).

**Postpartum Recovery:**
- "No asanas should be done during the first month after delivery. Thereafter they may be practised mildly... Three months after delivery all asanas may be practised with comfort" (Iyengar, *Light on Yoga*, p. 233).
- Core work must be approached with extreme caution: "There should be no abdominal pressure from core work or kapalabhati pranayama for at least six weeks... gradually move back into toning the abdominal core" (Stephens, *Teaching Yoga*, p. 714).
