# Trataka

# Trataka (Concentrated Gazing)

*Trāṭaka* is the Shatkarma of concentrated gazing. It is a dual-purpose practice designed to cleanse the physical eyes and simultaneously steady the mind, serving as a bridge between physical purification and deep meditation.

## Method

The practice involves focusing the vision on a single point to induce physiological and mental stillness.

### Bāhya Trāṭaka (External Gazing)
1. Sit comfortably in a meditative posture.
2. Gaze steadily at a small mark or a candle flame placed precisely at eye level.
3. Maintain the gaze **without blinking** until the eyes tire or begin to water profusely.
4. **Transition:** Close the eyes gently. Do not rub the eyes or open them abruptly afterward.

### Antar Trāṭaka (Internal Gazing)
Performed with the eyes closed, the internal gaze is focused either on the tip of the nose or the space between the eyebrows (*Ajna Chakra*). This helps develop the internal focus necessary for *Shambhavi Mudra*.

## Benefits

- **Visual Health:** It physically cleanses the visual organs through tearing and is traditionally believed to destroy eye diseases and sharpen eyesight.
- **Destroying Lethargy:** It is highly effective at destroying *tamas* (sloth, lethargy, and mental dullness).
- **Concentration:** By anchoring the visual sense, the mind becomes one-pointed (*ekāgratā*), paving the way for deep meditation.

## Safety and Contraindications

- **Eye Conditions:** Individuals with glaucoma, a displaced retina, or active eye infections (pus) should avoid forceful gazing.
- **Initial Discomfort:** Beginners may feel a slight burning sensation or experience heavy tearing; this is a normal part of the cleansing process and subsides as the ocular muscles strengthen.
