# Nervous system dysregulation — asana modifications

## Overview
During periods of nervous system dysregulation, the objective of asana practice shifts from "attaining depth" to "attaining stability." The practice should be used as a tool to down-regulate the sympathetic nervous system and avoid the "fight or flight" triggers that can occur during intense exertion.

**General Principle:** Prioritize the **parasympathetic shift** over cardiovascular intensity. Use the breath as a strict barometer; if the breath becomes jagged, the intensity is too high.

## Poses to Avoid Temporarily
- **Rapid Surya Namaskara:** Moving too fast can spike the heart rate and trigger a sympathetic loop.
- **Deep Backbends (Kapotasana, [[Urdhva Dhanurasana]]):** These poses naturally stimulate the SNS. If they trigger a sense of panic, clenching, or breath-holding, they should be modified or skipped.
- **Aggressive Jump-backs:** The explosive nature of transitions can contribute to a "rushed" and dysregulated state of mind.

## Poses to Emphasize
- **Forward Folds ([[Paschimottanasana]], [[Prasarita Padottanasana]]):** These are inherently calming and encourage sensory withdrawal (*Pratyahara*).
- **Long [[Savasana]]:** Extending the final rest to 10–15 minutes to ensure the PNS has fully taken over.
- **Balasana** (Child's Pose): Used as a frequent "reset" button whenever the breath is lost or the heart rate is too high.

## Key Modifications by Pose

### 1. Ujjayi Breathing
- **Modification:** Soften the sound of the breath. It should be audible to the practitioner but not a "forced" or "aggressive" hiss.
- **Cue:** "Breathe as if through a silk thread—steady, soft, and unbroken."

### 2. Drishti (Gaze)
- **Modification:** Use a "soft gaze" (*Nasagrai*) or even close the eyes during forward folds to reduce external sensory input. Avoid a piercing or intense focus.
- **Cue:** "Let the eyes be heavy and the internal focus be broad."

### 3. Vinyasa Count
- **Modification:** Add an "extra" breath in each transition or hold poses for 8–10 breaths instead of 5 to allow the nervous system more time to settle into the shape.
- **Cue:** "Wait for the breath to lead the movement."

## Practice Structure During Rehabilitation
- **Mysore vs. Led:** Mysore style is strongly recommended as it allows the practitioner to stop, rest in Balasana, or skip poses that feel overwhelming. Led classes can often force a practitioner into a sympathetic pace that bypasses their internal signals.
- **Finish Early:** If the mind becomes scattered or the jaw clenches midway through the series, move directly to the Finishing Sequence (Inversions and Savasana) to prioritize recovery.

## Return to Full Practice
The system is regulated when the practitioner can move through the full Primary Series with a steady 1:1 or 1:2 breath ratio, without jaw clenching, and without feeling "shaky" or emotionally raw after the practice.
