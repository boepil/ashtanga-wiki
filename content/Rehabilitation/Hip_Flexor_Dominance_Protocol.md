# Hip Flexor Dominance Protocol

This protocol addresses "Lower Cross Syndrome"—the systemic imbalance of overactive, shortened hip flexors and inhibited, weak glutes (Hogan, Built From Broken, p. 329). In Ashtanga practice, this dominance manifests as lumbar compression in backbends and a "stuck" pelvis in forward folds. Because the psoas major interweaves with the respiratory diaphragm at the T12–L1 junction (Myers, Anatomy Trains, p. 356), hip flexor restriction physically impairs the freedom of the breath, creating a cycle of structural and physiological tension.

## Overview

## Guidelines
- Frequency: 3-5 days per week.
- Session time: 25-35 minutes.
- Principle: Reciprocal Inhibition. Always follow a psoas release with a glute activation to "lock in" the new range (Starrett, Becoming a Supple Leopard, p. 272).

## Pain guide
- 0: No pain — good to train.
- 1-3: Stiff/Tension — proceed with mobility, focus on breath.
- 4+: Pinching/Stabbing — stop immediately, joint impingement likely.

## Phase A — Prep / Mobility (5-8 min)

### Targeted Gut Smashing (Psoas Release)
(Starrett, Becoming a Supple Leopard, p. 288-298)
- How: Lie prone on a lacrosse ball or soft medicine ball. Position the ball just inside the hip bone (ASIS). Gently "scrub" the tissue by moving side-to-side.
- Duration: 2 minutes per side.
- Target: Restores sliding surfaces between the psoas, iliacus, and abdominal wall.

### Banded Hip Extension (Distraction)
(Starrett, Becoming a Supple Leopard, p. 306)
- How: Secure a heavy band to a post. Step one leg through and place the band at the gluteal fold. Step back into a lunge. The band should pull the femur forward.
- Duration: 2 minutes per side.
- Target: Resets the femoral head in the center of the capsule to clear impingement before stretching.

### Active Lunge Stretch
(Goodman, Foundation, p. 340)
- How: From a kneeling lunge, tuck the pelvis (posterior tilt) and squeeze the glute of the back leg. Lift the chest and reach the arms overhead.
- Sets/Reps: 3 holds of 30 seconds.
- Target: Lengthens the psoas and QL without compressing the lumbar spine.

## Phase B — Strength (20-25 min)

### The Founder Exercise
(Goodman, Foundation, p. 314-336)
- How: Stand with feet shoulder-width apart, knees soft, weight on heels. Hinge from the hips, pushing the butt back and lifting the chest. Reach arms forward/up.
- Sets/Reps: 3 sets of 1-minute holds.
- Target: Redefines the core by shifting the load from the hip flexors to the entire posterior chain (glutes, hams, spinal extensors).

### Glute Bridge (Reciprocal Inhibition)
(Hogan, Built From Broken, p. 342)
- How: Lying on the back, knees bent. Squeeze glutes and lift hips. Avoid arching the lower back.
- Sets/Reps: 3 sets of 15-20 reps.
- Target: Directly inhibits the overactive psoas by activating its antagonist (Gluteus Maximus).

### Clamshells (Lateral Stability)
(Hogan, Built From Broken, p. 327-333)
- How: Lying on the side, knees bent. Lift the top knee while keeping feet together and pelvis stable.
- Sets/Reps: 3 sets of 15 reps per side.
- Target: Strengthens the glute medius to prevent the hip flexors from taking over during single-leg stability.

## Phase C — Control / Endurance (5-10 min)

### Standing Hinge with Chest Lift
(Goodman, Foundation, p. 336)
- How: Perform a partial hinge. Focus on pulling the shoulder blades *down* rather than back to accentuate the natural curve of the spine while keeping the glutes engaged.
- Sets/Reps: 3 sets of 12 controlled reps.
- Target: Teaches the "hinge" pattern needed for safe transitions in the Sun Salutations.

### Dead Bug (Psoas-Core Coordination)
(Hogan, Built From Broken, p. 328)
- How: Maintain lower back contact with the floor. Slowly lower opposite limbs.
- Sets/Reps: 3 sets of 10 slow reps per side.
- Target: Retrains the psoas to act as a stabilizer rather than a primary mover under low-load conditions.

### Bird-Dog (Spiral Line Integration)
(McGill, Back Mechanic, p. 389)
- How: On hands and knees, extend opposite arm and leg. Keep the hips level.
- Duration: 5 holds of 10 seconds per side.
- Target: Integrates the cross-body connection between the glute and the opposite lat.

## Progression
(Goodman, Foundation, p. 15)
Criteria for progress:
- **Weight on Heels:** You must be able to hold the Founder with weight strictly on the heels and a "straight line from butt to neck" before advancing to more complex sequences.
- **Glute Awareness:** Advance when you can feel the glutes firing during the Couch Stretch or Lunge Stretch (using reciprocal inhibition).

## When to seek help
- Numbness or tingling in the front of the thigh (Meralgia Paresthetica).
- Deep, "internal" snapping sound in the hip joint (Snapping Hip Syndrome).
- Radiating pain from the groin into the testicles or labia.
