# Breath Holding — asana modifications

## Overview
Breath holding (involuntary Kumbhaka) usually occurs when the physical demand of a pose exceeds the practitioner's metabolic efficiency or stability. The brain "locks" the breath to create trunk stiffness. Modifications focus on using the breath as a primary movement initiator and using Bandhas as "pressure valves" to maintain stability without gasping.

## Primary Modifications

### Paschimottanasana (Seated Forward Fold)
- **Segmental Coordination:** Initiate the exhalation with the lower abdominal muscles to deepen the fold. Direct the inhalation toward the *back* of the rib cage (posterior breathing) to create space where the abdomen is compressed (Kaminoff, p. 358).
- **Active Pause:** Do not "chase" the feet if it causes the breath to stop. Back off 10% until the Ujjayi sound is consistent and "thin" (Ashtanga Knowledge Base, p. 256).

### Urdhva Dhanurasana (Wheel Pose)
- **Quiet Breathing:** In this deep backbend, the body is already in a state of "maximal inhalation." Attempting to take deep, full breaths can cause panic. Use "quiet, relaxed breathing" to maintain the shape (Kaminoff, p. 372).
- **Exhale into Effort:** Exhale as you push up into the pose to engage the core and protect the lower back.

### Vinyasa Transitions
- **Bandhas as Pressure Valves:** Apply Mula Bandha and Uddiyana Bandha during the jump-back. This creates internal stability (IAP) so the diaphragm doesn't have to "lock" to hold the spine together (Vayu Jung Doohwa, p. 185).
- **Diaphragmatic Gasps:** During high-intensity movements (like the lift-off in Navasana), take short, concise nasal inhalations to maintain trunk stiffness without holding the breath (Starrett, p. 47).

## General Cues
- "Breath is the master, movement is the servant."
- "If the sound of the breath stops, the yoga stops."
- "Keep the throat soft and the tongue relaxed."
