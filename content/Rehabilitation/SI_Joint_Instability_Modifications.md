# SI Joint Instability Modifications

## Overview
The objective when practicing with SI joint instability is to minimize "shearing" forces across the joint. This is achieved by maintaining a neutral pelvis, shortening the stance in asymmetric poses, and avoiding extreme ranges of motion that pull the pelvic bones in opposite directions.

**General Principle:** Prioritize **Stability** over **Depth**. Hug the midline and move the pelvis and spine as a single unit when possible.

## Poses to Avoid Temporarily
- **Deep Asymmetric Twists ([[Marichyasana]]):** Forcing the spine to rotate while the pelvis is fixed can create high shearing force at the SI joint.
- **Wide-Legged Forward Folds ([[Prasarita Padottanasana]]):** The extreme abduction of the legs can pull on the SI ligaments.
- **Deep Lunges:** Poses like **Anjaneyasana** can create excessive tension on the iliopsoas, which attaches near the SI joint and can contribute to instability.

## Poses to Emphasize
- **[[Utkatasana]] (Chair Pose):** Excellent for symmetrical gluteal engagement and pelvic stability.
- **[[Setu Bandhasana]] (Bridge Pose):** Strengthens the posterior chain symmetrically.
- **[[Samasthitihi]] (Mountain Pose):** The blueprint for a neutral, stable pelvis.

## Key Modifications by Pose

### 1. [[Utthita Trikonasana]] (Triangle) & [[Utthita Parsvakonasana]]
- **Change:** Shorten the distance between the feet by 6–10 inches. Instead of reaching for the floor, place the hand on a block or the shin.
- **Cue:** "Hug the outer hips toward each other and press firmly through the outer edge of the back foot."
- **Return:** Increase the stance width only when the "hug" of the hips can be maintained without pain.

### 2. Seated Twists
- **Change:** Allow the "sitting bone" of the rotating side to lift slightly off the floor. This allows the pelvis to move *with* the spine, reducing the shear at the SI joint.
- **Cue:** "Move the pelvis and heart together as one unit."
- **Return:** Keep the pelvis fixed only when the deep core and gluteal strength can support the rotation safely.

### 3. [[Urdhva Dhanurasana]] (Wheel) & Backbends
- **Change:** Place a block between the thighs and squeeze it. This prevents the legs from splaying out and encourages the deep core to stabilize the pelvis.
- **Cue:** "Squeeze the block and lift from the back of the legs, not the lower back."
- **Return:** Remove the block when the legs can maintain parallel alignment naturally.

## Practice Structure During Rehabilitation
- **Shorten the Series:** Focus on the Standing Sequence and the Finishing Sequence. Skip the more complex seated twists of the Primary Series until stability is restored.
- **Symmetrical Vinyasas:** If jumping back creates a "clunking" sensation, step back to Plank to maintain pelvic control.

## Return to Full Practice
The SI joint is considered stable when the practitioner can perform **Utthita Hasta Padangusthasana** (Standing Hand-to-Toe) without the pelvis dropping or shifting laterally, and when twists can be performed without residual aching in the back of the pelvis.
