# Lumbar Compression Protocol

This protocol addresses "local hinge faults" where individual lumbar vertebrae bend independently, creating shear forces and disc compression (Starrett, Becoming a Supple Leopard, p. 54). In Ashtanga, this is most common during backbends (Urdhva Dhanurasana) or when "dropping back" without adequate hip extension. The goal is to build spinal stiffness through the "McGill Big 3" (McGill, Back Mechanic, p. 333) and restore the "S-shape" curve of the spine through axial extension and decompression breathing (Kaminoff, Yoga Anatomy, p. 237; Goodman, Foundation, p. 241).

## Overview

## Guidelines
- Frequency: Daily (especially "Spine Hygiene").
- Session time: 20-30 minutes.
- Principle: Spinal Stiffness. The spine is built to handle compression only when in a neutral, braced position (Starrett, p. 32). Avoid all loaded flexion/extension during the acute phase.

## Pain guide
- 0: No pain — good to train.
- 1-3: Dull Ache — proceed with decompression, avoid end-range.
- 4+: Sharp/Radiating — stop immediately. This indicates nerve root sensitivity or disc displacement (McGill, p. 351).

## Phase A — Prep / Mobility (5-8 min)

### Park Bench Decompression
(McGill, Back Mechanic, p. 301)
- How: Stand with hands on a table or counter (like a park bench). Push the "heels" of the hands down to post the bodyweight up, allowing the spine to hang and decompress.
- Duration: 3 rounds of 30 seconds.
- Target: Reduces internal spine friction and clears minor nerve impingements.

### Tummy Lying (Disc Bulge Shrinkage)
(McGill, Back Mechanic, p. 351)
- How: Lie prone on the floor with a pillow under the chest if needed. Relax the glutes and lower back completely.
- Duration: 3-5 minutes.
- Target: Helps "shrink" dynamic disc bulges and reduces radiating pain before standing or moving.

### Two-Hand Rule Awareness
(Starrett, Becoming a Supple Leopard, p. 42-43)
- How: Place one thumb on the sternum and the other on the pubic bone. Ensure they stay on the same horizontal plane. If they move apart, you are overextending (compressing the back).
- Duration: Practice during all standing and sitting transitions.
- Target: Diagnostic tool for maintaining a "braced neutral" spine in daily life.

## Phase B — Strength (20-25 min)

### The McGill Big 3: Modified Curl-Up
(McGill, Back Mechanic, p. 333-336)
- How: Lie on back, one leg straight, one bent. Hands under the lower back to maintain the curve. Lift head and shoulders 1 inch off the floor.
- Sets/Reps: Descending pyramid (e.g., 6 reps, 4 reps, 2 reps) of 10-second holds.
- Target: Builds core endurance without bending the spine.

### The McGill Big 3: Side Bridge
(McGill, Back Mechanic, p. 337-338)
- How: Support weight on elbow and knees (or feet). Maintain a straight line from head to knees.
- Sets/Reps: Descending pyramid of 10-second holds.
- Target: Stabilizes the lateral core and protects the spine from shear forces.

### The McGill Big 3: Bird-Dog
(McGill, Back Mechanic, p. 338-340)
- How: On hands and knees, extend opposite arm and leg. Hold the spine in its "most resilient position." Sweep the floor with hand/knee between reps.
- Sets/Reps: Descending pyramid of 10-second holds.
- Target: Targets hip extensors and deep spinal muscles (multifidus) for stability.

### The Woodpecker Exercise
(Goodman, Foundation, p. 245-246)
- How: From a staggered stance, hinge from the hips while keeping the chest high. Reach arms behind you, then overhead. Focus on weight on the front heel.
- Sets/Reps: 3 sets of 30-45 second holds.
- Target: Removes pressure from the spine/knees by shifting the load to the upper hamstrings and glutes.

## Phase C — Control / Endurance (5-10 min)

### Decompression Breathing
(Goodman, Foundation, p. 241-249)
- How: Squeeze adductor muscles to pull the pelvis down. Inhale deeply to expand the ribcage *upward* away from the pelvis, creating space in the lumbar spine.
- Sets/Reps: 10 breaths.
- Target: Directly reduces pressure between the discs.

### Axial Extension Integration
(Kaminoff, Yoga Anatomy, p. 237-238)
- How: Simultaneously reduce both the primary and secondary curves of the spine (tucking chin slightly, neutralizing pelvis) to increase the overall length of the spine.
- Duration: 5 holds of 20 seconds.
- Target: Physical expression of the Bandhas, providing internal support for the lumbar column.

### Spine Hygiene: Hip Hinge Practice
(McGill, Back Mechanic, p. 11)
- How: Practice bending from the hips (the "hinge") for all tasks (tying shoes, picking up props). Never flex the spine to reach the floor.
- Target: Removes the movement triggers that keep spinal tissues sensitive.

## Progression
(McGill, Back Mechanic, p. 333)
Criteria for progress:
- **Descending Pyramid:** Only advance the duration of holds once you can complete the 6-4-2 pyramid with perfect "spine-sparing" form.
- **Waking Symptoms:** If morning back stiffness decreases, the protocol is working. If it increases, check "Spine Hygiene" during sleep (e.g., pillow between knees).

## When to seek help
- Loss of bowel or bladder control (Cauda Equina Syndrome — EMERGENCY).
- Progressive weakness in the legs (e.g., foot drop).
- Pain that wakes you up in the middle of the night.
- Numbness or tingling in the "saddle area."
