# Hamstring Overstretching Protocol

This protocol addresses "Yoga Butt" (Proximal Hamstring Tendinopathy), an overuse injury at the ischial tuberosity caused by repetitive end-range passive stretching without adequate eccentric strength (Hogan, Built From Broken, p. 340). In the Superficial Back Line (SBL), the hamstrings function as a "jump rope" of tension from the pelvis to the feet (Myers, Anatomy Trains, p. 39). When the hamstrings are overstretched, this line "derails," leading to a loss of structural integrity and chronic inflammation at the tendon attachment.

## Overview

## Guidelines
- Frequency: 3-4 days per week.
- Session time: 25-35 minutes.
- Principle: Loaded Lengthening. Passive stretching is strictly prohibited during the acute phase. All lengthening must be performed under load to build motor control (Starrett, Becoming a Supple Leopard, p. 321).

## Pain guide
- 0: No pain — good to train.
- 1-3: Dull/Achy — proceed with eccentric load, reduce range.
- 4+: Sharp/Pinching at the "sitting bone" — stop immediately. This indicates tendon compression against the ischium (Hogan, p. 341).

## Phase A — Prep / Mobility (5-8 min)

### Executive Hamstring Smash and Floss
(Starrett, Becoming a Supple Leopard, p. 340)
- How: Sit on a firm chair or bench. Place a lacrosse ball under the hamstring, near the "hotspot." Slowly extend and flex the knee while applying downward pressure.
- Duration: 2 minutes per side.
- Target: Restores "sliding surfaces" between the medial and lateral hamstrings (Myers, p. 39).

### VooDoo Floss Band Compression
(Starrett, Becoming a Supple Leopard, p. 326-327)
- How: Wrap a compression band (VooDoo Floss) around the upper thigh with 50% overlap and 50% tension. Perform standing hinges and air squats for 2 minutes.
- Duration: 2 minutes per leg.
- Target: The "most powerful method" for ungluing matted-down tissue and restoring position (Starrett, p. 326).

### Contract and Relax (PNF) Mobilization
(Starrett, Becoming a Supple Leopard, p. 323)
- How: Lie on back, lift leg. Use hands or a strap to hold the leg. Push the leg *away* from the face into the strap (5 sec), then relax and move slightly deeper (10 sec).
- Reps: 5 cycles per leg.
- Target: Builds tension at end-range to restore contraction capacity.

## Phase B — Strength (20-25 min)

### The Woodpecker Exercise (Upper Hamstring Focus)
(Goodman, Foundation, p. 245-246)
- How: Staggered stance, front knee over ankle. Hinge back until you feel a "deep pull" in the upper hamstring. Lift chest, reach arms overhead.
- Sets/Reps: 3 sets of 45-second holds.
- Target: Shifts load from the knee and lower back to the upper hamstrings and glutes.

### Eccentric Hamstring Bridges
(Hogan, Built From Broken, p. 342; Starrett, p. 321)
- How: Perform a glute bridge. Slowly "walk" the feet out away from the body until legs are almost straight, then drop hips and reset.
- Sets/Reps: 3 sets of 10 slow "walk-outs."
- Target: Builds eccentric strength to protect the tendon during the lengthening phase of asanas.

### Romanian Deadlift (RDL) with Dowel
(Hogan, Built From Broken, p. 338; Starrett, p. 321)
- How: Hold a dowel against the spine (head, upper back, sacrum). Hinge forward by pushing hips back, keeping the dowel in contact with all three points.
- Sets/Reps: 3 sets of 15 reps.
- Target: Retrains the "Hinge" pattern while ensuring the hamstrings are loaded throughout the range.

## Phase C — Control / Endurance (5-10 min)

### The Founder (Full Posterior Chain Integration)
(Goodman, Foundation, p. 314)
- How: Feet wide, weight on heels. Hinge deep, arms reaching forward. Imagine pulling the floor apart with the feet.
- Sets/Reps: 2 sets of 1-minute holds.
- Target: Integrates the SBL from the heels to the base of the skull.

### Single-Leg Balance (Stabilization)
(Hogan, Built From Broken, p. 330)
- How: Stand on one leg, knee slightly soft. Maintain a level pelvis.
- Sets/Reps: 3 sets of 45 seconds per side.
- Target: Forces the hamstrings and glutes to work as stabilizers in an asymmetrical load.

## Progression
(Starrett, Becoming a Supple Leopard, p. 321)
Criteria for progress:
- **Loaded Range:** You must be able to perform a full-range RDL without "sitting bone" pain before returning to deep forward folds (Paschimottanasana).
- **SBL Continuity:** Advance when you can maintain a flat back in the Founder while keeping the knees straight (Myers, p. 39).

## When to seek help
- Persistent "stabbing" pain at the ischial tuberosity during daily activities (sitting, driving).
- Visible bruising or swelling in the back of the thigh.
- Inability to perform a basic glute bridge without cramping.
