This protocol addresses the structural failure in Chaturanga Dandasana, characterized by winging scapulae, rounded shoulders, and a sagging midsection. Grounded in the "Spiral Line" force transmission, it focuses on the myofascial continuity between the serratus anterior and the external obliques (Myers, Anatomy Trains, p. 289). Failure occurs when the Pectoralis Minor is "locked-short," pulling the scapula into an anterior tilt (Myers, p. 322), and the core fails to maintain Intra-Abdominal Pressure (IAP), resulting in an "arch fault" (Starrett, Becoming a Supple Leopard, p. 40).

## Overview

## Guidelines
- Frequency: 3-4 days per week.
- Session time: 25-35 minutes.
- Principle: Quality over quantity. Maintain a "braced neutral" spine throughout all pushing patterns (Starrett, p. 11).

## Pain guide
- 0: No pain — good to train
- 1-3: Mild — OK with caution, reduce range if needed
- 4+: Sharp — stop and modify immediately

## Phase A — Prep / Mobility (5-8 min)

### Pectoralis Minor Mobilization
(Myers, Anatomy Trains, p. 324-326)
- How: Use a lacrosse ball or thumb pressure just below the coracoid process (front of the shoulder). Lean into a wall and slide the hand up the surface into hyperflexion.
- Duration: 2 minutes per side.
- Target: Lengthens the proximal Deep Front Arm Line to prevent the scapula from being pulled into an anterior tilt.

### Thoracic Extension on Foam Roller
(Hogan, Built From Broken, p. 371)
- How: Place a foam roller at the mid-back (thoracic spine). Support the head and gently lean back, creating extension without arching the lower back.
- Sets/Reps: 2 sets of 10 slow extensions.
- Target: Relieves upper back tension and allows for better scapular positioning during pushing.

### The Bracing Sequence
(Starrett, Becoming a Supple Leopard, p. 40)
- How: 1. Squeeze glutes to set pelvis. 2. Align ribcage over pelvis. 3. Take a diaphragmatic breath. 4. Stiffen the belly as you exhale to create IAP.
- Duration: 5-8 repetitions of the sequence.
- Target: Establishes the "inner cylinder" stability required to prevent midsection collapse.

## Phase B — Strength (20-25 min)

### Band Push-Up Plus
(Hogan, Built From Broken, p. 363-364)
- How: Perform a standard push-up; at the top, protract the scapulae as far as possible, pushing the spine toward the ceiling.
- Sets/Reps: 3 sets of 10-15 reps.
- Target: Specifically activates the serratus anterior, the primary stabilizer against scapular winging.

### Pseudo Planche Push-Up (Leaning)
(Low, Overcoming Gravity, p. 278)
- How: In a plank position, lean forward so the hands move toward the waist. Perform a push-up while maintaining full scapular protraction at the top.
- Sets/Reps: 3 sets of 5-8 reps.
- Target: Increases load on the shoulders and chest while challenging protraction under higher force.

### Anti-Extension Dead Bug
(Hogan, Built From Broken, p. 190-240)
- How: Lying on the back, press the lower back into the floor. Slowly lower opposite arm and leg while maintaining spinal contact with the floor.
- Sets/Reps: 3 sets of 12 reps per side.
- Target: Builds the core stability needed to resist the "banana back" collapse in Chaturanga.

## Phase C — Control / Endurance (5-10 min)

### Eccentric "Negative" Push-Ups
(Low, Overcoming Gravity, p. 278)
- How: From a high plank, lower to the floor as slowly as possible (3-5 seconds). Focus on keeping the elbows close and the shoulder blades "glued" to the ribs.
- Sets/Reps: 3 sets of 5 reps.
- Target: Builds the necessary eccentric strength for the descent phase of Chaturanga.

### Serratus-Oblique Isometric Holds
(Myers, Anatomy Trains, p. 288-315)
- How: In a side plank or a "Bear Crawl" hold, focus on the diagonal connection between the supporting shoulder and the opposite abdominal wall.
- Duration: 3 holds of 45 seconds.
- Target: Integrates the Spiral Line force transmission for global torso stability.

## Progression
(Low, Overcoming Gravity, p. 278)
Criteria for advancing:
- Master **15-20 repetitions** of a standard push-up with perfect form (no sag) before moving to Diamond or Leaning variations.
- Master **3 consecutive 10-second descents** in the eccentric push-up phase before reintroducing the concentric (upward) phase in Chaturanga.

## When to seek help
- Sharp pain in the front of the shoulder (coracoid area) that doesn't resolve with mobility.
- "Clunking" or "popping" in the shoulder joint during the descent.
- Significant lower back pain immediately following push-up practice.
