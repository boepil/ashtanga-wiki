# Lumbar Compression — asana modifications

## Overview
Lumbar compression occurs when the lower back is forced to "hinge" at a single segment (usually L4-L5 or L5-S1) instead of distributing force throughout the entire kinetic chain. In backbends, this is caused by stiff thoracic segments; in forward folds, it is caused by tight hamstrings/hips. Modifications focus on stabilizing the lumbar "hinge" and mobilizing the areas above and below it.

## Primary Modifications

### Paschimottanasana (Seated Forward Fold)
- **Elevate the Hips:** Sit on a folded blanket or foam block. This facilitates an anterior pelvic tilt and prevents the lower spine from rounding (Ashtanga Knowledge Base, p. 254).
- **Lower Abdominal Brace:** Before folding, consciously draw the sit-bones together and contract the lower abdominals (Mula Bandha). This creates a "braced" entry that protects the discs (Vayu Jung Doohwa, p. 131).

### Urdhva Dhanurasana (Wheel Pose)
- **The Airbag Effect (IAP):** Gently engage the rectus abdominis to increase intra-abdominal pressure (IAP). This supports the lumbar spine from the front, acting like an "airbag" to prevent crunching (Ashtanga Knowledge Base, p. 19).
- ** thoracic mobilization:** Focus on "leading" the backbend from the upper back (sternum) rather than the lower back. Distribute the curve so it feels like an even arc (Kaminoff, p. 370).

### Vinyasa Transitions
- **The Two-Hand Rule:** During jump-backs and jump-throughs, maintain a "braced neutral" spine. Imagine your ribcage and pelvis are two hands that must stay parallel to one another (Starrett, p. 55). Avoid "worming" through Upward Dog.

## General Cues
- "Knit the ribs together."
- "Lengthen the tailbone toward the heels."
- "Push the floor away to find height, not depth."
