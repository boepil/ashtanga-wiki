Sacroiliac (SI) joint instability is often a result of ligamentous laxity or "overmobilization" in asymmetric yoga poses like Janu Sirsasana or Parivrtta Parsvakonasana (Kaminoff, Yoga Anatomy, p. 251). Stability in this joint is not derived from traditional muscle strength, but from "muscular stiffness"—a guy-wire system of tension that inhibits painful micro-movements (McGill, Back Mechanic, p. 31). This protocol focuses on "tuning" the core and adductors to restore congruence to the joint edges (Kaminoff, p. 246).

## Overview

## Guidelines
- Frequency: Daily for "Spine Hygiene" and Bracing.
- Session time: 20-30 minutes.
- Principle: Compression for Stability. Active adduction and internal rotation of the legs encourage the pelvic halves to move together, increasing SI joint integrity (Kaminoff, p. 246).

## Pain guide
- 0: No pain — good to train.
- 1-3: Dull Ache — proceed with adductor engagement.
- 4+: Sharp "Stab" during transition — stop immediately. This indicates a "local hinge fault" or shear force across the joint (Starrett, Becoming a Supple Leopard, p. 54).

## Phase A — Prep / Mobility (5-8 min)

### Adductor Smash and Floss
(Starrett, Becoming a Supple Leopard, p. 299)
- How: Lie prone with one leg out to the side (half-frog). Place a foam roller or medicine ball under the inner thigh. Move the leg through its range of motion while applying pressure.
- Duration: 2 minutes per side.
- Target: Restores sliding surfaces in the adductors, which are primary stabilizers for the back and pelvis.

### Pelvic Stiffening Diagnostic (One-Leg Stand)
(McGill, Back Mechanic, p. 64)
- How: Stand on the leg of the painful side. "Stand tall" and stiffen the core. If the pain decreases compared to standing on both feet, it proves that pelvic stiffening is the solution.
- Duration: 3 rounds of 30 seconds.
- Target: Diagnostic check and low-load stabilization drill.

### Active Internal Rotation (Neutralizer)
(Kaminoff, Yoga Anatomy, p. 246-248)
- How: Stand with feet parallel. Engage the inner thighs to "pull the pelvic halves together in the front." Focus on the sensation of nutation (top of sacrum nodding forward).
- Duration: 10 breaths.
- Target: Brings congruence to the joint edges of the sacrum and ilium.

## Phase B — Strength (20-25 min)

### The McGill Big 3: Side Bridge
(McGill, Back Mechanic, p. 337-338)
- How: Support weight on elbow and knees/feet. The Quadratus Lumborum (QL) is essential for "fixing" the pelvis during leg movement (McGill, p. 30).
- Sets/Reps: Descending pyramid (6-4-2) of 10-second holds.
- Target: Builds the "guy-wire" system required for pelvic stability.

### Adductor Squeeze (Ball/Block)
(Goodman, Foundation, p. 241)
- How: Lie on back or sit with a block between the knees. Squeeze the block firmly while maintaining a neutral spine.
- Sets/Reps: 3 sets of 15 reps (5-second squeeze).
- Target: Directly pulls the pelvis down to decrease spinal/SI pressure.

### The Founder (Pelvic Floor Integration)
(Goodman, Foundation, p. 314-322)
- How: Feet shoulder-width, weight on heels. Hinge deep while keeping the chest high. Squeeze the glutes to set the pelvis in neutral.
- Sets/Reps: 3 sets of 1-minute holds.
- Target: Retrains the hips to pivot while the SI joint remains braced and stable.

### Clamshells (Glute Medius Activation)
(Hogan, Built From Broken, p. 327-333)
- How: Lying on side, knees bent. Lift top knee without rotating the pelvis.
- Sets/Reps: 3 sets of 15 reps per side.
- Target: Strengthens the lateral stabilizers to prevent rotational collapse of the pelvis.

## Phase C — Control / Endurance (5-10 min)

### Bracing Sequence (The Dimmer Switch)
(McGill, Back Mechanic, p. 97; Starrett, p. 40-41)
- How: Squeeze glutes to set neutral pelvis. Tighten the abdominal wall "just enough" to control the task (like a dimmer switch).
- Duration: Practice during all daily movements (walking, sitting).
- Target: Removes the micro-movement triggers that keep the joint sensitive.

### Standing Hinge with Adductor Tension
(Goodman, Foundation, p. 241)
- How: Perform a partial hinge while imagining you are "pulling the floor together" with your feet.
- Sets/Reps: 10 slow, controlled hinges.
- Target: Integrates adductor stability into functional movement.

## Progression
(McGill, Back Mechanic, p. 36)
Criteria for progress:
- **Gristling Phase:** If the joint is highly lax, you must avoid all spine bending for months to allow the ligaments to "gristle" and tighten.
- **Spiral Line Balance:** Advance when you can perform a twisting movement (like Trikonasana) while keeping the back leg internally rotated and the core braced (Myers, p. 131).

## When to seek help
- Pain that shifts from one side of the pelvis to the other (Alternating SI pain).
- Feeling of "giving way" in the hip when walking.
- Pain that radiates into the groin or down the front of the thigh.
