# Hip Flexor Dominance — asana modifications

## Overview
When the hip flexors (psoas/iliacus) are overactive, they "pull" the pelvis into an anterior tilt, causing the lumbar spine to compensate with excessive lordosis. This is particularly visible as a "pinching" sensation in the front of the hip during forward folds or "crunching" in the lower back during backbends. Modifications focus on bypassing bone-on-flesh compression and using reciprocal inhibition to quiet the hip flexors.

## Primary Modifications

### Paschimottanasana (Seated Forward Fold)
- **Abduct the Legs:** Move the feet 2-4 inches apart. This allows the pelvis to move *inside* the thighs, bypassing the pinching sensation of bone-on-flesh compression at the hip crease (Clark, p. 530).
- **Micro-bend the Knees:** Slightly bending the knees releases the hamstrings and "frees" the pelvis to tilt forward without forcing the psoas to work too hard (Kaminoff, p. 358).

### Urdhva Dhanurasana (Wheel Pose)
- **Reciprocal Inhibition:** Firmly squeeze the glutes and tuck the tailbone (posterior tilt) before lifting. This signals the psoas to relax, allowing for a deeper and safer hip extension (Ashtanga Knowledge Base, p. 33).
- **Inner Thigh Engagement:** Contract the adductor magnus (inner thighs) to keep the knees from splaying out. Splaying the knees is a sign of psoas dominance that compresses the sacrum (Kaminoff, p. 360).

### Vinyasa Transitions (Chaturanga)
- **Drop the Knees:** During the descent from Plank to Chaturanga, lower the knees to the floor. This reduces the load on the iliopsoas, preventing the "butt-up" or "sagging-waist" faults that strain the lumbar spine (Ashtanga Knowledge Base, p. 254).

## General Cues
- "Hollow the belly before folding."
- "Lead with the chest, not the chin."
- "Weight back into the heels in standing poses."
