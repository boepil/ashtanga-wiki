# Nervous System Dysregulation — rehabilitation protocol

## Overview
This protocol focuses on retraining the respiratory system to restore autonomic balance (vagal tone) and increase carbon dioxide tolerance. In yoga, breath holding or "chest breathing" under effort triggers a sympathetic (fight-or-flight) response, which compromises both performance and spinal stability. By using the Body Oxygen Level Test (BOLT) to measure chemoreceptor sensitivity (McKeown, The Oxygen Advantage, p. 37), we can systematically down-regulate the nervous system and transition from a "stressed" to a "drowsal" (rest-and-digest) state (McKeown, p. 74).

## Guidelines
- Frequency: Daily.
- Session time: 10-20 minutes.
- Principle: Strict nasal breathing at all times (day and night). Use "air hunger" as a signal for adaptation, not stress (Nestor, Breath, p. 251).

## Pain guide
- 0: Calm — good to train.
- 1-3: Air Hunger — tolerable, keep inhalation calm.
- 4+: Panic/Gasping — stop, the air shortage is too intense (McKeown, p. 145).

## Phase A — Prep / Mobility (5-8 min)

### BOLT Score Assessment (Diagnostic)
(McKeown, The Oxygen Advantage, p. 37-49)
- How: 1. Rest for 10 min. 2. Take a normal breath in and out through the nose. 3. Hold nose and time the seconds until the *first* definite desire to breathe or involuntary contraction of breathing muscles. 4. Recovery breath must be calm.
- Goal: Target score is 40 seconds. A score below 20 indicates significant dysregulation.

### Nasal Decongestion Exercise
(Nestor, Breath, p. 355)
- How: Exhale softly, pinch nostrils shut. Shake your head or walk while holding. Release and take a controlled nasal breath only when air hunger is potent.
- Duration: Repeat 6 times.
- Target: Uses CO2 accumulation to dilate nasal passages and restore nasal pathway.

### Alternate Nostril Breathing (Nadi Shodhana)
(Nestor, Breath, p. 347)
- How: Close right nostril, inhale left. Pause. Close left, exhale right. Repeat alternating.
- Duration: 5-10 cycles.
- Target: Balances the autonomic nervous system and improves focus.

## Phase B — Strength (20-25 min)

### Coherent (Resonant) Breathing
(Nestor, Breath, p. 349)
- How: Sit upright. Inhale softly for 5.5 seconds, exhale softly for 5.5 seconds. Total of 5.5 breaths per minute.
- Duration: 10 minutes.
- Target: The "perfect" breath that coordinates heart, lungs, and brain into a single rhythm.

### "Breathe Light to Breathe Right"
(McKeown, The Oxygen Advantage, p. 143-145)
- How: Reduce breathing volume by 20-30% to create a "tolerable air hunger." Maintain for 5 minutes.
- Target: Resets the brain's CO2 threshold and activates the parasympathetic nervous system (signaled by increased warmth and watery saliva).

### Ujjayi & Bracing Integration
(Kaminoff, Yoga Anatomy, p. 21; Starrett, Becoming a Supple Leopard, p. 44)
- How: Partially close the glottis to create back pressure (Ujjayi). Coordinate this with the bracing sequence (stiffening the belly on exhale) to create Intra-Abdominal Pressure (IAP).
- Sets/Reps: 10 slow, braced Ujjayi breaths.
- Target: Protects the spine during movement by redistributing mechanical stress.

## Phase C — Control / Endurance (5-10 min)

### Breathhold Walking
(Nestor, Breath, p. 363)
- How: Exhale all air, walk and count steps until powerful air hunger. Resume calm nasal breathing while continuing to walk.
- Target: Increases functional CO2 tolerance and endurance.

### Diaphragmatic Gasps under Tension
(Starrett, Becoming a Supple Leopard, p. 47)
- How: Hold a plank or high-tension position. Take "short, concise breaths through the diaphragm" while keeping the trunk stiff.
- Duration: 3 rounds of 30 seconds.
- Target: Teaches the body to maintain stability while continuing to breathe under load.

### Mouth Taping (Sleep Tape) Setup
(Nestor, Breath, p. 267)
- How: Apply a small piece of surgical tape over the center of the lips before sleep.
- Target: Ensures 100% nasal breathing during sleep, preventing nighttime dysregulation and snoring.

## Progression
(McKeown, The Oxygen Advantage, p. 5)
Criteria for progress:
- **Waking BOLT Increase:** You will feel a significant improvement for every 5-second increase in your morning BOLT score.
- **Physical Integration:** Transition from seated practice to walking, and eventually to maintaining nasal-only breathing during the Primary Series.

## When to seek help
- Dizziness or fainting during breath holds.
- Severe anxiety or panic attacks triggered by air hunger.
- Persistent heart palpitations during or after breathing exercises.
