# Shatkarma Safety and Timing

The Shatkarmas are specialized, highly forceful purificatory techniques. Because they manipulate the body's internal pressure and organ systems, they carry significant risks if performed incorrectly.

## When to Practice

- **Time of Day:** The ideal time for purification is during the *Brahma Muhurta* (early morning, around 4:00 AM, or just before sunrise).
- **Empty Stomach:** All Shatkarmas **must** be performed on a completely empty stomach. For abdominal practices like Nauli, practitioners must wait at least six hours after eating.
- **Frequency:** Shatkarmas are primarily **curative**, intended for those with a distinct *dosha* imbalance (especially excess fat or phlegm). If the bodily humors are already balanced, these actions are not required and can even be depleting or habit-forming if overused. Think of them as a seasonal "tune-up," not a daily requirement.

## General Contraindications

> [!CAUTION]
> If you experience nosebleeds, sharp pain in the ears, or fainting spells during any Shatkarma, stop immediately.

- **Pregnancy:** Forceful abdominal practices (*Nauli*, *Basti*, *Kapalabhati*) are strictly prohibited.
- **Cardiovascular Issues:** Intense kriyas that increase thoracic or cranial pressure are contraindicated for those with high blood pressure, heart disease, or a history of stroke.
- **Cranial Pressure:** Practices involving rapid breathing or breath retention must be avoided by those with glaucoma, detached retinas, vertigo, or inner ear infections.

## Teacher's Scope of Practice

- **Not Self-Taught:** Traditional texts emphatically state that Shatkarmas should only be learned under the direct supervision of an experienced Guru. Attempting to learn them from books or videos is dangerous.
- **The Rule of Secrecy:** The *Hatha Yoga Pradipika* commands that these actions be kept secret like a "box of jewelry." This maintains their potency and ensures they are not diluted by unqualified practitioners.
- **Referring Out:** Yoga teachers must recognize their scope. Students with severe medical conditions should be referred to medical professionals or high-level therapeutic specialists rather than attempting to "cure" them with forceful kriyas.
