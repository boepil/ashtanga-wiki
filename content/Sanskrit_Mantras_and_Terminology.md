# Sanskrit Mantras and Terminology

## Summary
The Ashtanga Vinyasa tradition is deeply rooted in Sanskrit terminology and chanting. The practice is framed by the Opening Mantra (*Vande Gurunam*), which venerates the lineage, and the Closing Mantra (*Mangala Mantra*), which offers peace to the world. Furthermore, the use of Sanskrit for naming postures and counting *vinyasas* acts as a standardized matrix that preserves the integrity of the sequence across generations.

## Detail

### The Opening Mantra (Vande Gurunam)
At the beginning of each Led and Mysore practice session, practitioners "formally venerate Patañjali and the lineage of teachers through recitation of a set of verses" (Sarbacker, *Tracing the Path of Yoga*, p. 1248).

**Translation Extract:**
> "Vande Gurunam charanaravinde / Sandarshita svatmasukhavabodhe / Nishreyase jangalikayamane / Samsara halahala mohashantyai.
> [I worship the Guru’s lotus feet / Awakening the happiness of the Self revealed / Beyond comparison, acting like the jungle physician / For the peace of the delusion of the poison of cyclic existence.]" 
> — (Jois, *Yoga Mala*, p. 1511)

The second half of the mantra specifically honors Sage Patanjali: "I bow with folded hands to Patanjali... who removed the impurities of the mind with yoga, the impurities of speech with grammar, and the impurities of the body with medicine" (Simpson, *The Truth of Yoga*, p. 963).

### The Closing Mantra (Mangala Mantra)
The practice concludes with a chant that shifts the focus from internal purification to global peace and righteous leadership.

**Translation Extract:**
> "Om svastiprajābhyaḥ paripālayantāṃ nyāyena mārgena mahīṃ mahīśāḥ / gobrāhmaṇebhyaḥ śubhamastu nityaṃ lokāssamastāḥ sukhinobhavantu / oṃ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ || 
> Let Prosperity be Glorified / Let Rulers rule the world with Law and Justice / Let Divinity and Erudition be protected / Let People of the whole world be happy and prosperous."
> — (Sharath Jois, *Ashtanga Yoga Anusthana*, p. 60)

### The Importance of Sanskrit Terminology
Sanskrit is not merely decorative; it is the technical operating language of the system. "The meaning of many yoga concepts is still best stated in Sanskrit, and wherever there is translation there is concern about accuracy... The most commonly accepted and used terms... are drawn from the Krishnamacharya lineage" (Stephens, *Teaching Yoga*, p. 752).

### Sanskrit Counting (Vinyasa Krama)
The standardization of Ashtanga relies heavily on the exact Sanskrit count (Ekam, Dve, Trini...). 
- **The Format:** "The posture is named by the instructor, after which the students are counted through the three phases of the āsana (entry, posture proper, exit). This is... precisely the format adopted by Krishnamacharya in his early school teaching" (Singleton, *Yoga Body*, p. 204).
- **The Metaphor:** Pattabhi Jois described this system poetically: "His mala is a garland of yoga, in which each vinyasa is like a sacred bead to be counted and focused on, and each asana is like a flower" (Jois, *Yoga Mala*, p. 1500).
- **Vedic Roots:** Krishnamacharya linked this strict counting directly to Vedic chanting. "From ancient times, while doing veda adhyayanam, the svaras... in the aksharas (syllables) of the vedas are observed and mastered without fail... Similarly in yogasana, pranayama and the mudras, the vinyasas handed down from ancient times should be followed" (Krishnamacharya, *Yoga Makaranda*, p. 1489).
