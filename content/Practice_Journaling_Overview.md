# Practice Journaling

In the Ashtanga tradition, a personal practice journal is not merely a diary of workouts; it is an active tool for **Svādhyāya** (self-study). The journal transforms the yoga mat into a laboratory where the practitioner acts as both the performer of the action and the observer of the self.

## The Purpose of Journaling (Psychic Hygiene)

Through disciplined observation, a journal helps you identify subliminal imprints (**Samskaras**) and mental afflictions (**Kleshas**). By recording the specifics of each session, you cleanse the "lens" of the mind, ensuring it reflects reality precisely rather than through the filter of ego or past trauma.

## Progression Through the Koshas

A mature journaling practice tracks your evolution from gross physical effort to subtle energetic realization, moving through the **Koshas** (sheaths):

1. **Annamaya Kosha (The Gross Layer):** Initial entries focus on technical insights, aligning bones, and protecting joints (e.g., [[Adjustments_That_Worked]]).
2. **Prāṇamaya Kosha (The Vital Layer):** Entries shift to the synchronization of movement and breath, observing the flow of *Prana* and the manipulation of the *Vayus* (e.g., [[Daily_Sequence_Notes]]).
3. **Manomaya Kosha (The Mental Layer):** The focus turns inward to *Pratyahara* (sense withdrawal). Entries record emotional states, frustrations, and the *Gunas* (Sattvic, Rajasic, Tamasic).
4. **Vijnānamaya Kosha (The Wisdom Layer):** The breakthrough stage. Entries reflect deep discernment, where the practitioner successfully separates the silent observer (*Purusha*) from the active body (*Prakriti*) (e.g., [[Self_Inquiry_Journal]]).

## Practical Journaling Frameworks

### 1. Breath Awareness Tracking

The breath (*Ujjayi*) is the foundational thread that links the body and mind.

- **Ujjayi Quality:** Track the consistency of the soft sibilant sound in the throat. Is it steady, strained, or fading?
- **Sama Vritti (Equal Breath):** Monitor the ratio of inhalation (*puraka*) to exhalation (*rechaka*). Are they equal in length and depth?
- **Breath as a Barometer:** If the breath becomes short, fast, or labored in difficult *asanas*, it signals that physical capacity has been exceeded.

### 2. Physical Observations

Use the body to distinguish between changeable tension and structural limits.

- **Sthira (Stability) and Sukha (Ease):** Does the posture feel steady and comfortable?
- **"What Stops Me?" (WSM):**
  - **Tension:** Muscular resistance felt away from the direction of movement (e.g., hamstrings stretching). This can be changed over time.
  - **Compression:** Skeletal limit felt in the direction of movement (bone hitting bone, "pinching"). This cannot be changed.
- **Identifying Pain:** Differentiate between "Right Pain" (a taut pulling that leads to release) and "Wrong Pain" (sharp, stabbing, or joint-centered ache).

### 3. Tracking Mental and Emotional States

Observe the **Gunas** (qualities of nature) and **Kleshas** (mental afflictions) as they arise during practice.

**The Gunas (Energy Levels):**
- **Sattva (Luminosity):** Clear, calm, focused, and "thoughtfully thoughtless."
- **Rajas (Dynamism):** Agitated, rushing, passionate, or highly competitive.
- **Tamas (Inertia):** Heavy, dull, lethargic, or bored.

**The Kleshas (Sticky Spots):**
- **Raga (Attachment):** Clinging to past successes or how a pose "should" look.
- **Dvesha (Aversion):** Wanting to skip or rush through uncomfortable postures.
- **Asmita (Egoism):** Comparing oneself to others in the room.
- **Abhinivesha (Fear):** Anxiety arising in inversions or deep backbends.

### 4. Setting Goals and Intentions (Saṅkalpa)

Goals in yoga should be established through the lens of *Sattva* (clarity and peace), avoiding the *Rajasic* "no pain, no gain" mentality.

- **Intention (*Saṅkalpa*):** Set a primary resolve for the week/month (e.g., maintaining steady breath, practicing non-harming).
- **Gradual Progress (*Shanaih Shanaih*):** Tame the body slowly and gently to avoid injury.
- **Off-the-Mat Application:** Observe how patience, clarity, and discipline cultivated on the mat transfer to daily life, social interactions (*Yamas*), and personal observances (*Niyamas*).

### 5. The Teacher's Reflection Log

Teachers use journaling to refine their pedagogy and maintain ethical vigilance.
- **Observation:** Did I accurately identify the students' needs (tension vs. compression)?
- **Adjustments:** Were my physical assists safe, grounding, and consensual?
- **Ego Check:** Am I teaching to serve the student (*Karma Yoga*), or am I attached to their progress to validate my own authority?
