# Practice Journaling Overview

In the Ashtanga tradition, a personal practice journal is not merely a diary of workouts; it is an active tool for **Svādhyāya** (self-study). The journal transforms the yoga mat into a laboratory where the practitioner acts as both the performer of the action and the observer of the self.

## The Purpose of Journaling (Psychic Hygiene)

Through disciplined observation, a journal helps you identify subliminal imprints (**Samskaras**) and mental afflictions (**Kleshas**). By recording the specifics of each session, you cleanse the "lens" of the mind, ensuring it reflects reality precisely rather than through the filter of ego or past trauma.

## Progression Through the Koshas

A mature journaling practice tracks your evolution from gross physical effort to subtle energetic realization, moving through the **Koshas** (sheaths):

1. **Annamaya Kosha (The Gross Layer):** Initial entries focus on technical insights, aligning bones, and protecting joints (e.g., [[Adjustments_That_Worked]]).
2. **Prāṇamaya Kosha (The Vital Layer):** Entries shift to the synchronization of movement and breath, observing the flow of *Prana* and the manipulation of the *Vayus* (e.g., [[Daily_Sequence_Notes]]).
3. **Manomaya Kosha (The Mental Layer):** The focus turns inward to *Pratyahara* (sense withdrawal). Entries record emotional states, frustrations, and the *Gunas* (Sattvic, Rajasic, Tamasic).
4. **Vijnānamaya Kosha (The Wisdom Layer):** The breakthrough stage. Entries reflect deep discernment, where the practitioner successfully separates the silent observer (*Purusha*) from the active body (*Prakriti*) (e.g., [[Self_Inquiry_Journal]]).
