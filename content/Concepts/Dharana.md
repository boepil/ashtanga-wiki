# Dharana (Concentration)

**Dharana** is the sixth limb of the [[Eight_Limbs]] of yoga and marks the beginning of the internal, meditative disciplines (*antaranga*). Derived from the root *dhri*, meaning "to hold" or "to fix," Dharana is the act of binding the mind to a single point, object, or concept.

## Binding the Mind

In the [[Yoga_Sutras]] (3.1), Patañjali defines Dharana as *desha-bandhash chittasya dharana*: "Concentration is the binding of the mind to a single place." Following the sensory withdrawal of [[Pratyahara]], Dharana fastens the mind to achieve one-pointedness (*ekagrata*), leaving no room for distracting thoughts.

## Objects of Focus

The mind can be fixed on various objects, including:
- **Internal Loci**: The heart center, the navel, or the tip of the nose.
- **External Objects**: A deity, a flame, a yantra, or a mantra such as [[Om]].
- **The Breath**: Observing the flow of inhalation and exhalation.

## Integration in Practice

Dharana requires active mental effort to continuously bring the attention back to the chosen point of focus whenever it wanders. In daily life and physical practice, this translates to maintaining unbroken attention on the present moment or the specific task at hand, which serves as the foundation for entering the continuous flow of [[Dhyana]] (meditation).
