# Pranayama (Breath Control)

**Pranayama** is the fourth limb of the [[Eight_Limbs]] of yoga. The term combines *prana* (vital life force or energy) and *ayama* (extension, expansion, or control). It is the practice of regulating the breath to control and expand the life force within the body.

## The Mind-Breath Connection

In yogic philosophy, the breath and the mind are intimately linked. The [[Hatha_Yoga_Pradipika]] states: "When the breath moves, the mind moves. When the breath is still, the mind is still." 
- **The Harness**: Because respiration is the only physiological function that happens both automatically and can be controlled consciously, it serves as a bridge to harness the wandering mind.
- **Nervous System Regulation**: Inhalations stimulate the sympathetic nervous system (activation), while exhalations stimulate the parasympathetic nervous system (relaxation). The retention of breath (*kumbhaka*) provides a pause where the mind can reach profound stillness (*manonmani*).

## Purification of the Nadis

A primary goal of Pranayama is to purify the subtle energetic channels (*nadis*), specifically balancing the heating (*Pingala*) and cooling (*Ida*) currents. When balanced, energy can enter the central channel, the [[Sushumna]], leading to higher states of consciousness and deep meditation.

## Integration in Practice

Pranayama is considered highly potent and must be approached with patience, often compared to taming a wild animal. In Ashtanga Vinyasa, the foundation of pranayama is established during [[Asana]] practice through the continuous, rhythmic *Ujjayi* breath. Dedicated, seated Pranayama practice is traditionally introduced only after the body has been sufficiently purified and strengthened through asana.
