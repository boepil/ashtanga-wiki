# The Eight Limbs of Yoga (Ashtanga)

The Eight Limbs of Yoga, or **Ashtanga Yoga**, form the structural foundation of classical yoga philosophy as outlined by Patañjali in the [[Yoga_Sutras]]. The term "Ashtanga" translates literally to "eight-limbed" (ashta = eight, anga = limb). Rather than a linear sequence of steps to be completed and left behind, these limbs are viewed as an interconnected, organic whole, much like the branches of a tree growing simultaneously. 

## The Eight Limbs

1. **[[Yamas]] (Ethical Restraints)**: Universal moral codes concerning our interaction with the external world.
2. **[[Niyamas]] (Personal Observances)**: Internal disciplines for self-purification and personal growth.
3. **[[Asana]] (Physical Posture)**: The practice of physical postures designed to bring steadiness (*sthira*) and comfort (*sukha*) to the body.
4. **[[Pranayama]] (Breath Control)**: The regulation of life force (*prana*) through the conscious control of the breath.
5. **[[Pratyahara]] (Withdrawal of the Senses)**: The internalizing of consciousness by detaching from external sensory stimuli.
6. **[[Dharana]] (Concentration)**: The binding of the mind to a single point or object.
7. **[[Dhyana]] (Meditation)**: The uninterrupted flow of concentration; a state of sustained awareness.
8. **[[Samadhi]] (Absorption)**: The ultimate state of union where the meditator merges completely with the object of meditation.

## Integration in Practice

In the Ashtanga Vinyasa system, the physical practice (Asana) serves as the entry point and foundation for engaging with the other limbs. Through the method of Tristhana (posture, breath, and gaze), the practitioner simultaneously cultivates physical steadiness, energetic regulation (Pranayama), and sensory withdrawal (Pratyahara), laying the groundwork for the higher meditative states.
