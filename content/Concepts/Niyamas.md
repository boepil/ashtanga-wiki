# Niyamas (Personal Observances)

The **Niyamas** constitute the second limb of the [[Eight_Limbs]] of yoga. While the [[Yamas]] deal with our relationship to the external world, the Niyamas focus on our internal relationship with ourselves. They are personal practices and observances designed to purify the body and mind, fostering discipline and spiritual growth.

## The Five Niyamas

- **Saucha (Purity)**: Cleanliness of both the external environment and the internal body and mind. This includes dietary choices, yogic cleansing techniques (*shatkarmas*), and purifying the mind of disturbing emotions.
- **Santosha (Contentment)**: Finding peace and gratitude in the present moment, accepting circumstances as they are without craving or aversion.
- **Tapas (Discipline/Heat)**: The friction or "heat" generated by intense self-discipline. It involves the commitment to practice even when it is difficult, burning away impurities through effort and austerity.
- **Svadhyaya (Self-Study)**: The study of sacred texts (like the [[Yoga_Sutras]] or [[Bhagavad_Gita]]) and the introspective study of one's own mind, habits, and true nature.
- **Isvara Pranidhana (Surrender to the Divine)**: Dedicating the fruits of one's actions to a higher principle or the divine, letting go of egoic control and the attachment to specific outcomes.

## Integration in Practice

The Niyamas are cultivated on the yoga mat through the discipline of showing up consistently (Tapas), maintaining cleanliness of the body and breath (Saucha), observing the mind without judgment (Svadhyaya), finding contentment in one's current physical abilities (Santosha), and surrendering the effort to a higher awareness (Isvara Pranidhana).
