# Niyamas (Observances)

The **Niyamas** form the second limb of the [[Eight_Limbs]] of yoga. While the Yamas focus on external relationships, the Niyamas are internal disciplines meant to purify the body and mind, fostering personal growth and spiritual development.

## The Five Niyamas

- **Saucha (Purity)**: Cleanliness in both the physical body (external bathing, internal cleansing, and pure diet) and the mind (cultivating positive thoughts and intentions).
- **Santosha (Contentment)**: Practicing gratitude and acceptance of the present moment. It is finding happiness regardless of external gains or losses.
- **Tapas (Discipline/Heat)**: The self-discipline required to maintain a consistent practice. It is the "burning away" of physical and mental impurities through consistent effort and austerity.
- **Svadhyaya (Self-Study)**: The study of sacred texts (such as the [[Yoga_Sutras]]) and the practice of self-reflection to understand one's true nature and egoic patterns.
- **Isvara Pranidhana (Surrender)**: Dedicating the fruits of one's practice and actions to a higher power or universal intelligence, letting go of attachment to the ego and outcomes.

## Integration in Practice

In the Ashtanga tradition, the Niyamas are cultivated alongside the physical practice. For instance, **Tapas** is generated through the heat of the vinyasa, while **Saucha** is maintained through the internal cleansing process of sweating and correct breathing. Together with the Yamas, they provide the necessary moral and mental clarity for the deeper internal limbs of yoga.
