# Tapas (Internal Heat/Discipline)

## Summary
**Tapas** is the third of the [[Niyamas]] (internal observances) in Patanjali’s Yoga Sutras. The word is derived from the Sanskrit root *tap*, meaning "to burn," "to shine," or "to consume by heat." In the context of Ashtanga Yoga, Tapas represents both the literal physical heat generated by the practice and the metaphorical fire of self-discipline required to transform the body and mind.

## Physiological Aspect
In the Ashtanga tradition, Tapas is the "fire" generated through the friction of [[Ujjayi_Pranayama]] and the continuous movement of [[Vinyasa]].
- **Boiling the Blood**: Traditional teachings suggest that the internal heat "boils the blood," thinning it to allow deeper circulation into the organs and joints for purification [14].
- **Sweat and Detoxification**: This heat stimulates the skin to release impurities through perspiration, which is why practitioners are traditionally encouraged not to wipe away sweat, but to rub it back into the skin to conserve the "pranic" energy.

## Philosophical Aspect
- **Purification (Shodhana)**: Just as gold is purified by fire, the practitioner is purified through the heat of the practice. Tapas "burns away" the physical and mental impurities (*kleshas*) that cloud our perception.
- **Mental Discipline**: Tapas is the grit and determination required to show up on the mat consistently, especially when one is tired or facing mental resistance. It is the practice of remaining steady in the face of discomfort.
- **Transformation**: Without the heat of Tapas, the practice remains merely physical exercise. Tapas is the catalyst that turns asana into a spiritual discipline.
