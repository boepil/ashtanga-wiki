# Dhyana (Meditation)

**Dhyana** is the seventh limb of the [[Eight_Limbs]] of yoga. It represents the state where concentration ([[Dharana]]) matures into a perfect, uninterrupted flow of awareness toward the object of meditation.

## The Uninterrupted Flow

Patañjali describes Dhyana in the [[Yoga_Sutras]] (3.2) as the "continuity of cognition on that [object]." Traditionally, this state is compared to the steady, constant pouring of oil from one vessel to another. While Dharana involves the active effort of bringing a wandering mind back to a point, Dhyana is the effortless, continuous current of attention where the mind, body, and breath integrate entirely with the focus.

## Dhyana vs. Dharana

- **Concentration vs. Flow**: Dharana is the placement of focus; Dhyana is the resulting sustained current.
- **Awareness of Effort**: In Dharana, the practitioner is aware of the mental effort required to stay focused. In Dhyana, this effort drops away, and the focus sustains itself naturally.

## Integration in Practice

Dhyana forms the crucial middle step of *Samyama* (the combined practice of concentration, meditation, and absorption). It occurs when the mind begins to take the shape of the object it contemplates, setting the stage for the final dissolution of the ego in [[Samadhi]].
