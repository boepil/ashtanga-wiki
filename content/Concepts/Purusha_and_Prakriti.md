# Purusha and Prakriti

**Purusha** and **Prakriti** are the two foundational principles of Samkhya philosophy, the metaphysical system upon which the [[Yoga_Sutras]] of Patañjali are built. Understanding this dualism is essential for grasping the ultimate goal of classical yoga: the realization of the true self as distinct from the material world.

## The Two Principles

- **Purusha (Pure Consciousness)**: Purusha is the eternal, unchanging, unmanifested conscious principle. It is the "Seer" or the ultimate witness. It lacks form, attributes, and action; it simply *is*. Purusha is what observes the mind and the world but is never affected by them.
- **Prakriti (Material Nature)**: Prakriti encompasses everything that is manifest and changing in the universe, including the physical body, the senses, the intellect, and the mind itself. Everything that can be observed is Prakriti. It operates through the interplay of three fundamental qualities known as the [[Gunas]].

## The Cause of Suffering (Avidya)

In yogic philosophy, suffering arises from the entanglement of Purusha and Prakriti. Through spiritual ignorance (*avidya*, the root of the [[Kleshas]]), the eternal Seer (Purusha) identifies with the changing phenomena of nature (Prakriti), confusing the ego and the mind with the true Self. 

## Integration in Practice

The entire eight-limbed path ([[Eight_Limbs]]) is a systematic methodology to disentangle Purusha from Prakriti. By purifying the body and mind through practices like *Asana*, *Pranayama*, and *[[Dhyana]]*, the yogi purifies *Prakriti*. The ultimate goal is *Kaivalya* (liberation), where *Purusha* realizes its absolute independence and no longer identifies with the changing, suffering world of *Prakriti*.
