# Asana (Physical Posture)

**Asana** is the third limb of the [[Eight_Limbs]] of Ashtanga yoga. While in modern times it is often conflated with the entirety of yoga, in the classical context of the [[Yoga_Sutras]], it refers specifically to a seated posture for meditation. Over time, particularly in texts like the [[Hatha_Yoga_Pradipika]], the concept expanded to include a wide variety of physical postures designed to purify the body and balance energy.

## Sthira Sukham Asanam

Patañjali’s primary definition of asana (Sutra 2.46) is *sthira sukham asanam*:
- **Sthira**: Steadiness, firmness, stability, and alertness.
- **Sukha**: Ease, comfort, relaxation, and lightness.

A perfect posture is achieved when the dualities (*dvandvas*) of effort and relaxation are balanced, allowing the body to be held with stability but without tension.

## Core Principles

- **Purification**: Asana acts as a method to purify the physical body, preparing the nervous system to handle the higher energetic currents of [[Pranayama]] and meditation.
- **Mastery of Dualities**: By establishing physical stillness, the practitioner becomes unaffected by the pairs of opposites (heat/cold, pain/pleasure), making the body a neutral instrument rather than a source of distraction.
- **Energetic Container**: Asana cleanses the energy channels (*nadis*), acting as a steady container for the vital life force (*prana*).

## Integration in Practice

In Ashtanga Yoga, Asana is practiced dynamically through *vinyasa* (breath-synchronized movement). The practitioner cultivates *sthira* and *sukha* in every pose, ensuring that physical alignment is married with steady breathing and a focused gaze, setting the stage for [[Pratyahara]] and deeper inner focus.
