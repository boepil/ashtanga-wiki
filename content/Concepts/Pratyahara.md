# Pratyahara (Withdrawal of Senses)

**Pratyahara** is the fifth limb of the [[Eight_Limbs]] of yoga. The word derives from the roots *prati* (away or against) and *ahara* (input or nourishment). It translates to the withdrawal or detaching of the senses from external stimuli, so they conform to the nature of the mind rather than wandering outward.

## The Bridge to the Inner World

Pratyahara serves as the pivotal bridge between the external practices (*bahiranga*)—[[Yamas]], [[Niyamas]], [[Asana]], and [[Pranayama]]—and the internal, meditative practices (*antaranga*)—[[Dharana]], [[Dhyana]], and [[Samadhi]]. By consciously retracting sensory perception, like a tortoise drawing its limbs into its shell, the practitioner ensures that external distractions do not disturb the ensuing meditative focus.

## Integration in Practice

In the Ashtanga Vinyasa method, Pratyahara is actively cultivated through the three pillars of practice (*Tristhana*):
1. **Drishti (Gazing Point)**: Anchoring the eyes prevents the mind from wandering outward.
2. **Ujjayi Breath**: Listening to the rhythmic sound of the breath acts as an internal mantra, drawing awareness inward.
3. **Bandhas**: Engaging energetic locks keeps the vital energy (*prana*) and focus internal.

Without Pratyahara, the mind remains scattered by the senses, making true concentration impossible.
