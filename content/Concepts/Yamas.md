# Yamas (Ethical Restraints)

The **Yamas** form the first limb of the [[Eight_Limbs]] of yoga. They are universal ethical precepts that guide a practitioner's interactions with the external world and other living beings. Patañjali describes them as the "Great Vow" (*mahāvrata*), applicable to all spheres of life, regardless of time, place, or circumstance.

## The Five Yamas

- **Ahimsa (Non-Violence)**: The foundational precept of non-harming in thought, word, and deed. It involves cultivating compassion toward oneself (such as avoiding overexertion in [[Asana]]) and others. When established in ahimsa, hostility ceases in one's presence.
- **Satya (Truthfulness)**: The alignment of thought, word, and action. It means speaking the truth, but always filtered through the lens of ahimsa (truth should be kind and beneficial).
- **Asteya (Non-Stealing)**: Abstaining from taking what has not been given freely. This extends beyond material goods to include not stealing others' time, energy, or credit.
- **Brahmacharya (Right Use of Energy)**: Often translated traditionally as celibacy, in a broader sense it refers to the conservation and conscious direction of vital energy (*prana*) toward spiritual growth, avoiding sensory over-indulgence.
- **Aparigraha (Non-Hoarding)**: Freedom from possessiveness and greed. It involves taking only what is necessary and letting go of attachment to outcomes and material accumulation.

## Integration in Practice

The Yamas serve as the ethical foundation necessary for stilling the mind (*citta vritti nirodha*). Without them, advanced practices like [[Pranayama]] or meditation can amplify egoic tendencies rather than dissolve them. In daily life, practicing the Yamas means relating to the world from a place of integrity, non-harming, and non-attachment.
