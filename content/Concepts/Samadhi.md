# Samadhi (Union and Absorption)

**Samadhi** is the eighth and final limb of the [[Eight_Limbs]] of yoga. It is the culmination of the meditative path, representing the ultimate state of cognitive refinement, absorption, and spiritual union. The word translates as "putting together" or "absorption."

## Merging of Observer and Object

In Samadhi, the uninterrupted flow of [[Dhyana]] shines forth purely as the object itself. The mind becomes completely empty of its own form, much like a clear crystal taking on the color of whatever rests beneath it. In this state, the distinction between the observer (knower), the process of observation (knowing), and the object observed (known) dissolves entirely. 

## Distinctions in Samadhi

Patañjali’s [[Yoga_Sutras]] outline different levels of Samadhi:
1. **Samprajnata Samadhi (Seeded)**: A state still accompanied by subtle mental activities, reasoning, and bliss. It relies on a "seed" or object of focus.
2. **Asamprajnata Samadhi (Seedless)**: The supreme state where all mental fluctuations are totally suppressed, leading toward absolute liberation (*kaivalya*).

## Integration in Practice

Samadhi is the final fruit of the yogic path. While the initial limbs like [[Yamas]], [[Niyamas]], and [[Asana]] require willful action and effort, Samadhi is a state of grace and surrender that arises when the mind is perfectly purified and still, revealing the true nature of the self.
