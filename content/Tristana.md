# Tristana (The Three Pillars of Practice)

## Summary
**Tristana** (often spelled *Tristhana*) is the three-pronged focus that transforms physical asana into a moving meditation. By integrating the breath, the energy locks, and the gaze, the practitioner develops the concentration (*Ekagrata*) necessary to still the mind.

## The Three Pillars

1.  **Breath ([[Ujjayi_Pranayama]])**:
    - The breath should be steady and even, with an audible sound generated in the throat.
    - It regulates the internal heat (**[[Tapas]]**) and ensures that the nervous system remains balanced during intense exertion.
2.  **Bandha ([[Bandha_Overview]])**:
    - The internal energy locks (**[[Mula_Bandha]]** and **[[Uddiyana_Bandha]]**) stabilize the core and direct the flow of prana.
    - They provide the physical "force closure" necessary to protect the spine and joints.
3.  **Drishti ([[Drishti]])**:
    - The specific focal point for the eyes in every asana (e.g., nose, navel, hand).
    - It stabilizes the mind and prevents the external environment from distracting the practitioner's internal focus.

## Synergistic Effect
When the three pillars are applied simultaneously, the practice becomes "Tristana." This state is characterized by:
- **Internal Heat**: For purifying the organs and blood.
- **Mental Clarity**: For developing single-pointed focus.
- **Physical Grace**: For moving through the sequence with lightness and strength (*Sthira Bhaga*).
