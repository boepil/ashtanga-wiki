# Surya Namaskar (Sun Salutation)

## Summary
Surya Namaskar (Sun Salutation) is the dynamic, rhythmic opening sequence of the Ashtanga Yoga practice. It links breath with movement to generate internal heat, awaken the spine, and prepare the body and mind for the subsequent asanas.

## Detail
In Ashtanga Yoga, there are two variations of the Sun Salutation: Surya Namaskar A and Surya Namaskar B. They serve as the gateway to the practice, establishing the rhythm of Ujjayi breath, the activation of the Bandhas, and the focus of Drishti.

**Surya Namaskar A (9 Vinyasas):**
1. Ekam: Inhale, raise arms (Urdhva Hastasana)
2. Dve: Exhale, fold forward (Uttanasana)
3. Trini: Inhale, lift head (Ardha Uttanasana)
4. Chatvari: Exhale, jump back to Chaturanga Dandasana
5. Pancha: Inhale, Upward Facing Dog (Urdhva Mukha Svanasana)
6. Shat: Exhale, Downward Facing Dog (Adho Mukha Svanasana) - Hold for 5 breaths
7. Sapta: Inhale, jump forward, lift head (Ardha Uttanasana)
8. Ashtau: Exhale, fold forward (Uttanasana)
9. Nava: Inhale, rise up (Urdhva Hastasana)
Samasthiti: Exhale, equal standing.

**Surya Namaskar B (17 Vinyasas):**
Builds upon A by introducing Utkatasana (Chair Pose) and Virabhadrasana A (Warrior I) for both the right and left sides. This sequence increases the cardiovascular demand and brings deeper heat to the legs and hips.

The rhythmic integration of movement and breath in these salutations shifts the practitioner's state from external distraction to internal focus.
