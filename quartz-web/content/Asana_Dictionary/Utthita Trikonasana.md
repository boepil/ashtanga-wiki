# Utthita Trikonasana

"Utthita means extended, stretched. Trikona (tri = three; kona = angle) is a triangle. This standing asana is the extended triangle pose." 

![Uthitha-Trikonasana-A](https://www.devvratyoga.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Uthitha-Trikonasana-A.png)


## Sanskrit Meaning
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 63) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"Utthita Trikonasana has five vinyasas, of which the 2nd and 4th are the states of the asana." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1545) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"First, beginning with puraka [inhaling], jump the legs open to the right, three feet apart, stretch the arms out wide... this is the 1st vinyasa." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1545) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Then, turn the right foot to the right and exhaling, reach down and take hold of the big toe of the right foot with the right hand... this is the 2nd vinyasa." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1545) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Stretch the left arm up... bringing it in line with the right shoulder and extend the trunk. The back of the legs, the back of the chest and the hips should be in a line. Gaze at the thumb of the [left] hand." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 64) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Completing your exhale, root down more firmly from the top of your back hip into your back foot. Maintaining that strong energetic action, inhaling, draw your torso back up to standing." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 846) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
Finger tip of the left hand
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Movement
Feet 3 to 3.5ft apart, right foot 90, left foot 45, bend to the right over the foot, hold big toe, left arm up, chest open. Repeat on the left side.
<div class="citation-block">(raw PDF) </div>

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- "Utthita Trikonasana optimally contracts the psoas major portion of the iliopsoas muscle. Contraction in this posture anteverts the pelvis. This action draws the hamstrings’ origin... away from their insertion... and accentuates their stretch." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The oblique abdominal muscles shorten on the right side of the torso and lengthen on the left side, increasing the overall strength of the core area." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The serratus anterior muscles contract... drawing the scapula away from the midline and extending the arms." *(Yoga Mat Companion 1, Ray Long) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "The spine is in right lateral flexion, moving in a frontal plane from a sagittal axis. The pelvis is in a lateral tilt to the right." *(The Manual of Yoga Anatomy, Sally Parkes) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The front hip joint experiences abduction and a little external rotation, while the back hip joint experiences adduction and some internal rotation." *(Identifying Postural Imbalances, Vayu Jung Doohwa) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"Rooting evenly through your feet while lifting your inner arches, spiral your inner thighs back while drawing your sitting bones toward each other and lifting steadily from the front floor of your pelvis with mula bandha." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 965) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"In the 2nd and 4th vinyasas, which are the states of this asana, the body should be held tightly, and rechaka and puraka done slowly and as much as possible." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1547) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- "Due to the side-bend of the lumbar spine, those with lower back injuries and/or conditions should practise this asana with caution." *(Science of Yoga, Ann Swanson) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "If your right arm tilts back, you will lose the correct alignment of the hips and buttocks. Your neck and head will jut forward and your weight will fall on your left palm, and not on your left heel." *(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "For those predisposed to femoral acetabular impingement syndrome (FAIS), adding internal rotation before commencing the fold can be problematic." *(Your Body, Your Yoga, Bernie Clark) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Microbend the knee in straight-legged poses such as Triangle (Trikonasana)... to prevent hyperextension and ligament stress." *(Yoga of the Subtle Body, Tias Little) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "To reduce or eliminate hyperextension of the knee... co-contract the quadriceps and the hamstrings by simultaneously hugging the thighs together." *(Your Body, Your Yoga, Bernie Clark) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Lengthen before you deepen: Use the lower-side arm to press into a block or shin to create length in the spine before rotating." *(Science of Yoga, Ann Swanson) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
