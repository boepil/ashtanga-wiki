## Sanskrit Meaning
"Janu means the knee. Sirsa is the head. In this posture sit with one leg stretched out on the ground and the other bent at the knee. Then catch the extended foot with both the hands and place the head on that knee." 
*(Light on Yoga, B.K.S. Iyengar, p. 227) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Janu Sirsasana: Head to Knee Forward Bend Pose." 
*(Teaching Yoga Essential Foundations and Techniques, Mark Stephens, p. 960) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Vinyasa Count & Entry
"Janu Shirshasana... is of three types, each of which has twenty-two vinyasas. The 8th and 15th vinyasas are their states." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1547) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

**Variation A:**
"For the 7th vinyasa... press the area between the anus and the organ of generation with the heel of the right foot, pulling the right knee back 90°, bend forward at the waist, take hold of the foot of the outstretched leg with both hands... place the forehead or chin on the knee of the outstretched leg... this is the 8th vinyasa." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1548) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

**Variation B:**
"In the 7th vinyasa... stretch the left leg out straight, bend the right knee back 85°, sit the anus directly on top of the right heel, take hold of the left foot with both hands... Then, doing rechaka, touch the chin to the left knee... this is the 8th vinyasa." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1549-1550) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

**Variation C:**
"For the 7th vinyasa... bring the right foot in toward the groin, twisting it so that the toes are pressed to the floor and the heel is pointing up toward the navel, press the right knee forward to a 45° angle, take hold of the left foot with both hands... place the forehead or chin on the outstretched leg, and press the right heel into the navel... this is the 8th vinyasa." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1550) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Drishti
Big toe of the extended leg

## Movement
**Variation A:** Right knee bent, heel close to groin, extend the left leg, foot flexed, grab the right wrist over the left foot with the left hand, bend forward, place the chest on the thigh.
**Variation B:** Right knee bent, heel over the anus, extend the left leg, foot flexed, grab the right wrist over the left foot with the left hand, bend forward, place the chest on the thigh.
**Variation C:** Right knee bent, right foot on the floor with toes flexed on the floor, heel pushing into the lower belly, extend the left leg, foot flexed, grab the right wrist over the left foot with the left hand, bend forward, place the chest on the thigh.

## Anatomical Focus
**Primary Muscles Engaged:**
- "Adductors on the bent leg are lengthening; core engagement helps the slight twist toward the extended leg." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The gluteus medius contracts and abducts the bent leg... Anterior fibers also medially rotate the thigh, protecting the knee." *(Key Muscles of Yoga, Ray Long) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The sartorius... flexes, abducts, and externally rotates the thigh." *(Key Muscles of Yoga, Ray Long) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "The straight leg quadriceps contract, stretching the corresponding hamstrings [through reciprocal inhibition]." *(Key Muscles of Yoga, Ray Long) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Joint Actions:**
- "Joint Action: Hip flexion (both legs), external rotation (bent leg), spinal rotation (slight)." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Progressive Hip External Rotation: A: Gentle external rotation with forward fold; B: Deeper external rotation with foot under perineum; C: Maximum external rotation challenge." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

## Bandha Application
"While practising this asana, however much the stomach is pulled in, there will be that much increase in the benefits received.... pull in the stomach beginning with the navel... and pull these upwards." 
*(Yoga Makaranda, T. Krishnamacharya, p. 98) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

"Janu Shirshasana (A & B) press on the virya nala [nerve connecting to the liver] for men, and Janu Shirshasana (C) presses on it for women." 
*(Yoga Mala, K. Pattabhi Jois, p. 1551) — Yoga Philosophy and Origins*

## Common Mistakes & Injury Flags
**Anatomical Risks:**
- "If the hip stops rotating... the torque transfers to the knee. This stresses the medial collateral ligament (MCL) and meniscus." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Do not allow the thigh [of the extended leg] to lift off the floor." *(B. K. S. Iyengar Yoga: The Path to Holistic Health, B. K. S. Iyengar, p. 136) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*

**Injury Prevention Cues:**
- "Rule: No rotation torque. Modification: In Hip Openers (Lotus, Janu Sirsasana), keep the knee flexion closed (heel to butt). Support the knee with a block/blanket." *(ASHTANGA_YOGA_KNOWLEDGE_BASE.pdf, Unknown) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
- "Be conscious of this, and try to equalize the stretch on both sides [of back and torso]. Keep your elbows out, widening them to increase the expansion of your chest." *(B. K. S. Iyengar Yoga: The Path to Holistic Health, B. K. S. Iyengar, p. 135) — Yoga and Human Anatomy*
