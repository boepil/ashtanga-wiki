# The Five Kleshas

The **Kleshas** are the five mental afflictions or obstacles identified by Patañjali in the [[Yoga_Sutras]] (Chapter 2). They are the root causes of all human suffering and the primary barriers to spiritual liberation. They exist as latent impressions in the mind and are activated by external triggers.

## The Five Afflictions

1. **Avidya (Ignorance)**: The root of all other kleshas. Avidya is spiritual ignorance—the fundamental misidentification of the eternal, pure Self ([[Purusha_and_Prakriti|Purusha]]) with the temporary, changing material world (Prakriti). It is confusing the ego for the true self.
2. **Asmita (Egoism)**: The false identification of the "I" with the physical body, the mind, or the intellect. It creates a sense of separation and a need to defend the ego.
3. **Raga (Attachment)**: The clinging to pleasure and the desire to recreate past pleasant experiences. It leads to addiction and a perpetual cycle of craving.
4. **Dvesha (Aversion)**: The repulsion toward pain and the intense desire to avoid unpleasant experiences. It breeds anger, fear, and hostility.
5. **Abhinivesha (Fear of Death)**: The instinctive clinging to physical life and the deep-seated fear of annihilation, present even in the wise.

## Integration in Practice

Yoga provides a methodology to attenuate the kleshas. By engaging in Kriya Yoga (action yoga consisting of *Tapas*, *Svadhyaya*, and *Isvara Pranidhana* from the [[Niyamas]]), the practitioner weakens the grip of these afflictions. The ultimate eradication of the kleshas occurs through deep meditation ([[Dhyana]]) and the dawn of true discernment (*viveka*).
