# Muscular System

## Summary
The muscular system in Ashtanga Yoga is trained to provide **dynamic stabilization** and optimal load distribution through neuromuscular re-education [1, 4]. Beyond providing the force for movement, the system functions as an "internal rigging" that prioritizes joint safety and core integration [3]. Key focus is placed on the deep core stabilizers, the iliopsoas as a structural intermediary, and refined gluteal differentiation for safe spinal extension.

## Detail

### The Deep Core: The "Stabilized Cylinder"
The core is a complex system of deep stabilizers that form a pressurized chamber or "stabilized cylinder" to protect the spine [5, 6]:
- **Transversus Abdominis (TVA)**: The deepest abdominal muscle, acting as a biological "corset" to maintain internal abdominal pressure (IAP) [7, 8].
- **Multifidus**: Powerful muscles running vertically along the spine; they work as a "dynamic duo" with the TVA to send stability status messages to the brain and prevent lumbar injury [9].
- **Diaphragm & Pelvic Floor**: The "roof" and "floor" of the core. They move in a synchronized "piston rhythm," descending on inhalation and ascending on exhalation to regulate IAP [6, 7].
- **The Airbag Effect**: When these four components co-contract (synonymous with applying the [[Bandha_Overview|Bandhas]]), they create an internal "airbag" that supports the lumbar spine from the inside out, particularly during backbends [5].

### The Iliopsoas: "The Flying Buttress"
The iliopsoas (psoas major and iliacus) is the strongest hip flexor and a critical anchor connecting the spine to the legs [13, 14].
- **Structural Role**: Like the architectural struts of a cathedral, it props up the spine and torso from the "ground" of the legs [16].
- **Respiratory Connection**: Because its upper fibers interweave with the respiratory diaphragm, a taut psoas can physically impair the "wave of breathing" [16, 18].
- **Ashtanga Application**: Vital for "jump-throughs," lifts (Lolasana), and maintaining an upright spine in [[Navasana]].

### Gluteal Differentiation
In backbends, the use of the gluteal group must be refined to avoid Sacroiliac (SI) joint compression [20, 21]:
- **Firm Engagement vs. Clenching**: Firm contraction is necessary to facilitate hip extension and protect the lumbar spine, but excessive "clenching" can jam the SI joint [20, 22].
- **Spiraling the Thighs**: Because the gluteus maximus is an external rotator, practitioners must co-activate the gluteus medius and adductors to "spiral" the inner thighs back. This keeps the knees parallel and broadens the sacrum [22].

### The Serratus Anterior: "The Boxer's Muscle"
The serratus anterior is the primary stabilizer for the shoulder blade (scapula) during weight-bearing [25].
- **Preventing Winging**: It protracts the scapula (drawing it away from the spine) to prevent "winging" in [[Surya_Namaskar#Surya_Namaskar_A_9_Vinyasas|Chaturanga Dandasana]] and Handstands [25, 26].
- **Lifting Power**: Provides the force required to lift the hips and stabilize the torso in arm balances like [[Bakasana]] and [[Bhujapidasana]] [17, 29].
