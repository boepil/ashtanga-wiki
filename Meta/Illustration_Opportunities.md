# Illustration Opportunities in the Ashtanga Wiki

The "10-Minute Shoulder Routine" example you provided is an excellent **multi-step, instructional infographic**. It goes beyond a simple photo of a pose by providing a flow, timing, key cues, and anatomical focus points. 

Here are the best places in the wiki to implement this specific style of illustration, starting with your intuition about the Rehabilitation section.

## 1. Rehabilitation Protocols (High Priority)
You already have the text and structure for these in the `Rehabilitation` folder. Creating a "10-Minute Routine" graphic for each protocol would make them instantly actionable for practitioners.

*   **Shoulder Impingement Protocol:** (Phase A Mobility, Phase B Strength, Phase C Control). *The example you attached is perfect for this!*
*   **SI Joint Instability Protocol:** A visual routine showing the progression from isometric holds (like block squeezing) to dynamic stabilization.
*   **Lumbar Compression Protocol:** A routine focusing on decompression, traction (like active hanging), and TVA engagement.
*   **Hamstring Overstretching Protocol:** A routine highlighting eccentric hamstring loading (like sliders) and glute activation.
*   **Chaturanga Collapse Protocol:** A targeted routine focusing on Serratus Anterior activation (scapular pushups, banded work) and core integration.

## 2. Surya Namaskar (Sun Salutations) Flowcharts
While the `Asana_Dictionary` has individual pose photos, the Sun Salutations are about **flow and linking**.
*   **Surya Namaskar A (9 Vinyasas):** A single-page infographic showing the 9 steps in sequence. Below each step, include three quick icons/text for: **Breath** (Inhale/Exhale), **Drishti** (Gaze point), and **Bandha** focus.
*   **Surya Namaskar B (17 Vinyasas):** Similar to A, but highlighting the complex transitions (like stepping into Virabhadrasana A).

## 3. The Shatkarmas (Cleansing Practices)
These are procedural practices that can be intimidating without clear visual steps. 
*   **Jala Neti (`Jala_Neti.md`):** A 3-step visual: 1. Preparation (salt ratio/water temp), 2. Posture/Action (head tilt angle, pouring), 3. Drying (specific forward folds and Kapalabhati to clear water).
*   **Trataka (`Trataka.md`):** A visual guide showing the setup: the distance of the candle from the face, eye level, and the sequence of open-eye gazing followed by closed-eye visualization.

## 4. Pranayama & Mudra Guides
*   **Nadi Shodhana (`Nadi_Shodhana.md`):** A visual breakdown of **Vishnu Mudra** (how to fold the fingers) paired with a simple flow chart of the breathing pattern (Inhale Left -> Exhale Right -> Inhale Right -> Exhale Left).
*   **Bandha Engagement (`Bandha_Overview.md`):** An anatomical infographic showing the 3 main bandhas (Mula, Uddiyana, Jalandhara) with step-by-step cues on *how* to physically engage them (e.g., "Exhale completely, hollow the belly upward...").

## 5. Sequence Transition Breakdowns
There are "sticking points" in the Ashtanga sequences that practitioners struggle with. A step-by-step "Routine" style graphic breaking down the mechanics of these transitions would be invaluable.
*   **Jump Through / Jump Back:** A multi-panel breakdown showing the stages of lifting, crossing the legs, shifting the center of gravity, and shooting back.
*   **Chakrasana (Backward Roll):** A step-by-step guide on hand placement, neck safety, and pushing through the roll, which is heavily reliant on technique.

---
**Summary:** The key to replicating the success of the "10-Minute Shoulder Routine" is to look for areas in the wiki that require a **sequence of actions, specific cues, and timing**. The Rehabilitation protocols are definitely the best place to start!

## 6. AI Prompt Template for Rehabilitation Infographics

If you plan to use an AI image generator (like Midjourney v6, DALL-E 3, or Claude/ChatGPT with image generation) to create these graphics, use the following prompt template. Just replace the bracketed information with the specific protocol details.

***

**Prompt Template:**

> Generate a rehabilitation protocol infographic for [Condition, e.g., Shoulder Impingement] in Yoga, same style and layout as the [Reference Infographic Name] previously generated.
> 
> Title: "[Condition, e.g., Shoulder Impingement] — Rehabilitation Protocol"
> Subtitle: "[Actionable Subtitle, e.g., Restore scapulohumeral rhythm. Build what was missing.]"
> 
> Same color coding:
> - [Color 1, e.g., Coral]: [Target Muscle 1, e.g., Serratus Anterior] (target)
> - [Color 2, e.g., Amber]: [Target Muscle 2, e.g., Lower Trapezius] (target)  
> - [Color 3, e.g., Teal]: [Target Muscle 3, e.g., Rotator Cuff external rotators] (target)
> 
> Central panel: anatomical view of the [Joint/Area, e.g., shoulder] showing the target muscles with arrows indicating their correct activation direction.
> 
> Left panel: Phase A — Prep/Mobility
> - [Exercise 1 Name, e.g., Serratus Wall Slides] ([Brief description, e.g., forearms on wall, V shape, slide up maintaining dome])
> - [Exercise 2 Name, e.g., Controlled Articular Rotations] ([Brief description])
> [Sets/Reps, e.g., 2 sets each], illustrated with practitioner.
> 
> Middle panel: Phase B — Strength
> - [Exercise 1 Name, e.g., Scapular Push-ups] ([Brief description])

> 
> Same color palette, typography, and layout as reference infographic series.

*** 

**Tip for AI Image Generation:** Since AI often struggles to get complex anatomical text and exact yoga posture nuances perfectly right on the first try, the best workflow is to use the AI to generate the **layout, figures, and arrows**, and then use a tool like Canva or Figma to drop the image in and add your own perfectly accurate text, timing, and labels on top of the AI's clean background.
